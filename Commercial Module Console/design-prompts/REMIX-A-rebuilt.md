# Remix A: rebuilt, and re-pointed

One of two parallel remixes being made from the current build. **This is the ambitious one.** It rebuilds the frame, applies everything the second remix does, and then changes what several sections are actually for.

A second, deliberately conservative remix is being made separately. This one should not hold back on its account.

---

## Who reads this console

A carbon desk professional at a large emitter with an annual, date-stamped compliance obligation. They have committed capital against a soil carbon module: 125,000 hectares of Australian grazing land, assembled property by property, over a seven year term with five crediting years.

This module is one line in a large book. It will never be the whole answer to their obligation and does not need to be. **What they need from this line is not scale, it is predictability.** So nothing is ever framed against their total obligation - the denominator is always what this module committed to deliver.

Credits are generated on a model and validated by physically sampling a tenth of each cohort annually. Sampling validates the estimate rather than replacing it. Everything shown is the purchaser's share, net of the landholder share and the reversal discount, and labelled as such.

---

## Part one: rebuild the frame

The console has grown by accretion, and the structure now carries the residue: content belonging to one tab rendering inside another, components left behind by removed features, state that outlived whatever set it. A remix that copies that structure forward is not worth doing.

**One panel per tab.** Selecting a tab replaces the panel and returns to the top of it. No tab shares a scroll surface with another. Content belongs to exactly one tab: fulfilment carries recruitment, delivery carries production, marketplace carries what else this land can do.

**One place where state lives.** Time state, selected tab, selected region or cohort, focus setting, and which capabilities are enabled are held once and read everywhere.

**One visual system, defined once.** Three type sizes at most, one small fixed set of colours, weights and greys, applied everywhere rather than chosen per section. This will do more for how the console reads than any other single change, and it is far easier now than as a retrofit.

**Nothing orphaned.** Removed content is gone completely and is not reachable by scrolling, resizing or changing time state.

**Every section behaves at every time state**, or is absent with something saying why and when it resolves. Nothing renders half-populated.

### Carry these across intact

A rebuild is where good work disappears. These all earned their place: the map as the persistent left surface and the catchment framing, the time stepper, the focus control including its most condensed option, the fill projection chart, the pipeline stage chart, hectares by stage, the cohort cards, the model monitoring panel including its decreases, the plot of modelled against validated, and the marketplace moment where enabling a capability adds a section to the console.

---

## Part two: the reductions

Everything here also applies to this remix.

Out entirely: the recruitment engine view, "what changed in the way we work", the spot against locked price chart, "what moved the model yields", the recruitment-closed summary on delivery, the reversal buffer row, the personalisation tab, and the early-harvest interaction.

The principle underneath most of those: **report outcomes, never process.** Panels showing stage dwell times, weekly rates against internal targets and cycle-time improvements invite the reader's analysts to performance-manage the delivery team rather than assess the module. Where a process improvement matters it appears as an earlier date on the fill projection.

Simplifications: lead fulfilment with progress against target as a percentage rather than pipeline depth; region cards keep their shape thumbnail and lose everything beneath it; pipeline stages lose all subtext, stage one becomes "Horizon assessed and eligible", stages four and five stop overlapping; cohorts are coloured dots rather than polygons or joined lines; property lists show five rows with an internal scroll; explanatory copy comes out throughout.

Renames: "Ask HORIZON" becomes Horizon AI, "nameplate" becomes at capacity, "all land physically sampled" becomes reference baseline established.

Climate and land condition moves to the delivery tab.

Additions: total addressable market per catchment with a penetration figure, harvestable volume available from outside the module as an expression of interest, and a disable path for enabled capabilities.

## Part three: what changes direction

### Delivery is anchored on position, not on a result

The delivery tab currently opens on a result for one cohort out of ten. The reader's live question is whether they will get what they contracted.

**Lead with position over time.** For each period: what was committed, what has been delivered, what is modelled for the periods ahead, and where that leaves them. Delivered volume accumulates, modelled volume revises, and the gap between the two is the answer.

Position anchors the tab for a structural reason rather than a matter of taste - it is the only element meaningful at every point in the module's life, because the commitment and the schedule both exist before a single hectare does. Everything else on the tab is empty or partial for at least half the term.

Beneath it, the **delivery schedule as a spine**: ten cohorts across roughly ten quarters, each an event with a date, a modelled volume and a state. In development, forming, ready, sampled, credited, and back to in development the following year. Around fifty harvest events across the term. That cadence is the rhythm of the product.

For a reader whose obligation is annual and date-stamped, **flag schedule risk against their compliance year** rather than only against our plan. A cohort scheduled near a boundary is a different risk from one mid-year, because a quarter of slippage crosses the line and misses the year entirely. And say plainly that surplus banks forward, or a compliance reader will assume it is stranded.

### Model accuracy becomes a track record

The progression from modelled at estimation to validated by the sampled tenth to issued is currently shown once, for the one credited cohort.

Build it as two figures doing two jobs. **Per cohort, a waterfall** - modelled, the adjustment the sample made, what issued. **Across the portfolio, a calibration plot** with every sampled cohort as one point, modelled on one axis, validated on the other, the identity line through it. Points clustering on that line is the most persuasive image this product can produce, and a technical reader takes it in without a legend.

It degrades honestly - at one cohort it is a single point and visibly so. Build the accumulator now so it has history when it matters, and let the earlier time states show it filling in.

### The model is alive, and it should look it

Every modelled figure carries its trajectory, not just its value, because the model re-reads this land continuously and revises. And when the portfolio figure moves, **say what moved it** - a rise of four per cent, of which three points from above-median autumn rainfall in one region and one point from cover recovery in another.

No other carbon product can tell a buyer why their forecast changed. This is the clearest demonstration on the console that something is reading the land rather than producing an annual estimate.

### The marketplace reduces to five, grouped by whose land

Nine overlapping items become five of material weight, grouped not by product category but by **whose land they read**, because that is how this reader thinks about assets.

| Capability | Whose land |
|---|---|
| **ESG reporting agent** - obligations across every framework they answer to, what evidence is in hand, what is missing, drafted output that updates as measurement lands | all of it |
| **Ecological co-benefits** - what else this ground generates, quantified on their own properties | land they hold credits on |
| **Mine rehabilitation monitoring** - soil carbon, effective rainfall, ground cover and erosion read across post-mining landforms and buffer country, with a dated record against completion criteria | land they operate |
| **Acquisition intelligence** - land condition, ecological state and monetisable potential on ground before they bid | land they do not own yet |
| **Property potential** - which of their own holdings are worth registering as projects | land they own that is not working |

Closure liability folds into rehabilitation monitoring. Catchment water and the nature baseline fold into co-benefits. Value chain footprint folds into the reporting agent. Denser sampling leaves the marketplace, being a delivery upgrade rather than a use case. The product-category filters go with them.

**The reframe to carry into the copy:** they bought this pointed at 125,000 hectares of someone else's grazing country, and it reads any land. Three of the five concern their own estate, which for a large miner is a far bigger surface than the module. The module was the trial.

The reporting agent is not a peer item, it is the destination - everything else produces measurement and the agent turns it into filed disclosure.

### The cards invert

They currently read as a software pricing grid, which is the wrong register for someone spending real money. All nine carry identical weight so nothing leads, each opens with a name and two lines of description, and the strongest content sits small and low.

**The finding leads.** *1.23 million hectares of your acquisition pipeline already screened* opens far harder than *Acquisition screening*. The findings are proof the work has already been done on their ground, and proof belongs before the pitch. The description can go.

**Preview on my land becomes the primary action.** Seeing a capability rendered on ground they know is what converts. Enabling follows previewing rather than sitting beside it.

### The offer arrives as an event, not as furniture

When a new instrument becomes available across land the reader already holds, that is a finding, and it arrives the way every other finding arrives - as the derived insight at the top of the tab, selecting through to where the purchase happens. Nothing about it sits permanently on screen.

*Reef Credits have come online across 13,600 hectares of your Fitzroy projects* is information about their asset. *Buy Reef Credits* is an advertisement. Same fact, opposite reception.

Lead with availability on land they already hold. Handle the reverse direction carefully - a compliance professional knows their own obligations better than we do, so state what exists and what their land generates against it, without advice. And if they are short in a given compliance year, say so in the same breath, because that makes the position view and the offer the same object seen twice.

Fire rarely, and stay dismissed when dismissed. An offer that recurs teaches the reader to ignore the insight line.

### Horizon AI absorbs personalisation

There is no personalisation tab. The conversational surface at the foot of the console is where it lives, which is where this reader will look for it.

It reasons across the whole console and returns an answer with the relevant chart beside it rather than a block of text. It takes attachments, because a spreadsheet of targets from a sustainability team is how personalisation will actually happen.

**Scope, deliberately narrow:** it records the reader's own commitments and reflects them against the delivery position. They state a retirement target for a given year, Horizon answers when the schedule reaches it, and offers to mark that commitment on the charts as a reference line. Personalisation adds their context to what is shown; it does not change what is shown. Everything reversible, nothing that can leave the console in a state they cannot get out of.

Surface it early rather than at the end. The whole claim that this is a living system rather than a reporting dashboard rests on it.

### The map carries projects nationally

Recruitment reaches beyond worked catchments through organic enquiry and partner referral, so projects appear across the country. Catchment boundaries become an overlay showing where field teams work rather than the organising geography.

Cohort formation then becomes a real optimisation - members close enough to sample in one mobilisation and similar enough for the model to calibrate across. **Show that as something the model solves.** It is a better story than an administrative grouping and it explains why a cohort can be restructured while a catchment cannot.

### The property view, and getting back out

A property opens from its point on the map and from its row in the list. Inside, the reader is looking at an individual farm - its land, its baseline, its measurement history, its projected yield. It is the only part of this product that is a place rather than a portfolio.

**Returning is obvious, permanent and predictable.** A visible control always on screen rather than a browser action. Present from the moment the property opens, at every scroll position. And it returns the reader to exactly where they left - same tab, same time state, same scroll position, with the property still identifiable where they came from.

A named path reads better than a back arrow because it says both where you are and where you will land, and it keeps the module present while the reader is inside one farm, which is the relationship being shown. Entering from the map returns to the map; entering from the list returns to the list.

One thing worth deciding rather than assuming: the property view was built for the landholder who owns that land, and a module holder may be seeing detail that landholder considers theirs. A reduced view may be right.

## The four time states

The console moves between four points in the module's life, and most of the difficulty is in the early ones. **Design the earliest state first.** It is the hardest and the one that sells, and it is a strong filter - an element that cannot say something useful when empty probably should not exist.

At the earliest state no credits have been issued and the first issuance is more than two years away. The tab's job is to make that wait read as a plan rather than a void. Every element that will hold a figure later is present, carrying what will appear there, when, and what will produce it. An empty calibration plot with its axes drawn and the first sampling quarter marked is a promise with a date attached, which beats both a filled mock and an absence.

The module may be well short of full when the first cohort credits, because early-signed projects reach their crediting year while recruitment continues. Recruitment insight lives on the fulfilment tab at full depth; delivery carries only the finished summary once the module completes, and while filling continues, what the remaining recruitment does to the delivery schedule.

---

## Working state

It has to work from a shared link on someone else's machine, with no local setup. Every route, not just the property view. A demonstration is run by someone who did not build it.

No dead ends. Every tab, card, cohort and row either goes somewhere or is visibly inert.

---

## Constraints

- Australian English, and no em dashes anywhere including titles. Spaced hyphens instead.
- Three type sizes at most, one shared colour and weight set.
- Modelled figures described as modelled, never as measured or validated.
- All volumes are the purchaser's share, labelled as such.
- No dollar value on any credit volume, and no commentary on market price.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable.

## Yours to decide

Proportion, grid, type, colour, motion, chart forms, component structure, how the transition into and out of a property is handled, and how enabling a capability animates the console reorganising around it.

The brief is the reader and the argument. Where something already in the build does one of these jobs better than what is described here, keep yours and say why.
