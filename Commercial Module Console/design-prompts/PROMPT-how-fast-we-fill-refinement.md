# How fast we fill - refinements

The section works. Converting every operational improvement into one currency, months off the fill date, is the right decision and it solves the problem that a set of improvements measured in days, hectares and multiples cannot otherwise be compared on one panel. These are refinements to it, not a rebuild.

---

## 1 · The track in each comparison row must encode the size of the change

Three rows currently show a value last year, a value now, and a dot on a track between them. The dot sits in roughly the same place in all three rows.

The three changes are not the same size. They are approximately **18 per cent, 36 per cent and 44 per cent**. A reader takes the position of the dot as meaning something, because a mark on a track always does, and here it means nothing.

**Either the position carries the magnitude of the change, on a scale shared by all three rows, or the track comes out and the row is two numbers.** A shape that looks quantitative and is not is worse than no shape, because a reader who trusts it leaves with a false impression and a reader who tests it stops trusting the panel.

If the position is kept, note that these metrics run in opposite directions - fewer days is an improvement, more hectares is an improvement. **Direction of benefit must be carried by the mark rather than by the reader knowing which way is good for each row.**

---

## 2 · A single quarter's pace will move the fill date by a year

The projection is driven by the pace measured in one quarter, compared against the pace measured in the previous quarter.

Quarterly step times in this business are extremely noisy. Measured across recent quarters, the median time to produce commercial terms has moved between roughly **25 and 71 days from one quarter to the next**, on samples of eight to seventeen deals. A fill date derived from a single quarter inherits all of that.

The consequence: a reader shown December 2027 this quarter and June 2028 next quarter concludes that the projection is not a measurement, and everything else on the panel loses its standing. **The section's credibility depends on the headline date being stable enough to be revisited.**

Two options, either acceptable:

- **Drive the projection from a rolling four-quarter pace**, and compare it against the same rolling window a year earlier. Slower to move, and it moves for real reasons.
- **Show the fill date as a range rather than a point**, spanned by the paces measured across the last several quarters. Less striking, considerably more honest, and it survives being checked again in three months.

Whichever is chosen, **the window each pace was measured over must be stated on the panel.**

---

## 3 · Now is not a window

The comparison is labelled last year against **now**. A twelve month window is being compared against a point, or against an unstated window.

Every figure on this panel changes materially depending on the window chosen. **Both sides carry an explicit, matched window** - year to August 2025 against year to August 2026, or the equivalent for whatever is actually measured.

---

## 4 · Two contradictions on one screen

**The chart carries a note that nothing here is a credit volume. Directly below it, an outcome states 52,000 to 61,000 ACCUs.** Both cannot stand. Either the note is scoped explicitly to the projection chart, or the ACCU figure moves out of this section.

**The same improvement is expressed twice in two different units.** The chart says months sooner, the outcome says one quarter earlier. These do not agree with each other. **One unit for one claim, used everywhere it appears.**

---

## 5 · Precision the method cannot support

A projection built on quarterly samples of eight to seventeen deals is quoted to a tenth of a month. **A single decimal place on a projected date asserts a precision that nothing upstream of it supports**, and it is the kind of detail a technical reader notices first. Round it to whole months, or express it in whole quarters and use that unit consistently.

The same applies to the ACCU range. A range is the right form. It needs the basis of its width stated - what varies between the low and high figures.

---

## 6 · State the population, and state that it is not this module

The footer correctly says the rates are measured across earlier projects rather than this module. Keep that. It is the most important sentence in the section and it should not be the smallest text on the panel.

Add to it: **how many projects, over what period, and what makes a project eligible to be counted.** A stated population of forty one is credible. An unstated one invites the reader to assume it was chosen to suit the answer.

---

## 7 · One row is doing less work than it could

The three comparison rows describe the field operation. The improvement with the strongest measured evidence behind it is not among them: **the rate at which a personalised soil carbon assessment can be put in front of a landholder has gone from one every 23.6 hours to one every 6.4 hours, at a marginal cost of about two cents each.** That is roughly three and a half times, it is measured, and it is upstream of every other rate on the panel.

If the row selection is deliberately restricted to inputs that feed the fill model, this one qualifies - it sets how many landholders can be reached per week, which sets everything downstream. Consider whether it belongs in place of, or alongside, one of the existing rows.

---

## 8 · Redundancy to cut

Each row states the before value, the after value, and the difference. **The difference is arithmetic the reader has already done.** Two of these three can go, and the row gets quieter without losing anything.

The legend under the chart names three series. **Two of them are self-evident from the marks** - a solid line against a dashed line reads as current against previous without being told. Keep the name of the shaded interval, because that one genuinely cannot be inferred.

---

## 9 · The interval belongs on both curves or neither

The current pace carries a shaded interval. The previous pace does not. **Two curves compared without comparable uncertainty are not comparable**, and the asymmetry implies the previous figure is known more precisely than the current one, which is the opposite of the truth.

---

## 10 · Unchanged

The toggle between the projection and the step times. The chart as the dominant element. The note distinguishing a projection at fixed field capacity from a forecast of demand, which is the most trustworthy sentence in the section and should not be shortened. The outcome expressed in time to first issuance.

Australian English. **No em dashes anywhere, including titles.** Spaced hyphens instead. Nothing modelled presented as measured. Unconsented landholders never identifiable.
