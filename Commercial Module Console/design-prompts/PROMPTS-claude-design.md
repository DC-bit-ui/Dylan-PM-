# Prompts for Claude Design

Send these **one at a time, in this order.** Each is a single intent so you can keep the thread focussed and see clearly whether it landed before you open the next one.

**Do not attach my renders.** They are solutions, and sending them collapses the space you are trying to open. The brief and the images are for you, to judge what comes back against. What goes across is the problem, the material and the outcome.

Order is deliberate. One is small and calibrates the working method. Two is mostly subtraction. Three is the real build.

---

## 1 · The fulfilment panel

> **What we want a reader to feel**
>
> They look at this for two seconds and think: there is plenty, and we are in control of it.
>
> **What it does now**
>
> It shows five per cent filled against ninety five per cent empty. That is true, and it is the most pessimistic true story available from this data. It answers *how full is it* at month one, when nobody is worried about how full it is at month one.
>
> **The material, all of it real**
>
> 13,409,709 hectares eligible. 355,200 qualified. 125,000 is the module target. 5,990 signed.
>
> Read together those numbers say something quite different from what the panel says. There is 107 times more eligible land than the module needs, and the qualified pipeline on its own is 2.8 times the target. Supply is not the constraint here. Conversion is. The panel currently hides that completely.
>
> **The question**
>
> What does abundance look like when the quantities span four orders of magnitude and a linear bar physically cannot hold them?
>
> **Yours to decide**
>
> Form, scale, motion, whether this is one object or several, and whether *fulfilment* is even the right name for a panel whose real subject is supply.
>
> If you think the fill bar is right and we have this wrong, say so and show us why.

## 2 · The credits tab

> **What we want a reader to feel**
>
> The scarcity, before they read a single word. At the moment they have to read three screens of reasoning to arrive at it.
>
> **What it does now**
>
> It is a well argued document. Every claim on it is true and sourced, which is exactly the problem: the argument is carried entirely in sentences, and the strongest fact on the page is delivered as a caption.
>
> **The material**
>
> The Reef Credit scheme has issued 65,547 credits in nine years. 42,055 are already retired. So 23,492 unretired Reef Credits exist anywhere in the world. This one land holding offers 31,400.
>
> The Nature Repair market has two registered projects nationally and has issued zero certificates. This land would be the third project in the country.
>
> ACWIS has published its method and issued nothing, anywhere.
>
> **The question**
>
> Scarcity is a physical sensation rather than a statistic. What does it look like when the thing sitting on this land is larger than everything the market has left?
>
> **One thing to keep and promote**
>
> First refusal. You invented that device and it is the best thing on the tab: costs nothing, commits nothing, holds the volume while terms are drawn. It is currently a button underneath a paragraph.
>
> **One thing to cut**
>
> The three numbered bands are our reasoning, not the product. They belong behind a single control, the way the framework rigour already does.
>
> **Yours to decide**
>
> Everything else.

## 3 · The ESG and climate reporting environment

This is the big one. Send it on its own and give it room.

> **The reader**
>
> A carbon desk professional at a large mining company. Their company filed its first mandatory climate report this year. It already chose to take a full audit over its Scope 1 and 2 emissions when only a lighter review was required, and by FY2029 it faces a full audit over everything, with its legal protection expired.
>
> **The fact that should shape this entire environment**
>
> A full audit means the auditor **re-performs the calculation.** Not reviews it. Re-performs it, from retained records, with every transformation reproducible.
>
> So the pressure on this reader is not reporting, and it is not cost. Their compliance costs are published, audited and modest. The pressure is evidence. A framework list with progress bars is a status report, and they already have those.
>
> **What we want them to feel**
>
> When they enable this capability: *nobody else can show me this.* Not because we cover more frameworks, but because we hold every link between a hole in the ground and a number inside an audited disclosure.
>
> **What we actually hold, and it is the raw material**
>
> Georeferenced sampling points with dates and depth increments. Laboratory certificates from accredited labs, with method codes. A gazetted method determination and its version. A calculation engine with a build and a parameter set. An aggregated figure with a stated reporting boundary.
>
> That sequence is the substance. How it becomes a surface is entirely open.
>
> **What binds this particular reader, and it is not what you would guess**
>
> They have **not** adopted the voluntary nature frameworks. Do not build on those.
>
> What they have committed to publicly, with dates, is natural capital accounting on a national science agency's methodology. They have **one** account today. Their own milestone for next year is **three.**
>
> And they have a nature goal of **two million hectares** of land under conservation, restoration or regenerative practices by 2030. They have delivered **246,000**, and almost all of this year's increase came from a single project.
>
> That gap is public, dated, and theirs. It is the most interesting thing on this reader's desk and nothing currently on our screen speaks to it.
>
> **The AI should feel like a colleague who has already done the work**
>
> Not a chatbot that explains standards. Something that assembles the actual artefact, and then tells them, without being asked, what it could not evidence and why.
>
> We suspect the list of what it *could not* evidence may be the most valuable thing on the screen, because it is the only part that starts a conversation rather than ending one. Test that idea rather than accepting it.
>
> **The one hard line**
>
> We produce contributing inputs. We never imply we produce their report, their disclosure, or their assurance opinion. That boundary needs to be legible in the interface, not just understood by us.
>
> **The question**
>
> What does an evidence chain look like when it has to feel rigorous enough for an auditor and effortless for a buyer, at the same time, on the same screen?
>
> **Yours to decide**
>
> All of it. Including whether *environment* is the right container, and whether this belongs inside the marketplace at all.

## 4 · The ESG card

Only send this once three has come back, because the card is a window into the environment and there is no point designing the window first.

> The capability cards each carry a small working preview of what sits behind them. The ESG and climate reporting card now has a real environment behind it, and its preview is the least differentiated of the five.
>
> What we want is for someone scanning five cards to understand, without clicking, that this one holds the evidence rather than reporting on it.
>
> The preview is yours. So is the question of whether a preview is even the right device for this particular capability, given what is now behind it.

## 5 · Mine rehabilitation

Hold this until you have chosen the property. It needs a real DROVER run behind it or it will come back generic.

> **The situation**
>
> Queensland's specification for returning mined land to grazing requires landform stability and erosion **comparable to surrounding non-mined landforms.** That is a reference-area comparison, which is precisely what our DROVER analysis already does: it benchmarks a property against least-disturbed land within ten kilometres.
>
> **The evidence gap, in the operator's own words**
>
> One large operator's most recent rehabilitation report records thousands of hectares disturbed and **zero hectares achieving completion**, with the stated reason that vegetation has not matured to a point where long-term sustainability can be demonstrated. Their monitoring is manual transects, plus remote sensing used only for erosion.
>
> **What we want to achieve**
>
> They cannot currently say *when* a hectare will meet its criteria. We can show a measured trajectory against a reference distribution, continuously, between their field surveys. The outcome to design for is a reader who can put a date on something that currently has no date.
>
> **Two honesty positions that must survive into the design**
>
> We have no track record in mining rehabilitation, so this is a validation against their existing field survey rather than a replacement for it. And whether a regulator accepts this kind of evidence against completion criteria is untested. The surface should be confident about the measurement and candid about the standing.
>
> **Yours to decide**
>
> Everything, including whether this belongs beside the other capabilities or is a different kind of thing altogether.

## What to watch for in each response

**Did it reject anything?** Every prompt invites disagreement. A response that accepts all of our framing has probably not thought hard enough about it.

**Did it name what it chose not to do?** Good work has edges.

**Did it answer the question or fill the brief?** The questions are the point. A response that satisfies every line and never engages the question has treated the prompt as a spec.
