# Module holder console: the next direction

An edit to the console you have built. It carries one governing principle, one correction to how the module and its territories relate, and a set of changes to what several views are for.

---

## The governing principle

**Visual-led, with as little copy as possible.**

Meaning is carried by shape, weight, position and colour. Words appear only for a name, a value, a unit or a date.

**This is not an instruction to make anything plainer, and it must not produce a wireframe.** A view carrying four confident marks and eleven words should be more beautiful than the version carrying paragraphs, not less, because the space the copy occupied goes back to the composition. Quiet in the sense that a well-designed instrument is quiet. Generous space, deliberate hierarchy, strong shapes, restrained and confident colour. Not sparse, not unfinished, not grey.

Three type sizes at most. One small fixed set of colours and weights, defined once and used everywhere. One dominant element per view. No explanatory subtext under headings, no captions under figures.

Every time a sentence comes out, the design gets a chance to say the same thing better. Take it.

There is a reason beyond taste. This console sits beside a commercial proposition the buyer will judge on price per hectare. Its job is not to make the argument - it is to make the argument look safe. **A busy screen reads as compensating for something.** A quiet screen with four hard numbers reads as a business that knows exactly where it stands.

---

## Who reads it

A carbon desk professional at a large emitter with an annual, date-stamped compliance obligation. They have committed capital against a module: 125,000 hectares of Australian grazing land, assembled property by property, over a seven year term with five crediting years.

This module is one line in a large book. It will never be the whole answer to their obligation and does not need to be. **What they need from it is predictability, not scale.** Nothing is framed against their total obligation - the denominator is always what this module committed to deliver. And a surplus banks forward rather than being stranded, which a compliance reader will not assume unless told.

Credits are generated on a model and validated by physically sampling a tenth of each cohort annually. Sampling validates the estimate rather than replacing it. Every volume shown is the purchaser's share, net of the landholder share and the reversal discount, and labelled as such.

---

## The model correction

The console allocates the module across catchments, each holding a target contribution that sums to the module target. **That is not how modules and territories relate, and it breaks the moment there is a second module.**

**A recruitment territory is an operational zone.** It is a campaign area worked by a field team, roughly two people - one in the field, one central. It exists before any module and outlives it. It has its own capacity, its own pipeline, its own rate of production.

**A module draws from whichever zones are producing.** There is no fixed one to one allocation, and one zone will eventually feed several modules.

On screen:

- A zone shows **what it has contributed to this module**, and separately **its own capacity and pipeline**. Not a quota it owns.
- Module fill is the **sum of contributions**, not three or four quotas being satisfied.
- Language shifts to **operational zone** or **campaign**. Catchment stays as the geographic descriptor, because that is what the boundary is, but it is not the organising unit of the module.

This scales. A module carved into fixed regional quotas does not.

## Four zones

Year one runs four operational zones, for buffer against the 125,000 hectare target.

Four metrics, defined once and populated for every zone. Where a figure could not be sourced directly it is estimated from a stated basis.

- **Total addressable market** - grazing land inside the catchment. The ceiling.
- **Recruitable hectares** - grazing land that can actually carry a project, after scale, condition and tenure. The real ceiling, and the denominator for penetration.
- **Approachable operations** - grazing enterprises holding that land. The recruitment workload, because field teams talk to businesses rather than hectares.
- **Average enterprise size** - recruitable hectares per operation. The cost per hectare of recruitment, and the most decision-relevant number on the card.

**Across the four zones: 21.7 million recruitable hectares, around 5,850 approachable operations, and pipeline standing at 1.6 per cent of the recruitable base.** That is enough land to support 173 modules of 125,000 hectares. The year one target is 0.58 per cent of it; the year four cumulative target is 5.8 per cent.

Supporting figures worth having on selection:

- **Fitzroy**: 2.64 million cattle, the largest cattle region in Australia. $1.285 billion in cattle meat, 54 per cent of all cattle meat value in the reef catchment. Rainfall 700 to 750 mm, summer dominant.
- **Burdekin**: 1.3 to 1.4 million cattle, third largest in Australia. Over 70 per cent of properties held by the same family for an average of 35 years. Around 39 per cent of the region on practices likely to maintain good land condition, leaving roughly 61 per cent with improvement headroom. Mostly leasehold tenure.
- **Lachlan**: 6,692 agricultural businesses across the overlapping sub-region, $2.38 billion output, 705,000 cattle. Rainfall 767 mm east to 367 mm west.
- **Murrumbidgee**: $2.85 billion output. Grazing and mixed farming in the east, broadacre cropping and irrigated horticulture in the west.

An estimated figure is shown as a plain number with its basis available on demand, not hedged on the face of the card. A reader who wants the working can ask for it; a reader who does not should see a number.

### The number that answers the hardest question

**Average enterprise size varies nineteen-fold across the four zones**, and it is what determines how many conversations a target costs.

| Zone | Hectares per conversation | Reach of one field team per cycle | Modules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burdekin | 16,513 ha | 2,476,950 ha | **19.8** |
| Fitzroy | 8,780 ha | 1,317,000 ha | 10.5 |
| Lachlan | 1,393 ha | 208,950 ha | 1.7 |
| Murrumbidgee | 862 ha | 129,300 ha | 1.0 |

Reach assumes a two-person zone team carrying around 150 live landholder relationships.

**Same two people, twenty times the land.** Give this prominence somewhere. It is the most direct answer available to whether the target is reachable, it explains the zone mix rather than asserting it, and it is how the machine scales without scaling headcount proportionally.

It carries a counterweight that is stronger said first than discovered later: **the northern zones are dry tropics rangelands with lower carbon uplift per hectare than improved southern pasture.** Cheap hectares and slow decisions in the north, higher yield and more conversations in the south. The four-zone mix is the argument, not any single zone.

**One definitional point that matters throughout.** An enterprise is not a property. Southern landholders typically run one and a half to two titles each, so a single conversation covers more land than a single property record suggests. Recruitment counts enterprises, delivery counts properties, and the two are never interchangeable.

## The map

Real catchment boundaries accompany this brief as geometry files. They are hydrological River Regions, already clipped to the coastline, so nothing extends into the ocean.

**Every area shown on screen must be the area of the polygon drawn.** These boundaries do not always match commonly quoted figures - the Murrumbidgee is often cited at 84,000 square kilometres but the hydrological region is 47,376. Both are defensible and measure different things, but a reader who measures the shape against the card and finds a mismatch stops trusting both. The `area_ha` property on each feature is authoritative for that geometry.

### Three tiers, and the gap between them should be large

| Tier | What it is | Count | How it reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operational zones** | Field teams on the ground now | 4 | The subject of the map. Immediately, from across a room |
| **Soil carbon opportunity** | Catchments where the method performs | 27 | Present, quiet, clearly secondary |
| **Water quality opportunity** | Reef-draining catchments where sediment is creditable | 8 | Same weight as carbon, different identity |

Thirty-five shapes at similar weight is a jigsaw. Four bright shapes on a field of quiet ones is a statement: here is the whole opportunity, here is where we work it.

**Nothing outside the set gets drawn at all** - not a fainter boundary, not a dotted outline. Unworked country is land, coastline and the shape of the continent. That absence is what gives the zones their weight.

Adjacent catchments in the same tier need a hairline separation or the Murray-Darling regions merge into one mass.

### The lenses

The four operational zones are **not a lens. They are always on.** What the lens switches is which market lights up behind them: **soil carbon** or **water quality**. Constant foreground, switching background. Two zones sit in both sets, which is worth reading instantly rather than explaining.

### Projects are the figures, zones are the ground

At national extent the map draws zone boundaries and every project in the module. Prospect land is aggregate at that scale and only becomes individual when a reader is close enough to act on one. If a boundary reads louder than the projects inside it, the emphasis is inverted.

Recruitment reaches beyond worked zones through organic enquiry and partner referral, so projects appear nationally rather than only inside the four.

## Regulatory standing, currently absent

A carbon buyer's largest unpriced risk is the method changing under them. The console says nothing about this and now can.

The method review has closed. **Five of six offsets integrity standards were met, the three times cap was accepted, Schedule 2 is intact, and three tonnes per hectare commerciality is preserved with no further review until sunset.** Separately, **33 of this business's projects were scrutinised in that review and no systemic errors were found.**

That last fact is the strongest sentence this product has available. Put it where the reader meets it early, stated plainly with its date, no adjectives.

## One set of funnel numbers

Define each stage once and derive everything from it. The measured rates:

- **Qualified to onboarding: 50 per cent**
- **Onboarding to signed: 40 per cent**
- Close rate on commercial terms issued this year: **50 per cent**

So 125,000 hectares signed needs roughly **312,500 at onboarding** and **625,000 qualified**, against about **1.56 million hectares assessed and eligible** - around two and a half times cover.

**That arithmetic is the console's credibility.** Any figure that cannot reconcile to it is wrong or its stage is misnamed. Live opportunity currently in play is 64,127 hectares, which is a different quantity from assessed and eligible and needs a different label so the two are never read as the same thing.

Four year trajectory if a longer horizon is useful: **125,000 hectares cumulative in year one, 419,000 in year two, 839,000 in year three, 1.25 million in year four.** Each additional zone carries two headcount.

## Delivery leads forward

The delivery view opens on a result for one cohort. The reader's live question is whether they will get what they contracted.

**Lead with position over time.** For each period: what was committed, what has been delivered, what is modelled ahead, and where that leaves them. Delivered accumulates, modelled revises, and the gap between them is the answer.

Position anchors that view structurally rather than by preference - it is the only element meaningful at every point in the module's life, because the commitment and the schedule exist before any hectare does. Everything else is empty or partial for at least half the term.

Beneath it, cohorts as a **schedule of dated delivery events** rather than a list. Each carries its scheduled estimation or validation date and whether it is tracking. Around fifty harvest events across the term, and that cadence is the rhythm of the product.

Harvest timing is not a lever the reader can pull - the method locks a cohort to an annual cadence once estimation and validation sampling have run. What they see is status against the schedule, not a control.

For a reader whose obligation is annual and date-stamped, **flag schedule risk against their compliance year**, not only against our plan. A cohort near a boundary is a different risk from one mid-year.

### Two figures carry the validation story

**Per cohort, a waterfall**: modelled at estimation, the adjustment the sampled tenth made, what issued. Everything shown is post-discount, so no buffer row.

**Across the portfolio, a calibration plot**: every sampled cohort as one point, modelled against validated, the identity line through it. Points sitting on that line is the most persuasive image this product can produce and a technical reader takes it in without a legend. At one cohort it is a single point and visibly so, which is honest. Build the accumulator now so it has history when it matters, and let earlier time states show it filling in.

### The model is alive and should look it

Every modelled figure carries its **trajectory**, not just its value, because the model re-reads this land continuously. When the portfolio number moves, **say what moved it** - a rise of four per cent, of which three points from rainfall in one zone and one from cover recovery in another. Keep the decreases as well as the increases; a panel that only rises is not believed.

No other carbon product can tell a buyer why their forecast changed.

### Additional volume

Below the reader's own cohorts, harvestable volume available from projects **outside their module**, as an expression of interest rather than a transaction. Priced below market and above their contracted rate, with delivery cost taken from the credits produced so the reader receives the surplus.

## Marketplace, grouped by whose land

Five capabilities of material weight, grouped not by product category but by **whose land they read**:

| Capability | Whose land |
|---|---|
| **ESG reporting agent** - obligations across every framework, evidence in hand, what is missing, drafted output | all of it |
| **Ecological co-benefits** - what else this ground generates, quantified on their own properties | land they hold credits on |
| **Mine rehabilitation monitoring** - ground cover, erosion and soil carbon across post-mining landforms | land they operate |
| **Acquisition intelligence** - land condition and monetisable potential before they bid | land they do not own yet |
| **Property potential** - which of their own holdings are worth registering | land they own that is not working |

They bought this pointed at other people's grazing country, and it reads any land. Three of the five concern their own estate, which for a large miner is a far bigger surface than the module.

The reporting agent is the destination rather than a peer - everything else produces measurement and the agent turns it into filed disclosure. **One-click reporting is the goal**, so build toward that rather than a document library.

**Cards invert so the finding leads.** *1.23 million hectares of your acquisition pipeline already screened* opens harder than *Acquisition screening*. The finding is proof the work is already done on their ground, and proof belongs before the pitch. **Preview on my land becomes the primary action**, with enabling following it. Enabled capabilities need a disable path from the same place.

**The offer arrives as an event, not as furniture.** When a new instrument becomes available on land they already hold, that is a finding, and it arrives as the derived insight at the top of the view. *Reef Credits have come online across 13,600 hectares of your Fitzroy projects* is information about their asset; *buy Reef Credits* is an advertisement. Fire rarely, dismissible, staying dismissed. If they are short in a compliance year, say so in the same breath.

## Horizon AI

The conversational surface reasons across the whole console and returns an answer with the relevant chart beside it rather than a block of text. It takes attachments, because a spreadsheet of targets from a sustainability team is how personalisation will actually happen.

There is no separate personalisation surface. Scope stays narrow: **it records the reader's own commitments and reflects them against the delivery position**, offering to mark a stated target on the charts as a reference line. It adds their context to what is shown; it does not change what is shown. Everything reversible, nothing that leaves the console in a state they cannot get out of.

Surface it early rather than at the end. The claim that this is a living system rather than a reporting dashboard rests on it.

## The property view, and getting back out

A property opens from its point on the map and its row in the list. Inside, the reader is looking at an individual farm - its land, its baseline, its measurement history, its projected yield. The only part of this product that is a place rather than a portfolio.

**Returning is obvious, permanent and predictable.** A visible control always on screen, present at every scroll position, returning the reader to **exactly where they left** - same view, same time state, same scroll position, with the property still identifiable. A named path reads better than a back arrow because it says both where you are and where you land, and it keeps the module present while the reader is inside one farm.

Entering from the map returns to the map; entering from the list returns to the list.

It has to work from a shared link on someone else's machine. Every route, not just this one. A demonstration is run by someone who did not build it.

## Time states

**Design the earliest state first.** It is the hardest and it is the one that sells, and it is a strong filter - an element that cannot say something useful when empty probably should not exist.

At that state no credits have been issued and first issuance is more than two years away. The job is to make that wait read as a plan rather than a void. Every element that will hold a figure later is present, carrying what will appear there, when, and what will produce it. An empty calibration plot with axes drawn and the first sampling quarter marked is a promise with a date attached, which beats a filled mock and beats an absence.

The module may be well short of full when the first cohort credits, because early-signed projects reach their crediting year while recruitment continues. Recruitment detail lives on the fulfilment view; delivery carries the finished summary once the module completes, and while filling continues, what the remaining recruitment does to the delivery schedule.

---

## What stays out

Anything describing how the work is done rather than what was delivered. Stage dwell times, weekly rates against internal targets, cycle-time improvements from internal automation. That granularity invites the reader's analysts to performance-manage the delivery team rather than assess the module. Where a process improvement matters it shows up as an earlier date on the fill projection.

No commentary on market price. Price is not something this business controls, and the reader knows their own position better than we do.

---

## Constraints

- Australian English, no em dashes anywhere including titles, spaced hyphens instead.
- Three type sizes at most, one shared colour and weight set.
- Modelled figures described as modelled, never as measured or validated.
- All volumes are the purchaser's share, labelled as such.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable.
- No dead ends. Every tab, card, cohort and row either goes somewhere or is visibly inert.

## Yours to decide

Proportion, grid, type, colour, motion, chart forms, component structure, how a zone's contribution is expressed alongside its own capacity, how the lenses transition, and how enabling a capability animates the console reorganising around it.

The brief is the reader and the argument. Where something already built does one of these jobs better than what is described here, keep yours and say why. And where a choice exists, take the simpler one.
