# How fast we fill - condensing the two views

## What this section is doing

The module commits to assembling 125,000 hectares of grazing land inside about two years. The reader is a carbon desk professional at an energy major, miner or commodity trader, deciding whether to commit capital against that. They have done the arithmetic and know it is more land than has been signed in any comparable period.

This section is the answer to the question they will not ask out loud: what changed in the machine that makes that credible, and what does it buy them.

**Forward projection is the substance of this section, not a risk to be managed.** The reader is a commercial counterparty and expects a view of the future - a fill date, a first issuance quarter, an ACCU volume inside the term. Project freely. The thing that earns trust is not hedging, it is that the basis of the projection is visible: what was measured, over what window, across how many projects, and what is being held constant.

---

## The problem worth solving

There are two blocks of measures on screen and they overlap heavily. **First contact to signed contract appears in both, at 84 days, with a 19 day improvement, twice.** Hectares contracted per field week appears in both under two different names. Only one measure in the lower block is not already above it.

The interesting part is that they are not actually duplicates - the upper block compares against **last quarter** and the lower block compares against **last year**. That distinction is invisible, and it produces a strange implication when the same measure shows the same 19 day gain against both windows: it says nothing improved in the three quarters before this one.

Your instinct that Step times should be the route into the improvements behind the predicted time reduction is the right shape. The two blocks are trying to answer two questions:

- **Is it getting better right now?** Quarter on quarter. A momentum question.
- **Is it structurally better than it was?** Year on year. A capability question.

For someone committing to a seven year term, the capability question is the one that matters. Momentum measured on quarterly samples of eight to seventeen deals is mostly noise, and a headline that swings between quarters will read as a marketing figure rather than a measurement.

**The proposal, if it holds up when you build it:** one block of measures, comparing year on year, carrying the full set. The lower block then has nothing left to say and folds away. Step times becomes the mechanism sitting under the projection, which is what it should have been.

Take a different route if you find a better one. What matters is that a reader meets each measure once.

## The measures

Five distinct things are currently being tracked across the two blocks:

| Measure | Now | Movement |
|---|---|---|
| First contact to signed contract | 84 days | 19 days faster |
| Farm visit to landholder decision | 19 days | 7 days faster |
| Hectares added to the pipeline | 148,400 ha | 26,800 ha more |
| Hectares contracted per field week | 640 ha | 170 ha more |
| Farm visits per field agent each month | 13 | 4 more |

A sixth worth considering, because it sits upstream of all of them and has the strongest measurement behind it: **the rate at which a personalised soil carbon assessment can be put in front of a landholder has gone from one every 23.6 hours to one every 6.4 hours**, at a marginal cost of about two cents each. That sets how many landholders can be reached in a week, which sets everything below it.

## Things that are getting in the way

**The bars are close to full length in all four rows.** Because the rows carry days, hectares and rates, each bar is presumably scaled to something of its own - so lengths look comparable across rows and are not. Worth deciding what the bar is measuring against, or whether the bar is earning its place at all. The small marker for the prior period is doing more work than the bar is.

**The same applies to the dots in the lower block.** The three changes there are roughly 18, 36 and 44 per cent, and the dots sit in about the same place. If that block survives in any form, the position wants to mean something.

**The improvements are notated four different ways.** Two rows use a tilde and no sign, two use a plus. Two deltas are dim, two are in the accent. A reader works out the convention before reading the numbers. One convention across every row, whatever it is.

**"3 to 5 months sooner - range across the four measures below, not a high and low forecast"** is a genuinely good sentence and an unusual construction. The thing it leaves open: if all four measures improved, does the reader take the effect as 3 to 5 months in total, or 3 to 5 months from each, compounding to something much larger? Right now it can be read either way. Saying which would strengthen it.

## Projection, since it is welcome here

Two things this frees up.

**The credit volume note can go.** The chart currently carries a line saying nothing here is a credit volume, while an ACCU range sits directly below it. The reader is buying a credit volume. Let the section say so.

**The consequence deserves more room than it has.** *One quarter earlier, first issuance Q2 FY29 against Q3 FY29* and *52,000 to 61,000 ACCUs more inside the seven year term* is the part the reader actually came for, and it is currently the smallest block on the panel. The fill date is the mechanism; the issuance timing and the volume are the outcome. Consider whether the hierarchy is the right way up.

Where a projected figure appears, what it rests on is worth stating alongside it - the measured rates, the window, the number of projects, and what is being held constant. That is not a hedge, it is the difference between a projection and a claim, and this reader can tell.

One inconsistency to resolve: the fill chart expresses the gain in months, the outcome expresses it in quarters, and the two do not agree. Pick the unit that suits a seven year term and use it throughout.

## Provenance

The footer notes the rates are measured across earlier projects rather than this module. That is the most important sentence in the section and it is currently the smallest text on the panel.

It would carry more weight with the population attached - how many projects, over what period, and what made a project eligible to be counted. A stated population reads as confidence. An unstated one invites the reader to assume it was chosen to suit the answer.

The toggle between the projection and the step times. The chart as the dominant element in the projection view. The note separating a projection at fixed field capacity from a forecast of demand.
