# Representing projects on the map

The boundaries and the zone tiering are working. Projects are the part that has not caught up. Six changes, in order of what they cost.

---

## 1 · Every mark on the map is accounted for in the cards below it

There are project marks sitting outside the four zone boundaries - east of the southern zones, and inland and coastal of the northern ones. The zone cards below add exactly to the module total, so those outside marks are not module land.

Either they belong to the buyer and the cards are understating, or they do not and they should not be drawn as though they do. **A reader who counts dots and then reads the cards must arrive at the same answer.**

If land recruited outside the four zones is real and should be shown, the accounting has to follow it - a zone card is not the only container, and the module fills from wherever the land comes from. If it is not module land, it comes off this surface entirely.

---

## 2 · Size the mark by hectares

Every project currently draws at the same size, so a 16,000 hectare northern property and an 800 hectare southern one look identical.

That is a missed opportunity and it works against the argument. **Northern holdings are roughly ten to twenty times the size of southern ones**, and that difference is the whole reason the zone mix is what it is. Sizing the mark by area makes the point without a table, a caption or a sentence: a handful of large marks in the north, a scatter of small ones in the south, and the reader understands the strategy before anyone explains it.

Scale by area rather than by radius, or the large ones will overwhelm everything.

---

## 3 · The zone is a vessel, and it should fill

Right now the zones look the same at five per cent recruited as at one hundred. Progress is legible only by counting marks or reading a card.

**Let the zone polygon itself carry its fill.** Empty outline when nothing is contracted, progressively filled as land is signed, solid when the zone has delivered what it is going to deliver.

This gives the map a job it does not currently have. It also makes the time control genuinely worth using: stepping from pre-launch to first crediting becomes four vessels filling, which reads as progress before a single number is looked at. That is the most valuable thing this map could do and it costs no copy at all.

---

## 4 · State should read as a sequence, not as a palette

Projects move through an ordered progression - contracted, registered, baselined, in a cohort, credits issued. That is a sequence, and sequences read badly as five different colours.

**Use one hue and vary the fill.** Hollow at the start, filling through the middle states, solid when credits have issued. A reader then sees maturity as density rather than having to learn a key, and the pale mark currently used for the credited state stops looking like an error.

Keep hue for something categorical, if anything needs it at all.

---

## 5 · Overlapping marks need resolving, not hiding

By first crediting the marks collide and clump. These are the buyer's assets, so they should not be thinned or dropped.

**Merge colliding marks into one, sized by their combined hectares and carrying a count.** Zooming separates them. That keeps the total area on screen honest, which matters because the composition is doing the work of showing how much land there is.

---

## 6 · The lenses should not delete the module

Switching to the water lens currently removes the southern zones and their projects from the map. **The four zones are constant foreground** - they are where the buyer's land is, and that does not change with which market is being viewed.

What should change is the background: which national catchments light up, and which of the four are marked as carrying that instrument. Two of the four have a water instrument and two do not, and that is exactly the fact the water lens exists to show. It cannot show it if half the module disappears.

The soil carbon lens has the opposite problem in a smaller way - the national set arrives at a saturation close to the working zones, so the gap between tiers narrows just when it should hold. **The four zones stay the brightest thing on the surface under every lens.**

---

## While in there

The provenance note under the zone cards has grown to a paragraph of small grey type. It is the longest piece of copy on the surface and it sits directly beneath the numbers it qualifies. Collapse it to a line, with the detail available on demand.

The card headers say "across three catchments" while four cards are shown.

---

## The test

At national extent, before reading anything, a reader should take:

**Where the work is** - four zones, unmistakably.
**How much land is in each** - from the size and number of marks.
**How far along each is** - from how full each zone reads.
**Which zones carry a second instrument** - under the water lens.

If any of those needs a number to be understood, the map has not done its job.

---

## Constraints

- Australian English, no em dashes anywhere including titles, spaced hyphens instead.
- No explanatory copy on the map. Names and values only.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable, so prospect land stays aggregate at national extent.
- Three type sizes at most, one shared colour and weight set.

## Yours to decide

Mark shape, the fill treatment for zones and for projects, how merged marks are drawn, how the lens transition animates, and the scale relationship between the smallest and largest project mark.

The requirement is that the map reads as a composition rather than a scatter, and that the size of the operation is visible before it is read.
