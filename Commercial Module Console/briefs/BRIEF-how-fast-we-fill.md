# Please edit the "How fast we fill" section

An edit to the section you have already built in the commercial module console, not a rebuild.

**What stays:** the section itself, the toggle between the two views, the projection chart as the dominant element of the first view, and the note separating a projection at fixed field capacity from a forecast of demand.

**What changes:** the measures underneath. The section currently carries a set of comparison rows across two blocks. Those are replaced by the measured funnel set below, which comes from the sales and campaign systems and carries its own populations and measurement dates. One block, each measure met once.

**What is new:** the fill outcome becomes a range rather than a single date, for the reason given in view one.

The rest of this brief restates the whole section so you have it in one piece.

---

## The reader and the question

A carbon desk professional at an energy major, miner or commodity trader. They are considering, or have just committed to, a soil carbon module: 125,000 hectares of Australian grazing land, assembled property by property, over a seven year term with five crediting years. The land does not exist yet as a portfolio. It has to be recruited, one landholder at a time, mostly from a standing start.

They have done the arithmetic and they know that is a lot of land in a short time. The question they are carrying, and will not ask directly, is: **how fast can you actually sign land, and what makes you confident of that?**

This section answers it. It is the evidence layer under a claim made elsewhere in the console.

Forward projection is the point of the section, not a risk to be managed. This is a commercial counterparty, not a retail customer. Project the fill date, the first issuance quarter and the credit volume. What earns trust is not hedging but showing what the projection rests on.

---

## Two views, one toggle

The toggle stays as it is. The two views keep their jobs.

**Fill projection** - when the module reaches 125,000 hectares, and what that means for issuance.

**The recruitment engine** - the measured rates that produce that projection. This is the second view, and it is the route into the mechanism behind the prediction.

The relationship between them is the argument: the projection is not a promise, it is arithmetic on rates that can be inspected. A reader should be able to move from the outcome to the mechanism and back without losing their place.

---

## View one: fill projection

**The module.** 125,000 hectares. Campaign opens December 2026. Five crediting years inside a seven year term.

**The measured recruitment pace**, business-wide across all active campaigns:

| | Hectares registered per week |
|---|---|
| Long window average | 864 |
| Recent window average | 1,963 |

That spread is real and it is the honest input. A single fill date drawn from either one alone will be wrong in a knowable direction, so **the fill outcome is better expressed as a range bounded by the two paces than as a point**. A reader who is shown a range and its basis will trust it more than a date that moves every quarter.

Note for accuracy: these are whole-business rates, not this module's. The module will run its own dedicated field capacity. Whatever projection is drawn should be explicit that it is scaled from measured rates elsewhere rather than observed on this module.

**What the projection resolves to** - the part the reader came for:

- The quarter the module reaches nameplate
- The quarter of first credit issuance
- Credit volume inside the seven year term, as a modelled range

Give these room. The fill date is the mechanism. Issuance timing and volume are the outcome, and they currently deserve more weight than the curve that produces them.

## View two: the recruitment engine

This is the machine that produces the pace above, and it is measured end to end.

### The funnel, as at 1 June 2026

| Stage | Landholders reached | Converts to next | Median days in stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identified | 484 | 42.8% | 1 |
| In conversation | 207 | 28.0% | 8 |
| Farm visit booked | 58 | 67.2% | 1 |
| Farm visit completed | 39 | - | 1 |

### The effort behind it

- 1,136 outbound calls placed, 606 of them on the primary campaign
- 347 unique landholders engaged, 6.3 touches each on average
- **9.2 calls per farm visit booked**, or 10.9 visits per 100 calls
- 74% connect rate across 766 calls in the trailing 90 days
- Median connected call 47 seconds

### Reach at the top of the funnel

A personalised soil carbon assessment, modelled for a specific property, is what opens most conversations. The rate at which one can be produced and delivered:

- **One every 23.6 hours, improving to one every 6.4 hours** - approximately 3.7 times, over recent months to 7 July 2026
- Marginal cost approximately two cents each
- 4% of issued assessments convert to a prospect conversation

This is the constraint that sets everything below it. How many landholders can be reached in a week determines how many enter the funnel, which determines the pace in view one.

### Pace against target

- 7,853 hectares registered
- 864 hectares per week on the long window, 1,963 on the recent
- 4,429 hectares per week required to hit the current financial year target
- Eligible land in the pipeline covers 227.9% of the near-term target

### Channel economics, if it earns a place

Land arrives through more than one channel and they behave differently. This is capacity planning information and a reader doing portfolio thinking will want it.

| Channel | Entered pipeline | Won | Win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner referral | 18 | 16 | 84.2% |
| Direct inbound | 85 | 17 | 17.7% |
| Cold outreach | 12 | 2 | 16.7% |

The interesting content is not that one is better. It is that the referral channel converts far higher and cannot easily be scaled, while cold outreach converts lower and can be scaled almost without limit. A module is built from a mix of both, and the mix is a lever.

Use this only if it strengthens the section. It is secondary to the funnel.

## How the numbers should carry themselves

Every figure here is checkable, and the reader will check. Each one wants its **population and its measurement date attached to it**, close enough to read together - 484 landholders, as at 1 June 2026. A stated sample reads as confidence. An unstated one invites the reader to assume it was chosen to suit the answer.

The figures come from two vintages, 1 June and 7 July 2026. That difference should be visible rather than smoothed over.

Where a figure is projected rather than measured, what it rests on is worth stating alongside it: the measured rate, the window, and what is being held constant. That is not a hedge, it is the difference between a projection and a claim, and this reader can tell.

---

## What the section needs to do visually

**Meaning before reading.** The reader should take the shape of the funnel, the size of the improvement and the position against target before they read a single number. Numbers confirm what the shape already said.

**The measures span very different kinds of quantity** - counts, percentages, days, hectares per week, a multiple. They need to sit together on one panel without the large ones flattening the small ones into invisibility, and without implying a comparability they do not have.

**A stage that has not improved is as informative as one that has.** Showing where capacity was added, and by implication where it was not, is what makes the improved parts believable. Do not hide the flat parts.

**Explanatory copy is a signal that a form is not carrying its meaning.** If a sentence is needed to explain what a mark means, the mark wants changing rather than annotating. Values, units, dates, windows and populations are labels, not explanation, and they stay.

## Placement and behaviour

This section sits beneath the module's current recruitment position, as evidence for it rather than as the headline.

It is present at every stage of the module's life and its content changes as the module ages. Before the module opens it carries recruitment capability, because recruitment is the entire question. Once crediting begins it carries delivery capability alongside it - sampling throughput, model validation, issuance turnaround.

Selecting a measure should scope the view to the part of the funnel it acts on, so the reader can see the thing rather than be told about it.
