# Scaffold pass: get every major feature present

The console is being shown end to end for the first time tomorrow. This pass is about **completeness rather than polish**. Everything the system claims to do should be visible and navigable, even where it is thin. A missing capability reads as a gap in the product; a thin one reads as work in progress, which is fine.

Priorities are tiered. If time runs short, tier one is the demo.

---

## Tier one: the demo does not work without these

### 1 · The tabs must switch the panel

Selecting a tab currently scrolls a single continuous panel rather than changing what is in it, so every tab contains every other tab's content. The marketplace sits below the whole of Delivery, and Delivery sits below the whole of Hectares.

Each tab owns its own panel. Selecting Hectares, Delivery or HORIZON marketplace replaces the panel content and returns to the top of it. This is the single biggest fix available and it also resolves most of what has made the panel feel crowded.

Content belongs to exactly one tab:

- **Hectares** - recruitment. The pipeline, fill projection, recruitment engine, where the land came from, region monitor.
- **Delivery** - production. Cohorts, credits, validation, schedule, climate exposure, properties.
- **HORIZON marketplace** - what else this land can do.

### 2 · Show the console changing shape when a capability is enabled

This is the most important thing in the product and it is currently unproven. The marketplace reads "9 available, 0 enabled", which is a promise with no demonstration behind it.

**Ship the demo with one capability already enabled and one enabled live in front of the room.** The enabled one has visibly added its own section or tab, so the reader can see that buying something changes the console rather than unlocking a document. The tabs across the top become a statement of what has been bought, which is the whole idea.

Enabling should feel like the console reorganising around a new capability, not like a settings toggle.

### 3 · The personalisation surface

A distinct surface where the console learns what this reader cares about. Not a settings form. **Conversational and led by HORIZON**, consistent with the Ask HORIZON bar already at the bottom of the console.

It asks, in its own words rather than as fields: what volume is needed and in which compliance years, which reporting frameworks they answer to, what land they already hold, and what they are optimising for. It accepts uploads - an obligation statement, a sustainability report, a term sheet - and reads them rather than filing them.

What it produces is a profile that visibly reshapes the rest of the console: which metric leads each tab, what the delivery schedule is measured against, what the insight line watches, and which marketplace capabilities surface first.

**For the demo, arrive pre-populated.** The console should already look tailored, and it should say why - a line somewhere along the lines of *delivery targets set from your FY29 to FY31 surrender profile, read 12 August*. A personalisation feature that starts empty demonstrates nothing.

It also gives the early time states something the reader can do, which they currently lack.

### 4 · Delivery leads forward, not backward

Delivery currently opens on a result for one cohort out of ten. The reader's live question is whether they will get what they contracted.

Lead with **position over time**: what was committed, what has been delivered, what is modelled for the periods ahead, and where that leaves them. Delivered volume accumulates, modelled volume revises, and the gap between them is the answer.

Position is the only element on that tab meaningful at every point in the module's life, because the commitment and the schedule both exist before any land does. That is why it anchors.

Then the cohorts, which are the only thing on the tab the reader can act on and are currently buried below climate.

### 5 · The marketplace, reduced to five capabilities

Nine items, several overlapping, most of them thin. Reduce to five, each of material weight, and **group them by whose land they read**, because that is how this reader thinks about assets.

| | Capability | Whose land | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ESG reporting agent** | all of it | Their reporting obligations across every framework they answer to, what evidence is in hand against each, what is still missing, and drafted output. Updates as new measurement lands |
| 2 | **Ecological co-benefits** | land they already hold credits on | What else this ground generates - reef and sediment, catchment water, biodiversity - quantified on their own properties |
| 3 | **Mine rehabilitation monitoring** | land they operate | Soil carbon, effective rainfall, ground cover and erosion read across post-mining landforms and buffer country, the same way they are read across farmland, with a dated record against completion criteria |
| 4 | **Acquisition intelligence** | land they do not own yet | Land condition, ecological state and monetisable potential on ground before they bid on it |
| 5 | **Property potential** | land they own that is not working | Assess their own holdings and register eligible country as projects, moving them from buyer to producer |

Closure liability folds into rehabilitation monitoring, since it is the output of the same measurement. Catchment water and the nature baseline fold into co-benefits. Value chain footprint folds into the reporting agent. Denser sampling comes out of the marketplace entirely - it is a Delivery upgrade rather than a use case. The product-category filters come out with them.

**The reframe worth carrying into the copy:** they bought HORIZON pointed at 125,000 hectares of someone else's grazing country. It reads any land. Three of these five are about their own estate, and for a large miner the rehabilitation and pastoral estate is a far bigger surface than the module. The module was the trial.

### 6 · The cards, rebuilt

The current cards read as a software pricing grid, which is the wrong register for someone spending real money. All nine carry identical weight so nothing leads, each opens with a name and two lines of explanatory description, and the genuinely strong content is buried small and low.

**Invert them so the finding leads.** *1.23 million hectares of your acquisition pipeline already screened* is a far stronger opening than *Acquisition screening*. *24 sites eligible*, *8 years of record per site*, *11 catchments in scope* - these are proof that the work has already been done on their ground, and proof should precede the pitch. The explanatory description can go; the name identifies and the finding persuades.

**"Preview on my land" becomes the primary action.** It is the best interaction in the marketplace and it is currently a secondary button. Seeing a capability rendered on ground they know is what converts. Enabling comes after previewing rather than beside it.

Drop "Adds a Rehabilitation section" as a line of copy. What it describes should be visible when it happens, per tier one item two.

### 7 · The property entry works

A property opens its own view from both the list and the map, showing the land, its baseline, its measurement history and its projected yield. Mulloon is the entry point at the earliest state.

This is the only part of the console that is a place rather than arithmetic, and it is what an ESG team would put in front of a board.

## Tier two: present, allowed to be thin

**Validation as two figures.** Per cohort, a waterfall - modelled at estimation, the adjustment the sampled tenth made, the reversal buffer, what the regulator issued. Across the portfolio, a calibration plot with every sampled cohort as a point, modelled against validated, the identity line through it. At one cohort it is a single point and visibly so, which is honest and is what the existing note about one quarter not being a trend already says in words.

**Spot against locked**, over time, from a named and dated source. Frame it as the cost of certainty rather than as a win, because the line crosses in both directions across seven years.

**Harvest as a decision.** A harvest can be triggered ahead of schedule at the reader's discretion, which makes timing a genuine lever on realised volume. Give the decision its inputs rather than a bare button: how long the cohort has been ready, the seasonal model trend for that region, whether ground conditions allow clean sampling, whether it falls in a window the landholders can accommodate, and the modelled yield now against the modelled yield if held.

**Projects distributed nationally on the map.** Recruitment reaches beyond worked catchments through organic enquiry and partner referral, so projects appear across the country. Region boundaries become an overlay showing where field teams work rather than the organising geography.

**Ask HORIZON answers something.** Even a small set of prepared answers is better than a bar that does nothing, because a dead input in a demo reads as vapour.

**The properties list**, five visible with the rest on scroll, ranked by ACCUs produced then nameplate. Each row carries ACCUs to date, area, nameplate, predicted ACCUs per hectare per year, and the instruments the property carries.

## Tier three: if there is room

Yield attribution when the modelled figure moves - portfolio yield up four per cent, of which three points from above-median autumn rainfall in one region. Schedule risk flagged against their compliance year rather than only against our plan. The full dated record.

## Demo hygiene

**Label illustrative figures as illustrative.** Several numbers on the recruitment panel are placeholders rather than measurements. In an internal showing that is entirely fine, but unlabelled numbers get repeated in decks a week later. A quiet marker on any panel carrying indicative data protects everyone.

**No dead ends.** Every tab, every time state, every card and every property row either goes somewhere or is visibly inert. A demo dies on a click that does nothing.

**Every empty state carries its reason and the date it resolves.** Never a bare zero, never a blank container. An empty calibration plot with its axes drawn and the first sampling quarter on it is a promise with a date attached, which is stronger than a filled mock and more honest than an absence.

---

## Constraints

- Australian English, and no em dashes anywhere including titles. Spaced hyphens instead.
- Modelled figures described as modelled, never as measured or validated.
- Credits are generated on the model and validated by physically sampling a tenth of each cohort. Sampling validates the estimate rather than replacing it.
- Market prices carry their source and date.
- Where a capability touches an actual credit rather than an assessment, the transaction step names the licensed entity that executes it.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable.

## Yours to decide

Proportion, grid, type, colour, motion, chart forms, how the personalisation surface is entered, and how enabling a capability animates the console reorganising around it.

Presence beats polish on this pass. Where something already in the build does one of these jobs better than what is described here, keep yours.
