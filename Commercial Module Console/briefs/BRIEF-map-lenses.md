# Please edit the map in the commercial module console

The hexagons, the project markers, the panel and the interaction model all stay. What changes is the frame, the default colour treatment, and the set of lenses sitting over it.

---

## What the map is for

The reader is a carbon desk professional at a large emitter, looking at a 125,000 hectare soil carbon module assembled property by property across Australian grazing country. The map is the only part of the console that is a place rather than arithmetic.

It carries two arguments, and the difficulty of the surface is that they are different geographies:

- **Where the work happens.** Four hexagons, each a two to three hour drive radius around a field agent's base town. This is deployable capacity, and the honest answer to "can you actually reach this land".
- **What the land can earn.** Catchments. Grazing country with a measurable carbon opportunity, and separately, grazing country where a water quality instrument exists.

Hexagons are operational. Catchments are commercial. They overlap but they are not the same shape and should never look like they are.

---

## What is weak now

**The frame is cropped to the eastern seaboard.** Western Australia and most of South Australia are outside it. The reader sees four zones on the east coast with no sense of the country they sit in, which makes the operation look either bigger than it is or arbitrarily placed. The continent is the denominator, and four hexagons against the whole of Australia is a stronger and more honest statement than four hexagons filling a frame drawn around them.

**The recession is on by default.** Land outside the zones arrives already dimmed, so the reader's first sight is a country mostly switched off. Darkening is a good instrument being spent before there is anything to spend it on. At rest no comparison is being made, so there is nothing for the dimming to answer.

**The thematic lenses have lost their subject.** This is the most important item here. With catchments muted to background furniture, selecting soil carbon or water dims a map whose catchments were already dim. The lens changes the mood without changing the subject: the reader selects it, something gets darker, and they learn nothing.

---

## The default state

**Whole country, full colour, undarkened.**

The continent entirely visible and reading as itself. Australia's land colour runs from red interior to green coastal margin and that range is an asset, not noise to suppress. The four hexagons sit on it, project markers inside and outside them, nothing dimmed.

A reader should be able to look at this for ten seconds and take away: here is a continent, here is where we have people, here is where the land is.

---

## The lenses

A lens is a question asked of the same map. Selecting one is what earns the recession, because a lens is a claim that some of this country matters more than the rest for one purpose.

| Lens | The question | What comes forward |
|---|---|---|
| **Default** | Where are we working | Nothing dimmed. Hexagons and projects only |
| **Boots on the ground** | Where can we reach | The four hexagons, frame closed in, everything else recedes |
| **Soil carbon** | Which country can earn ACCUs | 31 catchments, solid olive |
| **Reef Credits** | Where a water credit market already exists | 8 catchments, solid blue |
| **ACWIS** | Where a water credit method exists but no market yet | 12 catchments, striped blue |
| **Catchments** | Where are the lines | All 219 boundaries, dashed and unfilled, no recession |

Six entries is a long list for a control that should be glanceable, and they are not all the same kind of thing. Three answer "what can this land earn" and are directly comparable to each other. Boots on the ground is operational. Catchments is reference. Grouping them that way rather than presenting a flat list of six is probably the difference between a control that reads instantly and one that has to be worked through. How is yours.

The reader should be able to get back to full colour undarkened country in one move.

### The principle underneath

**A lens selects a subject, and the subject should be the brightest thing on the screen.** Not the least dimmed. The brightest. Under a thematic lens the hexagons step back to outline, because they answer a different question and will otherwise keep winning attention they have not earned in that state.

**One lens, one treatment.** Everything a lens lights up gets the same style. No second category inside a lens, and no catchment picked out as special because it also appears in a different lens. A lens asks one question and gives one answer: "which country can earn carbon" is answered by 31 catchments, full stop. Anything else needs a legend, and a legend is a sign the marks are not carrying their own meaning.

Sixteen catchments do carry both a carbon and a water instrument, and that fact matters commercially. It is not a fact for this map. It is a portfolio construction insight, which makes it ours rather than the reader's: it tells us where to place field agents. The reader does not allocate our field capacity. The version that matters to them is specific rather than national, and it already has a home in the ecological co-benefits capability and the property view.

---

## Boots on the ground

Darkens the country outside the four zones and **closes the frame in on them**, so the reader goes from continental context to operational detail in one move. Within the crop the four zone catchments sit in a mid tone under the hexagons, which gives the hexagons something to be on rather than floating on dimmed ground.

The close crop and the darkening are exactly right as the answer to "where can we reach", because that question genuinely is about four places rather than a continent. The default frame carries the context; this lens spends it.

One thing to get right: the crop wants to be a composition rather than a bounding box around the zones. The two Queensland zones and the two NSW zones sit a long way apart, and a naive crop reads as two maps rather than one operation. The gap is real and honest but it needs deliberate handling.

---

## Soil carbon

**31 catchments, 112 million hectares.** An almost continuous arc from the Queensland central highlands down through the northern NSW slopes, the inland Murray Darling, western Victoria into the south east of South Australia, with Tasmania and two south west WA catchments detached from it.

This is the shape of Australian grazing country with a measurable soil carbon opportunity, and it is a much bigger statement than four hexagons.

---

## The two water lenses

There are two water credit markets, not one, and they are separated because they are genuinely different: different scheme, different geography, different buyers, different liquidity. A reader weighing up co-benefits needs to know which market they would be transacting in.

**Reef Credits: 8 catchments, 36 million hectares.** A tight Queensland coastal run from Cape York to Baffle Creek. Small, concentrated, and it should look concentrated. This is the one water market with an observable price.

**ACWIS: 12 catchments, 57 million hectares.** The Murray Darling. Condamine-Culgoa, Macquarie-Bogan, Border Rivers, Namoi, Gwydir, Castlereagh, Murray Riverina, Goulburn, Loddon, Upper Murray, and both southern recruitment zones. Bigger ground, earlier market.

**Both are blue.** Two markets but one domain, and the colour should say so. The distinction is carried by fill state rather than hue: **Reef Credits solid, ACWIS a light blue wash with blue interior stripes.** Solid reads as established and striped reads as provisional without anything having to explain it. Because both share one grammar the marks stay self describing whether the reader sees them separately or together, so if a single combined water lens reads better than two, that option is open.

The maturity difference has to survive, because 57 million hectares of unpriced eligibility must never read as equivalent to a transacting market. It lives in the fill state and in what each lens says about itself.

**Why this matters commercially.** All four recruitment zones carry a water instrument. Fitzroy and Burdekin through Reef Credits, Lachlan and Murrumbidgee through ACWIS. Every zone we have people driving to is country where the same hectare can carry a second instrument.

## The catchment lens

**Dashed, unfilled boundaries across the whole country.** All 219 river regions, so the lens can name any catchment the reader points at rather than only the ones we have opinions about.

- **Hover names it.** Any catchment, focus or not. A reader wondering what they are looking at over central Queensland should be able to find out by pointing at it.
- **Click focuses it.** The catchment becomes the subject: named, area available, and the projects and hexagon coverage inside it legible. A second click or escape returns to the whole country.
- **The four recruitment zones are flagged in the data**, so they can be drawn differently from the other 215 with no lookup.

This lens is the odd one out and should behave differently from the others: it is a reference layer rather than an argument, so it adds lines without taking colour away. No recession at all.

At 219 features the dashed lines read as a low weight graticule rather than a jigsaw, which is the right outcome. The risk to watch is the opposite of clutter: too faint and the lens does nothing. Worth testing at the zoom levels the console actually uses.

A dashed line does two useful jobs here. It reads as "reference, not thing" so it does not compete with the hexagons, and it survives crossing from red interior to green coast in a way a fill never could.

---

## Findings from testing

**A stripe needs a wash behind it or it loses its colour.** This decides whether the ACWIS treatment works at all. Stripes drawn straight onto the basemap read dark rather than blue, because the perceived colour of a hatched area is a mix of the lines and the gaps, and at national scale the gaps dominate. Thin blue lines over dark brown land average to dark brown, and the catchment ends up reading as textured land rather than a blue region.

The fix is a low opacity blue wash under the stripes so the gaps are tinted too. Around 0.22 to 0.28 was enough in testing. It stays clearly lighter than the solid reef fill so the established against provisional reading survives, but the hue becomes unmistakable. A coarser stripe pitch also holds up better on the smaller catchments. Goulburn, Loddon and Castlereagh are the size test, not Condamine-Culgoa.

**Darkening may not be needed under the thematic lenses at all.** The reference sheet puts saturated lens fills over completely undarkened land and the lens still reads immediately. A saturated fill against neutral land already produces the figure and ground separation, which means the recession would be solving a problem the fill has already solved. Worth trying with no dimming before adding any, because it keeps the country intact and spends less. Boots on the ground is the lens that genuinely needs it.

**A lime hexagon outline disappears under the soil carbon lens.** The hexagons sit inside the catchments that lens lights up, so a same hue outline over a same hue fill vanishes. The hexagon needs a treatment that survives sitting on top of its own theme colour, probably a light neutral rather than the accent.

**Reef Credits will look small and that is correct.** Eight coastal catchments on a continent is a sparse map next to 57 million hectares inland. The instinct will be to compensate. Worth resisting: the reef market is small, concentrated and real, and the ACWIS ground is large and not yet priced. A map that made them look comparable in weight would be flattering the wrong one.

**Two unfilled conventions now coexist.** The catchment lens is dashed neutral boundaries with nothing inside. ACWIS is a blue wash with blue interior stripes. Different mark, different colour, different place, boundary versus interior, so they should separate cleanly. Worth a look with both visible.

## Composition

The frame no longer changes at rest, so composition has to do the work the crop was doing.

The continent whole, but not necessarily centred. Everything in this product happens on the eastern third, and a frame giving the west its full share of the canvas spends most of the canvas on nothing. Weighting the composition east while keeping the whole coastline visible is the balance to find. The country sitting off centre with the panel taking the western space is one way; there will be others.

## Colour

**Two fills.** `#7f9c18` olive for soil carbon, `#3987e5` blue for both water lenses. Validated against the dark surface with all pairs checked: every check passes, worst pair at protanopia delta E 27.9 against a target of 8.0.

That margin is worth knowing about, because a third fill is genuinely hard to find here. Orange `#d95926` collapses against the olive to delta E 1.7 under deuteranopia, effectively the same colour for roughly one man in twelve. Every violet tested collided with the blue, and teal failed on tritan. Encoding the second water scheme as a fill state rather than a hue sidesteps all of it and lands three and a half times clear of the threshold.

**`#cdfd29` lime is a stroke and marker colour only.** Hexagon outlines, project markers, a focused catchment. It fails the 0.48 to 0.67 lightness band for area fills at 0.928 and passes contrast, which is the correct result for an accent rather than a problem. It has to hold against both red desert and green coastal country, which is a harder test than it sounds and is why it works as a stroke.

## Attached

**`catchments-all.geojson`** - all 219 Geofabric river regions, simplified for web, 246 KB. For the catchment lens.

- `label` display name · `division` drainage division · `area_ha` · `zone` true on the four recruitment catchments

**`catchments-lenses.geojson`** - the 35 curated high value catchments, 90 KB. For the three earning lenses.

- `carbon` true on 31, the soil carbon set
- `water` true on 20, and `water_scheme` either `Reef Credits` (8) or `ACWIS` (12). **`water_scheme` is the field each water lens selects on**
- `zone` true on the four recruitment zones
- plus `label`, `state`, `division`, `area_ha`, `official_name`

Sixteen features carry both `carbon` and `water`. The flags are independent so a catchment can appear in more than one lens, but nothing should be drawn from the combination on this map. They exist so each lens can select its own set, not so overlaps can be marked.

Four features carry `water` without `carbon`: Normanby, Herbert, Haughton and Baffle Creek, which were selected for the water instrument and are Cape York conservation tenure or cane dominated coastal floodplain rather than grazing carbon targets. A deliberate under claim rather than an omission.

**`deployment-zones.geojson`** - the four hexagons, 2 KB. Unchanged except that `water_instrument` is now `water_scheme` and names the scheme.

**`lens-reference-all.png`** - all six states on one sheet, in the validated colours. A legibility check rather than a design: ignore its composition and typography, and read it for the marks.

Geometry source is the Bureau of Meteorology's Australian Hydrological Geospatial Fabric, River Regions v3.3, coastline clipped so boundaries meet the sea correctly.

## Sourcing

ACWIS is the Australasian Catchment Water Improvement Standard, administered by Eco-Markets Australia, the same administrator as the Reef Credit Scheme. It is national rather than reef restricted, which is why the water story reaches the Murray Darling.

Two things that affect what goes on screen. **The twelve ACWIS catchments are our assessment**, since the scheme publishes no catchment list, so nothing should imply it has designated them. And Reef Credits can point at 14 projects with 65,547 credits issued and 42,055 retired, where ACWIS has a published method and nothing else, which is the difference the two lenses have to carry.

## Constraints

- Australian English, and no em dashes anywhere including titles. Spaced hyphens instead.
- Visual led, with as little copy as possible. If a sentence is needed to explain what a mark means, the mark wants changing rather than annotating. Labels, values, units and dates are not explanation and they stay.
- One lens, one treatment. A catchment is never styled differently because it also appears in another lens.
- Reef Credits and ACWIS are different schemes in different geographies at different maturity. Same colour, different fill state, never merged into one figure.
- ACWIS eligibility is our assessment, not a published scheme boundary.
- Catchment names as given. They are official and they will be checked.
- Hexagons are drive time reach, not tenure, ownership or quota. Nothing should imply they are a boundary of any kind.
- The lens sets show where opportunity is, not what can be delivered. Carbon lights 112 million hectares, Reef Credits 36 million, ACWIS 57 million; recruitable land inside the four zones is 21.7 million and the current pipeline is 355,200 hectares. A buyer's analyst will do that division, so the distinction wants to be structurally obvious rather than caveated in a footnote.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable.
