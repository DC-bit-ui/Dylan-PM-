# The Credits tab, again

## What I got wrong

You asked for two things: make it sell, and make sure the frameworks stand up. I did the second thoroughly and quietly dropped the first. What came back was a compliance audit with three bands, three verbs and a paragraph reference. Accurate, and completely inert.

**I built the due diligence as the shopfront.** The accuracy instruction was a constraint on the sell, not a substitute for it.

There is a second error underneath it and it matters more. I said stop leading with volume. Half right, wrong fix. **Volume on its own says nothing. Volume with a denominator says everything.** I removed the number instead of giving it something to be measured against.

## The numbers I should have looked up first

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reef Credits** | 65,547 credits issued across the entire scheme since 2017. 42,055 already retired. So **23,492 unretired credits exist in the world.** This land offers 31,400. |
| **Nature Repair market** | **Two registered projects nationally. Zero certificates issued.** As at 28 May 2026. |

So the Reef Credit line on this land is **48 per cent of every Reef Credit ever issued, and 1.3 times the entire remaining float.** And a biodiversity project here would be the **third project in a national market that has issued nothing at all.**

That is not a product catalogue. That is a position in a market that barely exists yet, and it is the strongest thing on the tab by a wide margin.

## Three arguments, in order of strength

### 1 · Market position, not volume

Put every volume next to the size of the market it sits in. It requires no framework claim, no assertion about their reporting, and nothing we cannot see. The facts do the work.

The current card says *31,400 credits available to FY31*. The same card could say the holding is roughly half of everything the scheme has ever produced. Same number, same data, a completely different sentence.

### 2 · Coincidence

This is the one no card can make and a map makes for free.

They operate in the **Bowen Basin.** The module holds **Fitzroy and Burdekin** land. Those are the catchments the Bowen Basin drains into, and both carry live Reef Credit instruments.

A catchment water outcome in a catchment where you hold approvals is worth considerably more to a mining company than the credits cost, because a miner's binding constraint is not tonnes. It is the ability to open and operate: approvals, conditions, water licences, community agreements. Measured improvement in the right catchment is currency in exactly that conversation.

**And the console already knows where their sites are.** The Mining rehabilitation capability on the other tab counts 24 of them. Put their operational footprint and the module's land on one catchment map, and where the two coincide is the entire argument. It also quietly answers the open question about whether Capabilities and Credits are one surface, because this is the join.

The contrast carries it too. Pilbara and Olympic Dam do not coincide with anything, which is what makes Fitzroy and Burdekin mean something.

### 3 · Stacking, and the marginal cost of the second instrument

The same hectare produces soil carbon now. It can also produce a water outcome and a biodiversity outcome. The land does not change; what comes off it does.

| To add a nature instrument here | To buy it anywhere else |
|---|---|
| land already under contract | new land to diligence |
| measurement already running | new measurement to trust |
| one counterparty, already signed | new counterparty to contract |
| one registry relationship | new catchment, possibly not yours |

**This is the argument the current cards are closest to making and the one they say least about.** The Environmental Plantings card already says *same registry and retirement path as your existing holding, so it needs no new process.* That is the whole point, in caption type, on one card out of four.

## Meet the objection rather than dodging it

A thin market is illiquid, and a buyer will think that within two seconds of reading *zero certificates issued*. So the surface should say it.

The honest answer is that early position in a market you will need for the next thirty years is strategy rather than speculation, and that a thin market is exactly where position is cheap. A surface that names the awkward part is more persuasive than one that hides it.

This is the same pattern that worked on the reconciliation panel. Naming the worst sampling round is what made the other ten rounds credible.

---

## Where the compliance work goes

Not deleted. Demoted.

Scale, coincidence and marginal cost are the shopfront. AASB S2 paragraph 36(e) and the framework audit are the **reassurance layer behind it**, which is what you show once someone is interested and needs to know it will survive their own legal team. Right content, wrong prominence.

The audit still applies as a hard floor rather than a pitch: no TCFD, no Climate Active, GRI 101 rather than GRI 304, no Scope tags on offset instruments, no claims about the state of their submission, and the issuing entity named on every line.

## The tab's name

*Volume from land you already hold* points straight at the weakest version of the case. The argument is position, coincidence and marginal cost. Worth a rename.

---

## Three things not verified, and the third is load bearing

**The operations on the map are illustrative.** BMA in the Bowen Basin is a defensible assumption, but the actual asset list and the catchments those assets sit in need confirming before this goes in front of them. If the coincidence is wrong the argument inverts.

**The market totals will move.** Any figure quoted as a market total needs a date attached and a way to refresh, or it becomes quietly wrong.

**Whether a Nature Repair project can actually be originated on this land.** The first argument rests on it entirely. If it cannot be registered, *this land would be the third* comes out and the biodiversity line drops back to being an outcome we can measure rather than an instrument anyone can hold.

## Constraints

- Australian English, and no em dashes anywhere including titles. Spaced hyphens instead.
- No price commentary. Marginal cost here means transaction and diligence effort, not dollars.
- Modelled and forecast figures described as such, never as measured, validated or issued.
- Market totals carry the date they are current as at, and their source.
- Issuing entity named on every line. Reef Credits and ACWIS water credits, Eco-Markets Australia. Soil carbon and environmental plantings ACCUs and biodiversity certificates, the Clean Energy Regulator.
- Volumes are the purchaser's share.
- Visual led, with as little copy as possible. Three type sizes is the ceiling.
- Dark surface. `#7f9c18` olive for carbon instruments and `#3987e5` blue for water instruments are the validated fills; `#cdfd29` lime is stroke, marker and low opacity fill only. No new hues, and the magenta goes.
