# Map pass: three changes, all reductions

A tight pass on the national map. **Every change here takes something away.** Nothing is added. Nothing else changes.

The principle throughout: **visual-led, with as little copy as possible.** Information moves into shape, weight and fill. Words appear only for a name or a value. Fewer colours, fewer marks, more meaning in the form.

---

## 1 · Every mark on the map is accounted for in the cards below it

There are project marks sitting outside all four zone boundaries. The zone cards below add to exactly the module total, so those marks are land the cards do not account for.

**A reader who counts marks and then reads the cards must arrive at the same answer.** Either that land belongs to the module and the accounting follows it, or it is not module land and comes off this surface.

This is the first thing a technically literate reader will test, and it is a removal rather than an addition.

---

## 2 · One hue for project state, varying fill

Project state is an ordered progression - contracted, registered, baselined, in a cohort, credits issued. Sequences read badly as separate colours, and five hues on a map that already carries zone fills is more palette than the surface can hold.

**Use a single hue and vary the fill.** Hollow at the start, filling through the middle states, solid once credits have issued.

Maturity then reads as density rather than as a key to be learned, and the pale mark currently used for the credited state stops looking like a rendering fault. **This reduces the palette rather than extending it.**

---

## 3 · The lenses stop deleting the module

Switching to the water lens currently removes the southern zones and their projects from the map entirely.

**The four zones are constant foreground under every lens.** They are where the buyer's land is, and that does not change with which market is being viewed. What changes is the background: which national catchments light up, and which of the four are marked as carrying that instrument.

Two of the four have a water instrument and two do not. That contrast is the entire reason the water lens exists, and it cannot be seen while half the module disappears.

The soil carbon lens has a milder version of the same problem - the national set arrives at a saturation close to the working zones, so the gap between tiers narrows exactly when it should hold. **The four zones stay the brightest thing on the surface under every lens.**

---

## Two free cuts while in there

The provenance note under the zone cards has grown into a paragraph of small grey type sitting directly beneath the numbers it qualifies. **Collapse it to one line**, with the detail available on demand.

The card headers say "across three catchments" while four cards are shown.

---

## Deliberately not in this pass

Three further changes are parked until this one is seen, so they do not confuse the read:

- Sizing project marks by hectares
- Merging colliding marks into one
- Any progress treatment applied to the zone shapes themselves

**Leave all three alone.** Marks stay a consistent size, overlapping marks stay as they are, and a zone polygon is geography rather than a gauge.

On that last one: a zone's recruited land is scattered across the whole catchment rather than concentrated in part of it, so filling the shape would put a non-spatial quantity onto a spatial form and imply the filled portion is somewhere. Progress belongs in the panel, where it has room and a stated denominator.

---

## Where progress lives

Progress is already on the map, and it does not need a new encoding. **Marks accumulate as land is signed and their fill deepens as projects mature.** That is spatially honest, because every mark is a real place, and it is exactly what change two above makes legible.

Stepping through the time control should therefore read as the map populating and maturing. Precise figures stay in the panel, where a denominator can be stated.

## The test

At national extent, before reading anything, a reader should take **where the work is** and **roughly how much of it there is**. If either needs a number to be understood, the map has not done its job.

---

## Constraints

- Australian English, no em dashes anywhere including titles, spaced hyphens instead.
- No explanatory copy on the map, and no legend or key added. Names and values only.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable, so prospect land stays aggregate at national extent.
- One shared colour and weight set. This pass should end with fewer colours on the map than it started with.

## Yours to decide

The fill treatment for zones and for project marks, how the lens transition animates, and how the tier separation is held.

The requirement is a map that reads as a composition rather than a scatter, and that ends up quieter than it started.
