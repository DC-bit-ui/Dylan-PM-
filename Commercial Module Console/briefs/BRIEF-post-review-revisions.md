# Console revisions from the product review

An edit pass across all tabs following a full walkthrough with the commercial and operations leads. Most of this is removal. The structure and the map were called right; the density and the exposure were not.

Two principles came out of the session and they explain almost every change below.

---

## Principle one: report outcomes, never process

Several panels currently show how the work gets done - average time in each funnel stage, registered hectares per week against internal targets, farm visits per agent, cycle-time improvements from internal automation.

That level of granularity invites the reader's analysts to performance-manage the delivery team rather than assess the module. The console reports **performance against commitment**. How that performance is produced stays internal.

This is not about hiding poor numbers. The same data will be shown during due diligence, in a frame we choose, and it is tracked internally regardless. It simply does not belong on a surface the buyer reads weekly.

Apply the test to anything new: does this describe an outcome the reader contracted for, or does it describe how we work?

## Principle two: cut the explanation, and cut the variety

There is a strong tendency in this build to explain. Subtext under headings, footnotes under charts, a caption under every figure. Where the title and the chart are clear, none of it is needed.

The same applies to the visual system. There are noticeably too many type sizes, too many colours, too many shades. Three type sizes is a reasonable ceiling. Fewer colours, fewer weights, fewer greys.

The test for every element: **does this need to be here at all.** Not "is it useful" - most of it is mildly useful - but does its absence cost the reader anything.

## Module fulfilment tab

### The headline changes

The tab currently leads with pipeline depth as a multiple of target. The first question this reader has is how much of the 125,000 hectares is actually signed, so **lead with progress against target as a percentage**. Pipeline depth stays, subordinate to it, because it answers "can you finish" - but it is not the headline.

### Region cards

Keep the shape thumbnails. They do real work: a reader matches a card to the map without reading a name.

Everything under them comes off the card and moves to selection: the percentage and hectare detail, the opening date, the instrument tags, the number of people on the ground, the aggregate hectares contracted line. Cards carry a name, a shape and one figure.

### Add: addressable market and penetration

Each region gains **total addressable market** - how much grazing and pasture land the catchment actually holds - and a **penetration figure** against it, showing pipeline and signed land as a share of what exists.

This is the most useful thing that can be added to this tab. It reframes the recruitment story from "we have signed X" to "here is the size of the ground we are working and how far into it we are", which is a far stronger answer to whether the target is reachable.

### Pipeline stages

Stage one becomes **Horizon assessed and eligible**, with its explanatory line removed. All stage subtext comes off.

Stages four and five currently overlap - one describes commercial terms going to the landholder, the next describes a contract being issued. Reword so they are distinct steps.

### Remove entirely

**The recruitment engine view.** Stage dwell times, weekly registration rates against internal targets, and recruitment source breakdown. Useful internally, wrong here. The fill projection view stays and is well liked.

**"What changed in the way we work".** The three before-and-after operational rows and the ACCU delta beneath them. Where a process improvement matters, it shows up in the fill projection as an earlier date. That chart is the right expression of it.

### Language on credit volumes

Everything shown to this reader is **their** volume, net of the landholder share and the reversal discount. That approach is right and it should not change. What must change is that the label says so - purchaser's credit volume, or equivalent - everywhere a volume appears, so there is never a question about which number is being quoted.

Replace "nameplate" with **at capacity** throughout.

### Timeline

"All land physically sampled" becomes **reference baseline established across all properties**.

Consider ending the recruitment timeline at "all projects declared with the regulator". Everything after that point is delivery and belongs to the delivery tab.

### Climate and land condition

Moves wholesale to the delivery tab. It concerns the land producing credits, not the land being recruited.

### Tab name

"Hectares" is not quite right, since the tab covers capacity and fulfilment rather than a unit. Module capacity or module fulfilment both read better. Low priority.

## Delivery tab

### Cohorts are drawn as coloured dots, not shapes

Cohorts must not be drawn as polygons or connected by lines. Within a catchment, cohort membership will be dispersed and cohorts will overlap each other spatially - a boundary drawn around one implies a territory it does not hold.

**Colour the project dots by cohort.** Nothing else. The cohort cards are working well and should stay as they are.

### Harvest timing comes out

The interaction offering an early harvest is removed. The crediting method locks a cohort to an annual cadence: once a model estimation event and its validation sampling have run and credits have been applied for, that cohort cannot report again until the minimum period has passed. Harvest timing is therefore not a lever the reader can pull, and offering it creates an operational problem rather than a feature.

What replaces it is **status against the schedule**. Each cohort shows its scheduled estimation or validation event date and whether it is tracking to it. The date takes the prominent position where the harvest control currently sits, at a size that suits a headline rather than a button, using the empty space above it.

### Add: additional volume available outside the module

Below the reader's own cohorts, a section showing **harvestable volume available from projects outside their module**, which they can register interest in.

The commercial shape: priced below market but above their contracted rate, with the delivery cost taken out of the credits produced so the reader receives the surplus. It is a low-friction way to take more volume from land inside the same system, and it is the demand mechanism the module was always meant to create.

Keep it simple - an expression of interest rather than a transaction. Something along the lines of registering a bid on additional volume.

### The validation figures

**Remove the reversal buffer row.** Everything shown is post-discount, so the buffer is an internal mechanic rather than something the reader needs to follow.

The remaining progression - modelled at estimation, adjusted by the sampled tenth, issued - stays, along with the plot showing modelled against validated accumulating over time. That plot was well received and gets stronger with every cohort.

### The model monitoring panel stays

The per-cohort revisions with their causes - a portfolio increase from rainfall anomalies, a reduction on another cohort from seasonal conditions - are among the strongest things on the tab. Keep the reductions as well as the increases. A panel that only ever moves upward is not believed.

### Remove

**The spot against locked price chart.** Price is not something this business controls or should comment on, and the reader knows their own position better than we do.

**"What moved the model yields".** It repeats the model monitoring panel above it.

**The recruitment-closed summary.** It belongs on the fulfilment tab.

### Land performance list

Five rows visible with an internal scroll behind them, since this list will run to dozens or hundreds of projects.

On the fulfilment tab it sorts by land size, projected return, or ACCUs per hectare per year. **On delivery it sorts by volume delivered to date.** Each row also carries whatever additional instruments that property supports.

It sits low in the hierarchy, which is correct - it is not a headline. Its job is diagnostic: when a cohort underperforms, the reader opens this to see whether one or two projects are dragging it.

### Needed before this tab can be finished

The per-module delivery schedule sets the floor volume by period from year three onward. Every fulfilment chart on this tab should be measured against it rather than against an assumed profile.

## Marketplace

This tab landed well and needs little. Two additions.

**A disable path.** Once a capability is enabled, the reader needs a way to turn it off again from the same place they turned it on.

**The enable moment is the centrepiece of the demonstration**, so it should be unmistakable. A capability is enabled, it appears in the row above, and its section is immediately available with real findings in it - the reporting agent showing what it has found across their business and a draft report already drawn from continuous monitoring. The console visibly gains a capability rather than unlocking a document.

On the property potential item: the reader holds around two million hectares of grazing country, including roughly six hundred thousand hectares of pastoral land in Western Australia. Much of that sits in country where soil carbon uplift is marginal and sampling is expensive. The item should be framed as an assessment of which of their holdings are worth pursuing rather than an assumption that all of it is, since the honest answer will be that only part of it is viable and saying so is what makes the assessment worth having.

## Personalisation becomes Horizon AI

**Remove the personalisation tab.** Its function moves entirely into the conversational surface at the foot of the console, which is where this reader will expect it.

### The conversational surface

Rename it **Horizon AI**. The prompt becomes something like *How can I help you today?* rather than an instruction to ask about the module - it is an assistant, not a search field.

Add an **attachment control** so a document can be brought into the conversation directly. Uploading a spreadsheet of targets from a sustainability team is the main way personalisation will actually happen.

It reasons across the whole console - fulfilment, delivery, and any enabled capability - and returns an answer with the relevant chart alongside it rather than a block of text.

### What personalisation means, scoped

The sanctioned behaviour is **recording the reader's own commitments and reflecting them back against the delivery position**. They state a retirement target for a given year, Horizon answers when the schedule reaches it, and offers to mark that commitment on the relevant charts so it appears as a reference line thereafter.

That is the whole scope for now. The reader is not reconfiguring the console, altering delivery schedules or restructuring the interface. Personalisation adds their context to what is shown; it does not change what is shown.

Anything the reader changes must be reversible, and nothing they do should be able to leave the console in a state they cannot get out of.

### For the demonstration

Prepare four or five interactions that show Horizon drawing across different parts of the console in one answer, and surface the conversational layer **early** in the walkthrough rather than at the end. The whole argument for this being a living system rather than a reporting dashboard rests on it.

---

## Density, and the summarised view

The most condensed of the layout options was singled out as the best of them - headline figure, short label, very little else. That reaction is the clearest available signal about density across the whole console.

**Move every view toward it.** A tile with a number and a few words beats a paragraph in every case tested during the review.

---

## Working state

The property entry into the individual farm view did not open for other viewers. It needs to work from a shared link, not only locally.

The conversational surface should return real answers during the demonstration. A dead input reads worse than an absent one.

---

## Constraints

- Australian English, and no em dashes anywhere including titles. Spaced hyphens instead.
- Three type sizes at most. Fewer colours, fewer shades, fewer weights.
- Modelled figures described as modelled.
- Credits are generated on the model and validated by physically sampling a tenth of each cohort.
- All volumes are the purchaser's share, net of discount, and labelled as such.
- Landholders who have not consented are never individually identifiable.

## Yours to decide

Proportion, grid, type, colour, motion, chart forms, and how the conversational surface absorbs what the personalisation tab was doing.

Where something already in the build does one of these jobs better than what is described here, keep yours.
