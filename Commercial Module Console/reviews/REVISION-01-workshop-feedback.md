## 1 · The funnel, in the buyer's language, led by hectares

Agreed on both counts, and putting hectares in the numerator changes the panel's argument.

**And the numbers move to hectares**, at roughly 2,400 ha average project size:

| Stage | Properties | Hectares |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed and eligible | 512 | **1,228,800** |
| Landholder engaged | 148 | 355,200 |
| Plan agreed | 92 | 220,800 |
| Boundaries mapped, terms out | 61 | 146,400 |

**The consequence you will want to look at.** In hectares, the top of the funnel is **1.23 million ha against a 125,000 ha target**. That number cannot be shown as progress. It has to be shown as **coverage**, and coverage is a far better answer to the buyer's real worry than progress is.

So the Position panel carries two numbers, not one:

- **Are we on track** → 12,000 ha under contract, on curve
- **Can you finish** → **9.8× coverage.** 1.23m ha assessed and eligible against a 125,000 ha target, and 10.9× against the 113,000 still to contract

That second number is the one that closes the room, and it is only available to a business that has pre-screened an entire region. It is the fill-story version of the moat.

## 3 · Funnel improvements instead of risk, with flags split out

Your instinct is better than what I had, and the reason is worth naming: **at T+0 the buyer's question is not "what could go wrong", it is "are you any good at this".** A risk panel answers the first. Velocity answers the second, and it is a management-capability claim rather than a disclosure.

**Panel: how fast we fill.** All of it derivable from HubSpot stage timestamps, so it is live rather than asserted.

- **Days from first contact to contract**, this quarter against last
- **Days from farm visit to decision**, this quarter against last
- **Hectares added to pipeline this quarter**
- **Conversion by stage**, trending
- **Hectares contracted per field week** — throughput, which is the one that translates to a fill date

Framed as: our process is getting faster, and that fills your module sooner. It compounds, and it is verifiable.

**One thing to accept deliberately.** This cuts both ways. A quarter where cycle time lengthens is now published to the buyer. That is the price of the panel and it is consistent with the radical-transparency position, but it should be a decision rather than something discovered later.

**Then a separate risks and flags panel** for climate and land condition. Fire outlook, drought exposure, flood recovery, cover below reference. This is a cleaner split than what exists now: the current build puts "limits on us" and "conditions of the land" in one list with different labels. **Two panels is better than two labels.** Our performance is one thing, the land's condition is another, and a buyer reads them with different parts of their brain.

## 4 · The obligation panel

**Your instinct is right and I would drop it from the console.** Four reasons, and the last one is the real one:

1. It states a customer's compliance shortfall back to them from our own derivation. If we are wrong on operated versus equity basis, or on the baseline decline factors, we look careless on the one thing they know better than us.
2. It is a sales argument sitting on a surface Kieren has just ruled is for delivery capability.
3. It needs per-prospect configuration, which breaks the one-shell, configurable-tabs productisation. Every demo becomes bespoke work.
4. An authorised representative of an AFS licensee telling a client the size of their carbon liability is close enough to advice that legal will want a look, and the answer will not come back quickly.

**The middle path that keeps the value.** State the module's **nameplate capacity plainly** — 375,000 ACCUs a year at full ramp — and never state their gap. Every buyer in the room knows their own surrender number to the tonne. They will do the division themselves, silently, and a comparison a reader performs is more persuasive than one you print. The sizing work stays in the deck and the proposal, where it belongs and where it can be checked before it is said.

## 5 · HORIZON Marketplace

**I think this is the strongest structural idea anyone has had on this project, and it solves three problems at once.**

**It makes "everything else layers in underneath" literal.** You do not navigate to Remediation. You **add** Remediation. Kieren's ruling stops being an ordering instruction and becomes the architecture.

**It kills the empty-tab problem completely.** Tabs are what you have bought and they are populated. The marketplace is what you have not. There is no longer such a thing as a tab with nothing in it.

**And it is Matthew's monetisation model, which he described repeatedly and which has had nowhere to live.** The fidelity ladder, unlocking the next layer, usage credits, *"you can answer all this, but you're going to need this pack"*. A marketplace is the surface that argument has been missing.

### Three things to get right

**Credits cannot be marketplace items.** This is the sharp one. "Monetise (credit opportunities)" as you wrote it would put ACCUs, Reef Credits and ACWIS into a priced catalogue. ACCUs are financial products and AgriProve is an authorised representative of an AFS licensee. **The marketplace carries services, data products and reports. It never carries credits.** The workaround is clean and probably better anyway: list the *assessment*, not the credit. "Reef credit eligibility assessment" is a service with a price. "Reef credits, 1,840/yr" is a financial product with a volume.

**Check the name.** Marketplace already means something at AgriProve. There is a **MarketPlace pipeline in HubSpot**, and `260324_Marketplace_product_pricing_UPDATED.xlsx` and `CERES_Gen6_Marketplace_Pricing.xlsx` are both sitting in the PM folder. Confirm HORIZON Marketplace does not collide with an existing product before it gets named on a screen.

**A marketplace of unavailable things is a roadmap wearing a costume.** It needs at least two items genuinely purchasable today or the whole device reads as vapour. Honest availability state on everything.

### What is in it

| Item | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Additional analytes on soil sampling | **Available** | Already a real upsell |
| Denser sampling regime | **Available** | Above the 4 strata × 6 sample floor |
| Land condition monitoring · cover, erosion | **Available**, manual | Verterra dependency is real and disclosed |
| Water accounting · VWBA alignment | **Near** | Framework's runoff method is already what the partner model runs |
| Higher resolution imagery · 30m to 5m | **Near** | |
| Reef and ACWIS eligibility assessment | **Near** | Assessment, not credits |
| EUDR geolocation evidence | **Near** | Satellite history to 2015 already held |

| Natural capital accounts · CSIRO frame | **Roadmap** | |
| 3D rendering | **Roadmap** | |
| Biodiversity, nitrogen fixation | **Roadmap** | Method does not exist. Say so. |

Each item carries: what it needs from you, what it returns, when it is available, and what it costs. Services can carry a price. That is the whole point of moving them here.

**One consequence worth noticing.** This resolves the five-sections-sharing-a-nav-bar problem that has dogged every version. Module and Delivery are the product. Everything else is a catalogue. That is a much more honest description of the business than a nav bar of equals ever was.

## 6 · The map

Confirms the read, and the Frontier precedent is the right one. Converted projects at their real locations, prospect locations around them, so the geospatial capability is the picture rather than a claim in a panel.

**One constraint to build in from the start.** Prospects have not consented to appear in a module holder's console. Matthew named this himself: the holder gets the daisy chain *"without breaching the primacy"*. So **converted projects are named, prospects are unnamed density**. Assessed and eligible land shows as coverage, not as identifiable properties.

That is the honest line, and it also happens to look better. A field of anonymous assessed land with a handful of named projects inside it is exactly what capability looks like. Naming 512 farmers' properties to a mining company looks like something else.
