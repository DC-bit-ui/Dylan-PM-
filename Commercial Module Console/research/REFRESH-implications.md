# What the refresh changes

Read the delta section of the handover first if you read nothing else. This is what it means for the console and the pitch.

---

## 1 · The single most important thing in it

BHP restated its nature goal from "at least 30 per cent" to **at least 2 million hectares** under conservation, restoration or regenerative practices by FY2030, and published progress against it for the first time.

**246,000 hectares delivered. 3.8 per cent.** And almost the entire FY2026 increase of 148,000 ha came from **one project**, Jackboot in South Australia.

They need roughly **1.75 million more hectares in four years.** Their own FY2027 milestone is 42 per cent of goal hectares in plan or under practices, from 12 per cent today. And natural capital accounts: **one today, three required next year.**

Alongside that, BHP published its land total for the first time: **7.3 million hectares owned, leased or managed.** Much of it pastoral grazing. Our method basis is grazing land.

**This is no longer a soft statement of unmet demand. It is a quantified, dated, public four-year shortfall on land they steward.** The dossier predicted their own estate was the larger contract. Both sides of that equation are now published numbers.

**The caveat that keeps us honest.** The goal is scoped to land BHP *stewards*. Module hectares on third-party grazing land do not count toward it. So this is an argument for work on their estate, not a way to inflate what a module delivers. Getting that wrong would be caught immediately and would cost more than the claim is worth.

---

## 2 · The compliance case shrank and something better replaced it

BHP now discloses its own Safeguard position: **~848,000 credits surrendered for FY2025 compliance**, a **US$23m** obligation at BHP share, and a forward ceiling **under US$75m per annum to FY2030.**

Cost pain is now audited, published and modest. **Any urgency framing built on cost is dead**, and the A$17m figure we were carrying should go.

What replaced it is stronger. Three things BHP said in its own FY2026 report:

- "BHP may also sell carbon credits ... or **originate carbon credits through project development or direct investment**"
- Voluntary credits for the long-term goal are marked **"(expected)"**
- The CY2050 residual needing offset is quantified for the first time at **3 to 5 MtCO2-e a year**

And on the balance sheet, **US$49m of prepayments for future delivery of carbon credits**, up from US$32m.

**They already forward-contract credit delivery.** A commercial module is structurally that instrument. That removes the "unfamiliar structure" objection before anyone raises it, and it is their own audited balance sheet making the argument rather than us.

## 3 · A correction I need to own

My framework ranking put TNFD and GRI 101 Biodiversity in "broadly applicable". For this buyer that is wrong.

**TNFD is not adopted** by BHP: four mentions across 248 pages, all definitional. **GRI 101 Biodiversity is not disclosed.**

Their actual frame is **natural capital accounting on the CSIRO methodology**, where they are behind their own milestone. I have rewritten the ESG prompt to anchor there instead.

Second correction. I built that prompt on **AASB S2 paragraph 36(e)**. The requirement is real and I verified it in the standard, but the refresh could not establish that BHP's disclosure is mapped to that paragraph. So the paragraph reference is out of the prompt. We can cite what the standard requires; we cannot cite BHP's compliance with it.

Third. The assurance story changed shape and got better. BHP took **reasonable assurance over Scope 1 and 2** for FY2026 when the statutory floor was limited. They have already chosen to be audited harder than required, which makes audit-grade evidence a demonstrated preference rather than a future problem. The refresh found **no BHP statement about FY2027 plans**, so we should not imply one.

Two traps worth knowing. **BHP's own climate governance page still names Fiona Wild**, who left for Westpac in early 2025, and the page carries a 2026 footer, so it was in scope of the refresh and was not corrected. Do not brief from it, and do not use any of the three VP names without reconfirming. And AR2026 states 13 per cent of disturbed land is under rehabilitation when 22,600 ÷ 154,500 is 14.6 per cent, so do not repeat the 13 per cent unqualified.
