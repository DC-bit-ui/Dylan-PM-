# Catchment data for the region cards

Researched 18 August 2026 for Lachlan, Murrumbidgee and Fitzroy. Every figure carries its source. Where a number had to be derived rather than read, that is stated.

---

## The headline finding

The three catchments hold **31.1 million hectares** between them. On the two where a grazing share is published, grazing land alone comes to **15.5 million hectares**.

The module needs 125,000 hectares. That is **0.8 per cent of the grazing country in two of the three catchments**, before Lachlan is even counted.

Current pipeline across the three is 355,200 hectares, which is **1.1 per cent of the total catchment area** and under 3 per cent of grazing land anywhere.

**That low number is the selling point, not a weakness.** It is the direct answer to whether the target is reachable: the ground has barely been touched. A buyer asking whether 125,000 hectares is a stretch can be shown that the campaign is operating inside about one per cent of the land available to it.

**One discrepancy to be aware of.** The Bureau of Meteorology gives the Fitzroy region as 142,544 km2. The Fitzroy Partnership for River Health gives 156,000 km2 for "the largest region draining into the Great Barrier Reef". ## Water instruments by catchment

**Fitzroy: Reef Credits, confirmed eligible.** Reef Credits apply across Great Barrier Reef catchments, and a grazing land management method has been approved and released, under which "projects on any grazing land within the Great Barrier Reef catchments may be eligible". The method rewards holding high ground cover ahead of high intensity rainfall to cut sediment run-off. Fitzroy is the largest reef-draining catchment, so eligibility follows. Confidence: high.

Scheme scale as published: **14 projects, 65,547 credits issued, 42,055 credits retired.** Worth knowing before quoting Reef Credits as a material revenue line - this is a young market, and the honest framing is optionality rather than a second income stream of comparable size to carbon.

**Lachlan and Murrumbidgee: not confirmable.** I could not verify ACWIS from any public source. There are clear signals that a national expansion of the reef water quality market beyond Great Barrier Reef catchments exists or is being developed - Meat and Livestock Australia has published on an environmental market opportunity for graziers "expanding from reef to national waterways" - but the article would not serve and no scheme rules, catchment list or method were retrievable.

**Do not put a named inland water instrument on the Lachlan or Murrumbidgee cards until someone internal confirms it.** The technical team will know the current status. Until then those cards either carry no water instrument, or carry it as a stated possibility with its status rather than as an available capability.

Sources: [Eco-Markets Australia Reef Credits](https://eco-markets.org.au/reef-credits/), [GreenCollar on the grazing land management method](https://greencollar.com.au/ground-breaking-reef-credit-method-for-grazing-land-management-approved-and-released-by-eco-markets-australia/), [Queensland Government Reef Credit Scheme](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/coasts-waterways/reef/reef-credit-scheme)

## Suggested card content

**Uninteracted, per card:** catchment name, shape, and one figure - contracted hectares against the region's target contribution.

| Catchment area | 8.47 m ha | 8.40 m ha | 14.25 m ha |
| Grazing land | to be derived | 4.37 m ha, 52% | 11.12 m ha, 78% |
| Assessed and eligible, ours | 149,184 ha | 120,768 ha | 85,248 ha |
| Penetration of grazing land | to be derived | 2.8% | 0.8% |
| Rainfall | 367 to 767 mm | mixed, temperate to semi-arid | 700 to 750 mm, summer dominant |
| Water instrument | unconfirmed | unconfirmed | Reef Credits |

Penetration is the figure that does the work. It converts the recruitment story from "we have signed X hectares" into "here is the size of the ground we work and how far into it we are", which is a far better answer to whether the target is reachable.

## Closing the Lachlan gap, and doing this properly

The published summaries do not give a consistent grazing breakdown across all three catchments, and the Murrumbidgee figure comes from an academic land use classification rather than a national standard.

**The authoritative source is ABARES Catchment Scale Land Use of Australia**, a national spatial dataset that classifies land use to secondary and tertiary levels, including grazing on native vegetation and grazing on modified pastures as distinct classes. Clipped to the three catchment boundaries it produces exact, consistent, defensible hectare figures for every land use class in all three, from one national methodology.

That is a straightforward spatial job for the team already running ArcGIS and reading land at national scale, and it is worth doing before any of these figures go in front of a buyer. It would also let the penetration figure be computed against **grazing land that clears the method**, rather than against all grazing, which is a more honest and considerably more impressive denominator.

Dataset: [ABARES Catchment Scale Land Use of Australia](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/abares/aclump/land-use/data-download)

## Two accuracy flags before this ships

**The Fitzroy area figure** has two published values. Use 142,544 km2, and be ready for the 156,000 km2 figure if anyone has read the regional report card.

**Mulloon.** The demo places Mulloon in the Murrumbidgee. Mulloon Creek, near Bungendore, drains east to the Shoalhaven rather than west to the Murrumbidgee. Moderate confidence, worth thirty seconds with a map. On a console whose entire regional logic is catchment-based, and where water instrument eligibility is inherited from the catchment, putting a named property in the wrong one is exactly the error a technically literate buyer would find.
