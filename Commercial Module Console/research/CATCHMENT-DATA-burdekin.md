# Burdekin, the fourth zone

Same treatment as the other three. Enterprise counts, holding size, output. Sources at the end.

## The headline, and it changes the recruitment maths

**The Burdekin has roughly 500 commercial beef businesses across about 11.8 million hectares of grazing land. The average property runs 28,000 to 30,000 hectares.**

| To sign | Burdekin | Southern NSW at 633 ha average | Southern NSW at 2,400 ha average |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28,000 ha | **1 property** | 44 properties | 12 properties |
| A full 125,000 ha module | **4.5 properties** | 198 properties | 52 properties |

**One conversation in the Burdekin is worth roughly forty in southern New South Wales.** That is the single most commercially important fact about adding this zone, and it is the most direct answer available to whether a 125,000 hectare target is reachable.

It also carries an honest counterweight, below.

## Four things that make this zone genuinely different

**Long tenure is a recruitment asset and a recruitment cost.** Over 70 per cent of properties have been in the same family for an average of 35 years. These are not people who make fast decisions, and the sales cycle will be longer than in mixed-farming country. But they are also not going anywhere, which is exactly what a seven-year term with a permanence obligation needs.

**Condition headroom is the dual-instrument story.** As at the last assessment, 39 per cent of the region was on practices likely to maintain good to very good land condition. **That leaves roughly 61 per cent with real improvement headroom** - and the same practice change that builds soil carbon is the one that holds ground cover and cuts sediment to the reef. One intervention, two instruments, on the same hectares. No other zone in the set has that as cleanly.

**Ground cover swings hard with rainfall.** After consecutive wet years around 5 per cent of the region shows reduced ground cover; after several dry years that rises above 40 per cent. That volatility is a delivery risk worth modelling rather than discovering, and it is exactly what continuous monitoring is for.

**Tenure needs checking early.** Most enterprises sit on leasehold country rather than freehold. Carbon projects on leasehold land carry an additional consent path that freehold does not, and it is better established at qualification than at contract. Worth confirming with the technical team before this zone is presented as equivalent to the southern three.

---

## The honest counterweight

These are dry tropics rangelands: phosphorus-poor soils, 4 to 20 hectares per animal, native pasture rather than sown. **Carbon uplift per hectare will be lower here than on improved pasture in the south.**

So the Burdekin trade is cheap hectares and slow decisions, against lower yield per hectare. Southern New South Wales is the reverse. **A module built from both is better than a module built from either**, which is a good argument for the four-zone structure rather than a reason to prefer one.

Anyone quoting Burdekin hectares alongside southern hectares without noting the yield difference will get caught. Say it first.
