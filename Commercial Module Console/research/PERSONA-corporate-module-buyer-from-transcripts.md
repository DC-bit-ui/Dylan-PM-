## Part 1 · What your correction changes

**Your correction, restated so it is on the record:** ACCU returns fall across **five crediting years inside a seven-year term**, from **projects recruited into the module**, not from the existing book. Delivery therefore **ratchets up** as recruited projects are baselined, calibrated and validated.

The development plan confirms every part of it. Slide 6: Year 1 recruitment, registration and baseline; Year 2 HORIZON calibration and Schedule 2 cohort configuration; **Year 3 onwards annual issuance**. Seven-year term from 1 April 2026, so crediting runs April 2028 to March 2033. Five years.

### 1.1 · I was wrong that the acceleration had no basis. I was right that it is invisible

`DIAGNOSIS §2.9` said the accelerating forecast curve had no stated basis. That was wrong and I withdraw it. The basis is the recruitment and calibration sequence, it is specified in the plan, and it is exactly what you have just described.

**What survives, in a different form: the console draws the ratchet and never shows the mechanism that produces it.** A carbon desk does not need to be told a curve accelerates. They need to know *why*, because the why is the risk. The why here is a recruitment pipeline, a baselining schedule and a calibration gate, all of which have dates.

### 1.2 · The ratchet is in the wrong place, and this is the larger finding

Two ramps exist in this module and they are not the same thing.

| | Period | What ramps | Status today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The assembly ramp** | 2026 to 2028 | Hectares recruited, registered, baselined, declared, calibrated | **Live right now** |
| **The delivery ramp** | 2028 to 2033 | ACCUs issued per year | Has not started |

Matthew's full-ramp figure is **year 2.5**, and crediting starts at year 3. So by the time the first credit is issued the module is essentially at nameplate. **The delivery years are close to flat, not accelerating.** The steep curve belongs to the assembly, which is happening this quarter.

The console built the delivery ramp, drew it accelerating through the final quarters, and omitted the assembly ramp entirely.

**Matthew asked for the assembly ramp explicitly.** 5 August:

> *"even from the beginning build, like how many hectares, like farmland hectares, soil carbon hectares, hectares by rainfall, hectares, however you want to configure all of that, because we have the digital twins and we know all of that information, we can report against that"*

And again on 7 August:

> *"you're seeing the build in terms of like hectares, the characterization of the hectares, you can see everything about your portfolio... as the baseline data fills in, like it says, you're documenting all of that data"*

**The development plan already quantifies it.** Slide 15 carries a month-by-month recruitment curve to 100,000 ha across twelve months, in three scenarios: 1,500 · 3,000 · 5,000 · 7,500 · 11,000 · 16,000 · 22,000 · 30,000 · 40,000 · 52,000 · 71,000 · 100,000. Registration applications lodged by month 12, all baselined by month 14, all declared by month 15.

That curve is the single most valuable thing available to this console, for three reasons. It is **real today**, unlike everything on the current Module tab. It is **the only content that changes week to week**, which is what makes a surface worth opening on a Tuesday. And **it is the honest form of the ratchet**, because every hectare on it has a verifiable state.

### 1.3 · The 2030 question, answered by the source

You did not answer my §7 question directly, but Hobbs did, relaying Matthew's brief on 5 August. This is the most important sentence in any of the four transcripts:

> **"he wants the demo to demonstrate what it currently does from the perspective of what it eventually can do."**

That is a precise instruction and it has two halves. The console built the second half and dropped the first.

**So selling the future state is not the error. It is the brief, and I should have weighted it higher.** Matthew is unambiguous and repeated: the twin will write its own offsets report, its own land management strategy, biodiversity and reef and water applications, Scope 3 and supply chain reporting. `memory/state/rules.md` already says forward capability is unconstrained. Nothing in my diagnosis should be read as arguing for restraint on that.

**The error is narrower and it is a tense error.** A surface dated 2030, carrying `[Measured]` badges on 761,856 ACCUs, is not selling the future state. It is asserting a present tense that does not exist. Those are different acts and only one of them is defensible.

And the fabrication actively weakens the sell. **Matthew's entire vocabulary is journey vocabulary:**

> *"next upgrade, the rendering, next upgrade, we get the baseline data, next upgrade"*
> *"unlock that next layer, unlock that next layer, unlock that next layer"*
> *"this becomes next step, next step, next step"*
> *"remember the power of 1, we don't just solve everything all at once, just make that one step"*

**A console showing a finished 2030 state has deleted the journey by showing only its destination.** The upgrade ladder is the commercial model - 60% base accuracy, 80% with a baseline, then analytes, denser sampling, 3D rendering, 30 metre to 5 metre pixels, usage credits. None of that is legible if the reader is dropped at the end.

**Revised finding, replacing §2.1:** the console should be set in the future and say so. Same measured-versus-modelled discipline, applied to time instead of method. Everything else in §2.1 stands, including that `[Measured]` on a projected figure is the honesty architecture certifying a fabrication.

### 1.4 · §2.2 is not my inference. It is Matthew's specification

I argued predicted-versus-cored-versus-issued was the missing exhibit, on the strength of four of your own documents. It turns out Matthew specified it himself on 5 August, in the passage where he describes the module holder's quarterly view:

> *"each quarter, when we come to report with Horizon, they'll actually be a predicted... These are the projects that we're calibrating at a cohort level, **this is what was predicted, and this is actually what was measured, and this is what credits have been issued**, so that you could start to see all of that"*

He also names the frame it sits in, twice: **work in progress.**

> *"what's being measured and validated and then what that work in progress... once we've run it and we know this is how many credits we're going to get then they're like in production credits"*
> *"you can see like the predicted nameplate and how [you're] tracking versus nameplate, so you can see all of that work in progress"*

**Issued · in production · predicted** is inventory language. It is what a commodity desk uses for physical stock. It is a three-state pipeline, it maps exactly onto the certainty ladder, and it is not on the console.

### 1.5 · Everything else in the diagnosis stands

The headline object error, the missing ladder, four disjoint datasets, the AI layer built as the two ruled-out patterns, the spot panel at option C, the Monetise hero undercutting the estate argument. None of those is touched by the term correction.

## Part 2 · The persona and the future state, in his own words

### 2.1 · The problem he is selling against

This is the sharpest thing in the corpus and it is the opening of the 7 August call:

> *"pretty much everything is just boring long docs and then maybe a bit of static understanding on the project, the project registration type, and that you'll get credits at a certain point in time. So it's a delivery schedule of credits. That's what you get. Big, long documents, a whole bunch of definitions and terms, and a defined schedule of delivery of credits. And maybe a quarterly get-together where you're kind of going, okay, things are going good or whatever."*

Three artefacts: a long document, a delivery schedule, a quarterly call. That is the entire incumbent product and he is contemptuous of it.

### 2.2 · The replacement, named

> **"We want to turn that around into, you know, this is really a smart contract because this taps into each and every digital twin, so you can see the performance of that module at any point in time."**

**A smart contract, not a dashboard.** The prior review already identified this framing as the best sentence in V4 and it originates here. It is the right frame because the buyer signed a contract, not a subscription, and it makes the surface a live rendering of a document they already have rather than a new thing to learn.

Hobbs's version, from the 5 August debrief:

> *"it's the central nervous system of your project, of your farm, of your investment. You know, **what if your investment came with a nervous system?**"*

### 2.3 · Two words he uses that are load-bearing

**Daisy chain.** His term for what the module holder sees:

> *"the farmer gets the digital twin, commercial module gets the **daisy chain view** of all those aspects **without breaching the primacy**"*

Two things in one line. The module view is a chain of individual twins, not an aggregate over them. And there is a **privacy constraint on the module view that is not documented anywhere in the brief corpus** - the holder sees the chain without seeing what belongs to the landholder. That needs resolving before "enter as the landholder" ships, because as specified it opens the landholder's own console to the module holder.

**Radical transparency.** 7 August:

> *"we are now entering in that contract piece of like almost like **radical transparency** where you get those answers and it's not just a static report"*
> *"if there are issues we've got line of sight as to where they are and **we're not hiding them, we're resolving them quicker**"*
> *"Oh well, we've been so busy, we've completely ignored that project. We've had no engagement with it. Now it's only got two days before it's out of its compliance period. Like you're seeing all of that is being done. It's being checked up on."*

**He is arguing that visible problems are the product.** Not a risk to manage, the thing being sold. That is the same instinct as the empty-state discipline and the limits-on-us flags, and it goes considerably further than anything currently built. A module holder seeing a project two days from a compliance deadline, and seeing it resolved, learns more about AgriProve than any statistic.

### 2.4 · What he wants on the module surface, consolidated

From both calls, deduplicated. Everything here is his, not mine.

**The build**
- Hectares enrolled, and their characterisation
- Baseline data status as it fills in
- Hectares by farmland type, by soil carbon, by rainfall zone, by land use, "however you want to configure all of that"
- Catchment area and state-based cuts

**Delivery, quarter by quarter**
- Credits issued, credits in production, credits predicted
- Predicted nameplate against actual tracking
- Per-cohort: predicted, then measured, then issued
- Next cohort and its size
- Average yield per hectare each quarter, used to update projections
- Uncertainty bounds, and *"the further you go out, the more uncertain you are"*

**Risk**
- Drought affected properties, rainfall above or below average
- *"whatever risk lens you want to put through"*

**Operations, which he lists alongside the investment metrics**
- When the offsets report is due, what has been applied for, what has been issued, expected wait time on credits

**Reporting outputs**
- Area, predicted sequestration, ESG reports
- *"their investment into this commercial module has unlocked this much property of this kind of description for this level of predicted soil carbon sequestration"*

Note that last one. **The reporting output he describes is about land unlocked, not credits delivered.** That is a materially different claim and it is closer to the estate argument than to a delivery report.

**Then the lens stack**
- Erosion and sediment reduction, then *"the stacks of coming soon"* - biodiversity, nitrogen fixation - *"as a cascade of co-benefits"*

### 2.5 · The uncertainty point, which cuts against something already built

> *"predictions in terms of like, you know, high confidence or like a plus or minus. **And the further you go out, the more uncertain you are.**"*

He wants the forecast band **widening with horizon**. The strategy work wants the confidence band **narrowing as hectares move up the ladder**. Both are correct and they are different axes: uncertainty grows with forecast distance, and shrinks with measurement maturity. A surface that shows only one of them is telling half the truth, and a surface that shows a single band without saying which axis it moves on will be misread.

### 2.6 · The demo structure he actually specified

5 August, to Kieren and Hobbs. **Four views and a horizon slide, in this order:**

1. **Architecture** - *"a bit on the architecture, why it works and how it works"*
2. **The farmer view** - *"this is their portal to their smart digital twin"*
3. **The channel partner view** - *"this is what our channel partner sees in terms of all their projects and the performance stacks"*
4. **The commercial module investor view** - *"full dashboarding and what I can show you in that time performance, all that functionality"*
5. **One slide on where we are going** - *"and where we're going, we don't need roads"*

**The channel partner view has never been briefed or built.** It is between the farmer twin and the module, it is a real persona (Lorico is named as an example), and it is missing from the five-section architecture entirely.

More importantly: **the argument is the zoom.** Farmer twin, then partner, then module, each a wider aperture on the same object. That is how *one machine* gets demonstrated rather than asserted, and it is the structural answer to the four-disjoint-datasets problem in `DIAGNOSIS §2.5`. The current console has the module view and one drill-down to a twin. Matthew asked for the full telescope.

### 2.7 · The future state, in sequence

The meta-narrative, 5 August:

> *"HORIZON functionally was a soil carbon model... **Schedule 2 means nothing to a farmer.** Where I landed on, which started to make sense, was actually what we've done is we've turned each of our soil carbon projects into a smart digital twin... And the first use case in terms of what report do we write is soil carbon credits."*

The twin as an operating system, with methods as installable modules:

> *"you'd go to your digital twin and you go, I'd like to do a soil carbon project. And it would basically go, **cool, I've downloaded this module**, here's the baseline plan, here's everything. Here's your land management strategy."*

**Three twins he says should already exist:**

> *"there's three that we should have already built. Digital twin soil carbon. Digital twin water catchment quality and water quality. Digital twin deforestation, credentialing. **And pointing to a whole bunch more coming through.**"*

The full ambition, 5 August to Kieren and Hobbs:

> *"The Smart Digital Twin should write its own offsets report, should write its own land management strategy, should be in daily contact if the landholders want it... It writes everything for biodiversity credits, for the reef credits, for water catchment credits, for an application to the local landcare... it's supply chain reporting, it's Scope 3 reporting, it's your carbon intensity certification, like everything. And it's just like, it should be as simple as **ask the twin or ask your farm what is it you want**."*

His worked example of the interaction:

> *"Oh, we're getting grief on compliance with deforestation beef. Oh sure, would you like me to write up your compliance statement? I can see here's where we are, **here's the missing bits**, here's the report."*

Note "here's the missing bits" is part of his own future-state fantasy. The gap disclosure is not a concession he tolerates, it is in the version he is selling.

### 2.8 · The BHP argument, verbatim

This is the one to build the corporate surface around and it is stated once, precisely:

> *"when we get in front of a BHP, the subliminal message to the BHP should be **all of their land should be managed by smart digital twins** to optimise all of that, and then what they get is essentially **a portfolio of smart digital twins that are knitted together**, and it can write their ESG reports, it can get the carbon credits to that new degree of validation. Their front seat for a whole raft of those green credentials **that they're enabling by owning a commercial module**."*

And the same argument for miners and banks, 5 August:

> *"We're pitching to a mining company... You should be building a digital twin of all of your mines... it's the same core activity. It's just, what's the different reporting lens that we load up and make accessible."*
> *"Or even for the banks, they don't use ours. **We'll go and build digital twins for you just using publicly available data so you're not breaching anything.** We give you the HORIZON estimate of what it is, not the actual."*

That last line is a genuine product decision hiding in a transcript: a **modelled-only twin on land AgriProve has never sampled**, offered deliberately, with the ground truth withheld. It is exactly the Acquisition section, and it means the "everything is modelled here" constraint is a feature of a named product rather than a caveat on a weak one.

### 2.9 · Two more things he wants that are not in any brief

**The ideas leaderboard.** The chat interface's second job:

> *"even if we say, look, that information's not immediately available, let us get back to you - we track it and put it into an **ideas leaderboard** of things we could potentially easily solve to give that insight... so we're not designing features that we think will be of use. **People are dragging it from us.**"*

Logging unanswered questions is already in the brief. Ranking them and showing the leaderboard is not, and the leaderboard is the part that makes it visible to the buyer that they are shaping the product.

**Explicit beta framing.** He volunteers it:

> *"we embrace the power of beta testing... this isn't beta testing, we haven't done our QAQC on it. **You've got to revise and check every output.**"*

Worth knowing. The person whose reputation is most exposed is comfortable with outputs labelled unverified. That is more headroom than the current build assumes.

### 2.10 · The named buyers, and one contradiction to resolve

Across the transcripts: **BHP, Trafigura, Mercuria, Nippon Steel, China Light and Gas, TotalEnergies** (existing holder, calendar year, deliveries tied to November), **Corporate Carbon** (holds a smaller module bought in March, now pitching to eight groups). Matthew: *"I'm pretty sure it's going to be all carbon desk people in those."*

**The contradiction.** On 5 August you argued, and I think you were right:

> *"rather than a single product, these are differentiated products, all built on the same underlying infrastructure and architecture, but surfaced for these different personas, **not through a single entry point, but rather through separate products**."*

The kickoff records the opposite as settled: one surface, no second door. That reversal is not documented anywhere and it is the decision that produced a five-section console trying to serve a carbon desk, a closure manager, corp dev and group sustainability at once. Worth reopening deliberately or recording why it was closed.

## Part 3 · What the numbers actually support

The development plan resolves more than I expected. Both contradictory yield figures are confirmed as printed:

- **Slide 3:** *"Over-performing or under-performing projects may be added or removed to ensure that the entire portfolio will average at **2.1 ACCUs/ha/annum**"*
- **Slide 24:** *"...to ensure that the entire portfolio averages **at or above 4 ACCUs per hectare per annum**"*

Same mechanism, same sentence structure, two targets. One is the base case and one is the stretch, or one is stale. AP-2707 needs this settled before anything ships.

### The entitlement arithmetic, which nobody has written down

The financier receives **25% of ACCUs issued**, with **220,000 as fixed delivery** over the term. So on 125,000 ha over 5 crediting years:

| Portfolio average | Total module issuance | Financier at 25% | Cover on the 220,000 floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 ACCUs/ha/yr (slide 3) | 1,312,500 | 328,125 | **1.49×** |
| 3.0 ACCUs/ha/yr (console) | 1,875,000 | 468,750 | **2.13×** |
| 4.0 ACCUs/ha/yr (slide 24) | 2,500,000 | 625,000 | **2.84×** |

Two things follow.

**The console's 1,875,000 is a real number.** It is total module issuance at the 3.0 planning rate. It is simply not anyone's entitlement, and "Contracted to you" is the wrong label on a correct figure. That is a smaller defect than I described in `DIAGNOSIS §2.3` and an easier one to fix.

**The floor is covered 1.5 to 2.8 times over and nobody is saying so.** Cover ratio is the native language of anyone who has financed anything, it is a physical-quantity statement so it is clear of the AFS constraint, and it improves visibly as hectares move up the certainty ladder. It is the strongest number in the development plan and it appears on no surface.

### The 2030 date may be borrowed from a different object

Kieren and Will, 5 August, on the TotalEnergies cap exposure work:

> Kieren: *"that cap's not static, and by 2030 that could materially have shifted... we just want to add in a column that's like a point in time... where does that sit in terms of the cap on that cumulative basis?"*
> Will: *"I'll go 1 November 26, 1 November 28, **1 November 2030** and just go, here is expected excess at this point in time, assuming these project levels are maintained."*

November because TotalEnergies deliveries are tied to November. **That is a genuine 2030 analysis, but it is about the existing Amber book's cumulative cap position, not the new module's delivery.** If the console's 2030 lens came from there, then a third distinct commercial object has been folded into the same surface, which would explain a good deal.

Worth confirming, because a cap-exposure snapshot and a delivery projection are different exhibits and only one of them belongs on a prospect's screen.
