# Catchment data for the region cards, commercial cut

Replaces the earlier version. Ecological content is out. What is here is the recruitment denominator, the enterprise mix, and the field economics.

---

## The reframe

Hectares are not the constraint. **Landholders are.** A field team books farm visits with people, so the question a region card has to answer is not only whether there is enough land, but how many conversations it takes to reach it.

That makes three things material: how many agricultural businesses sit in the catchment, what kind of enterprise they run, and how big their holdings are. The third one turns out to be the sharpest signal available and nobody has been using it.

---

## The finding worth leading with

**Share of rural-zoned parcels over 100 hectares:**

| Sub-region | Over 100 ha | Conversations for the same hectares |
|---|---|---|
| Western Riverina | **44%** | baseline |
| Central West Slopes and Plains | 35% | 1.3x |
| Eastern Riverina | 19% | 2.3x |
| Central Tablelands | **12%** | **3.7x** |

Signing 10,000 hectares in the Central Tablelands takes on the order of **three to four times as many landholder conversations** as signing the same area in the Western Riverina. Same target, same field team, radically different cost per hectare.

This is directly relevant to two open questions inside the business: why the assumed 2,400 hectare average project size sits so far from the 633 hectare average Frontier shows for identified properties, and how a field team should be sized per catchment. It also says something a buyer should hear - the campaign is weighted toward catchments where the arithmetic works.

**Note the enterprise mix difference.** The Eastern Riverina is a grazing and mixed-farming landscape - wool at 1,156 businesses is the most numerous enterprise. The Western Riverina is dominated by broadacre cropping and irrigated horticulture, with beef at only 429 businesses across a much larger area. For a grazing-only method, **the Eastern Riverina is the richer recruiting ground despite being smaller**, while the Western Riverina offers fewer, larger grazing enterprises.

## Three cautions on the numbers

**Do not sum commodity business counts.** ABS counts a business under every commodity it produces, so a mixed sheep, beef and cropping enterprise appears in three rows. Only the sub-region total, such as the 6,692 for Central West Slopes and Plains, is a true business count. Beef and wool counts overlap heavily.

**The NSW data is 2015-16.** These sub-regional snapshots draw on that agricultural census. The final ABS Agricultural Commodities release for 2021-22 notes that survey response quality was too low to produce a full set of regional statistics, so 2015-16 remains the last complete regional picture. Directionally sound, ten years old on the levels.

**The Lachlan proxy overstates.** Central West Slopes and Plains carries substantial Macquarie and Castlereagh country. Any Lachlan figure derived from it needs clipping to the catchment before it goes in front of a buyer.

## Closing the gaps properly

Three figures are still missing and all three are catchment-clipped derivations rather than lookups: grazing area for the Lachlan, agricultural business counts for the Murrumbidgee and Fitzroy, and holdings-over-100-hectares consistently across all three.

Two datasets close all of it, and both are jobs for the team already running ArcGIS at national scale:

**ABARES Catchment Scale Land Use of Australia** for land use by class, clipped to catchment boundaries. Gives grazing on native vegetation and grazing on modified pastures separately, so penetration can be computed against grazing that actually clears the method.

**Frontier's own property layer** for holdings counts and the size distribution. Frontier already holds assessed properties with boundaries at national scale, which means the business count, the average holding size and the parcels-over-100-hectares figure can be computed directly from data the business owns, per catchment, more accurately than any published summary and with no vintage problem.

That second one is worth flagging: **the most commercially interesting figure on these cards is one only AgriProve can produce.** No competitor can tell a buyer how many recruitable grazing holdings sit in a catchment and what they average, because nobody else has read the land.
