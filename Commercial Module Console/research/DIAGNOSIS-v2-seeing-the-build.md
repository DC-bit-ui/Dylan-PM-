## 2 · Why it does not convince

### 2.1 · The headline overclaims against a number the same panel volunteers

This is the finding. Everything else is smaller.

> **"The forecast clears the contract"** · On track · **+83,000 ACCUs against contract**
> *"The band on those cohorts is ±121,000, so a shortfall remains possible."*

**The band is 1.46 times the surplus.** On the console's own numbers the forecast does not clear the contract in any sense a trader would accept. It lands inside a range that comfortably contains a shortfall. The panel says so, in the third line, under a headline asserting the opposite and a status chip reading On track.

**This is the specific reason it does not build trust, and it is worse than an ordinary overstatement.** The honesty discipline generated the number that refutes the headline, and then the headline was written anyway. A reader who spots it does not conclude the forecast is optimistic. They conclude the numbers were assembled to reach a conclusion, and from that point every other disclosure on the page reads as decoration rather than candour. You have paid the full cost of being rigorous and then spent the credit it bought.

A carbon desk spots this in under a minute. It is the first thing they are trained to look for.

### 2.2 · The delivery curve requires more hectares than the module has

Reading the quarterly bars: the final four quarters carry **664,000 ACCUs**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Final year delivery, from the chart | 664,000 ACCUs |
| Contracted rate, stated on the same page | 2.9 to 3.0 ACCUs/ha/yr, described as steady state |
| **Hectares required at 3.0** | **221,000** |
| Module target | 125,000 |
| Hectares enrolled | 103,952 |

**The final year of the chart needs 77% more land than the module is contracted to hold.** Expressed the other way, it implies 5.31 ACCUs/ha/yr across the full 125,000, or 6.39 across what is enrolled. The peak quarter of 177,144 annualises to 5.67.

Nothing on the page supports that rate. The leaderboard's highest modelled yield is **3.50**, its median is **2.20**, and the leaderboard's own note says 2.9 to 3.0 is where accumulation *levels out*, so maturation cannot get there either.

There is a second version of the same problem in the present tense. The tile reads *"72,400 ha at crediting stage · 3 ACCUs/ha/yr"*, giving 217,200 a year. The chart delivers 354,000 in 2030 and 253,952 a year averaged across three years. On the same hectares that is **3.51 to 4.89 ACCUs/ha/yr**, against a stated 3.0, on one screen.

This is exactly the arithmetic the audience does with a phone during a demo. It is more damaging than a wrong number because it is a wrong number that only appears when you check, which is the category this buyer is paid to find.

### 2.3 · The chart's own legend flips the on-track verdict

The cumulative chart's legend includes **`Contracted straight line`**. The tile reads *"+19,356 against 742,500 due after 3 of 5 crediting years."*

A straight line to 1,875,000 over five crediting years puts **1,125,000** due after three. Against that, 761,856 is **363,144 behind**, not 19,356 ahead.

The 742,500 figure implies a back-loaded schedule, which is correct and consistent with the ratchet you described. **But the legend calls it a straight line, and the dotted line is drawn flat across the top of the chart at the 2,182,950 axis maximum, which is a total rather than a schedule.** So the console labels its own schedule as something it is not, and under the label it prints, the holder is a third of a million credits behind and the On track chip is wrong.

Naming the schedule honestly costs nothing and removes the trap.

### 2.4 · There are four different todays on one tab

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| Header, top right | **11 Aug** |
| Position panel | **year 5 of 7** of 2026 to 2032, so 2030 |
| Delivered tile | after **3 of 5 crediting years**, so start of 2031 |
| Cumulative chart | `today` line sits at **2031** |
| Spot panel | **2030 · today** |

And the lead insight is internally inconsistent on the same point: *Est. delivery 2030* in the figure, *2031 cohort schedule* in its own provenance chain, *Intended action Spring 2031* on the matching Monaro card below.

Separately, the flags panel refers to *"the 2029 event"* and *"hectares scheduled to credit in 2027"* in the same list. Today is simultaneously before and after 2029.

This is the tense problem from v1 made literal. **Nothing is wrong with setting the surface in the future, and Matthew's brief requires it. What is wrong is that the surface has not decided when it is.** A single stated as-at date, honoured everywhere, resolves the whole class.

### 2.5 · The leaderboard's first row is a property the holder does not own

**Mulloon Farm, 5,990 ha, Assessed, ranked first, marked Below bar.** Mulloon is a prospect with no cores and no project. It is not in the module.

I understand it is pinned for the impersonation route. The effect on a prospect reading the position screen is that the top of their portfolio is land they do not hold, which cannot be harvested, marked as failing the selection threshold. It undermines the leaderboard's claim to be a management instrument, and it is the sort of thing that is asked about in the room.

There is a related object question underneath it. The board is ranked by total modelled volume with Mulloon pinned, so the ordering is neither yield nor readiness nor volume, and rows two and three are named for regions rather than properties. What the row represents is not consistent.

### 2.6 · The lead insight is a good piece of reasoning wearing a bad sentence

> *"We recommend harvesting it now: cover has fallen below local reference on four of the six, and cover moves the carbon estimate before it moves a core result."*

The reasoning is genuinely strong and it is the only place on the page where the model tells the holder something they could not have worked out. Keep it.

But read the sentence as a sceptic: cover is deteriorating, cover leads carbon, therefore claim now. **That is a recommendation to book the credit ahead of a decline the model can already see.** The flags panel confirms the same 45,767 hectares carry both a fire season outlook and cover below reference. It is commercially defensible and it is one screenshot away from being a very bad look, on the surface whose entire argument is measurement honesty.

The logic should stay. The framing needs to be about acting inside the window where model and ground still agree, which is what `REVIEW-console-versions` records V2 saying rather well, not about harvesting before the number falls.

### 2.7 · On the visual, now that I can see it

Three things, and they are not taste.

**Colour carries identity instead of information.** Everything is lime and green on dark green, with teal and blue as the only variants. In a commodity professional's environment colour has one job, which is direction and severity. Here green is the brand, so a green figure means nothing in particular, and the one channel that should encode good or bad, up or down, ahead or behind is spent on livery. The palette is also the single most ESG-coded choice available, in front of an audience your own strategy note defines as commodity people rather than sustainability managers.

**Nothing establishes a focal point.** Every panel is the same card, the same weight, on the same background, at roughly the same type size. The two brightest objects on the first screen are a full-width lime chat button and a modelled surplus figure. So the loudest instruction on the position screen is go and talk to the assistant, and the second loudest is the number with the widest band.

`REVIEW-console-UI-and-usability` identified the fix already and it survives independent of anything else here: V5's single dark tile, one deliberate contrast point holding the model's own exhibit. It costs nothing and it is the only structural device on the page that would tell a reader where to look.

**The surface narrates itself constantly.** Almost every panel carries a two or three line explanatory paragraph in muted type. Some of it is excellent and load-bearing, such as the flags footnote and the bias adjustment note. Most of it is there because the object above it is not self-evident. A confident instrument does not explain what a bar means. And at this density the secondary text sits at or below usable contrast, which will get worse projected in a meeting room or screen-shared over compressed video.

### 2.8 · Still absent, and it is still the largest content gap

**Predicted against cored against issued, per cohort.** Matthew specified it himself on 5 August, describing this exact surface.

The build gets closer than the handover suggested. The bias adjustment at **−6.5%, calibrated against cores** is a real cores-versus-model number and it is the right idea. But it is a single scalar. The exhibit is a table with a row per cohort: what HORIZON predicted at baseline, what the cores returned, what the regulator issued. On credited land you can produce it and no competitor without a track record can.

Two smaller absences in the same family. **Nothing shows the band narrowing as hectares climb the ladder**, which is the visible form of the buyer's capital working. And **the ±121,000 band is stated but never decomposed** into which cohorts carry it, which is the first question anyone asks of a stated uncertainty.

One more thing to note: the certainty panel places the withheld amount, 1,420 against 1,240, or −12.7%, immediately beside the bias adjustment of −6.5%. They are different quantities doing different jobs and they will be read as one.

## 4 · The module-holder persona, re-cut

Replacing Part 2 of the persona note. Module holder and Corporate Carbon material only. Farmer twin content removed.

**The incumbent product he is displacing** (7 Aug, opening):

> *"pretty much everything is just boring long docs and then maybe a bit of static understanding on the project, the project registration type, and that you'll get credits at a certain point in time. So it's a delivery schedule of credits. That's what you get... And maybe a quarterly get-together."*

**The replacement, named:**

> *"We want to turn that around into, you know, this is really a smart contract because this taps into each and every digital twin, so you can see the performance of that module at any point in time."*

**What he wants on this surface**, all his:

- The build. *"you're seeing the build in terms of like hectares, the characterization of the hectares... as the baseline data fills in"*, and *"even from the beginning build, like how many hectares, farmland hectares, soil carbon hectares, hectares by rainfall, however you want to configure all of that"*
- **Issued, in production, predicted**, quarter by quarter. Inventory language, three states
- Per cohort each quarter: *"this is what was predicted, and this is actually what was measured, and this is what credits have been issued"*
- *"the predicted nameplate and how [you're] tracking versus nameplate, so you can see all of that work in progress"*
- Uncertainty that **widens with horizon**: *"the further you go out, the more uncertain you are."* Distinct from the band narrowing with measurement maturity. Both are true and the surface currently shows neither explicitly
- Risk lenses: drought exposure, rainfall above or below average, *"whatever risk lens you want to put through"*, plus catchment and state cuts
- Operational items he lists alongside the investment ones: when the offsets report is due, what has been applied for, what has been issued, expected wait time on credits
- Reporting framed as land unlocked: *"their investment into this commercial module has unlocked this much property of this kind of description for this level of predicted soil carbon sequestration"*
- The co-benefit lens stack, erosion and sediment now, biodiversity and nitrogen as *"stacks of coming soon"*

**Two of his words that are load-bearing.**

**Daisy chain**, and a privacy constraint that is not documented anywhere: *"commercial module gets the daisy chain view of all those aspects **without breaching the primacy**."* As currently specified, entering the landholder's own console from the leaderboard contradicts this.

**Radical transparency**, and it goes further than anything built:

> *"if there are issues we've got line of sight as to where they are and we're not hiding them, we're resolving them quicker"*
> *"Oh well, we've been so busy, we've completely ignored that project... Now it's only got two days before it's out of its compliance period. Like you're seeing all of that is being done."*

The manual layer dependency flag is the only place the build does this, and it is the best thing on the page. He wants it as a category, not an exception.

**The BHP argument, which is the one to build the surface around:**

> *"the subliminal message to the BHP should be all of their land should be managed by smart digital twins... what they get is essentially a portfolio of smart digital twins that are knitted together, and it can write their ESG reports, it can get the carbon credits to that new degree of validation. Their front seat for a whole raft of those green credentials that they're enabling by owning a commercial module."*

**And the brief, relayed by Hobbs on 5 August**, which remains the sentence that governs everything:

> *"he wants the demo to demonstrate what it currently does from the perspective of what it eventually can do."*

The build has the second half. The first half is the assembly ramp that is running right now, and the predicted-against-cored-against-issued record on land already credited. Neither is on the surface.
