# Operational zone metrics

Four metrics, defined once, populated for every zone. Where a figure could not be sourced directly it is estimated from a stated basis rather than left blank, and marked so.

---

## The four metrics and what each is for

| Metric | Definition | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Total addressable market** | Grazing land inside the catchment boundary | The ceiling. How much land could ever carry this method here |
| **Recruitable hectares** | Grazing land that can actually carry a project, after scale, condition and tenure | The real ceiling, and the denominator for penetration |
| **Approachable operations** | Grazing enterprises holding recruitable land | The recruitment workload. Field teams talk to businesses, not hectares |
| **Average enterprise size** | Recruitable hectares per operation | **The cost per hectare of recruitment.** The single most decision-relevant number here |

## The metric that carries the argument

**Average enterprise size varies nineteen-fold across the four zones**, and it is what determines how many conversations a target costs.

| Zone | Hectares per conversation | Relative to the southern baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Burdekin | 16,513 ha | **19.2x** |
| Fitzroy | 8,780 ha | **10.2x** |
| Lachlan | 1,393 ha | 1.6x |
| Murrumbidgee | 862 ha | 1.0x |

A field team of two carrying around 150 live landholder relationships reaches:

| Zone | Reach per campaign cycle | Modules |
|---|---|---|
| Burdekin | 2,476,950 ha | **19.8** |
| Fitzroy | 1,317,000 ha | 10.5 |
| Lachlan | 208,950 ha | 1.7 |
| Murrumbidgee | 129,300 ha | 1.0 |

**Same two people, twenty times the land.** That is the case for the northern zones stated in the only currency that matters operationally, and it is the answer to how the machine scales without scaling headcount proportionally.

**The counterweight, and it should be said first.** The northern zones are dry tropics rangelands with lower carbon uplift per hectare than improved southern pasture. Cheap hectares and slow decisions in the north, higher yield per hectare and more conversations in the south. **The four-zone mix is the argument, not any single zone.**

## This resolves the 633 hectare question

Frontier shows an average of 633 hectares for identified properties in the southern zones. The enterprise figures here put southern grazing enterprises at 862 to 1,393 hectares.

Those numbers are not in conflict. **An enterprise is not a property.** One landholder typically runs 1.4 to 2.2 titles, so a single conversation covers more land than a single property record suggests.

The practical consequence: **recruitment planning should count enterprises, delivery planning should count properties**, and the two should never be used interchangeably. Anything sized on the 633 hectare figure has been overstating the conversation count by roughly half.

## How each estimate was made

Stating the working so it can be argued with rather than guessed at.

**Grazing share, Lachlan - estimated at 62 per cent.** The adjacent Murrumbidgee measures 52 per cent grazing with about 25 per cent forest and woodland. The Lachlan carries materially less forest - its eastern tablelands are largely cleared and its western third is semi-arid shrubland and mallee, which is grazing country on native vegetation. Cropping runs through the central slopes at roughly the same intensity as the Murrumbidgee. Net, grazing share should sit above the Murrumbidgee and below the Queensland zones. **Confidence: moderate. This is the estimate most worth replacing with a real land use clip.**

**Eligibility factor - 70 per cent of grazing land, 75 per cent in the Fitzroy.** Three components of roughly ten per cent each: land below minimum viable project scale or too fragmented to assemble; land where uplift is marginal because it is too arid, too degraded, or already carbon-rich; and land carrying a tenure, existing project or legal encumbrance. The Fitzroy sits higher because holdings are large and fragmentation is low. The Burdekin does not, despite similar holding size, because most enterprises sit on leasehold country and that carries a consent path freehold does not. **Confidence: moderate. Directionally sound, worth calibrating against actual qualification rates once there are enough of them.**

**Approachable operations, Fitzroy - estimated at 950.** Derived from carrying capacity. The Fitzroy runs 2.64 million cattle on 11.1 million hectares of grazing, or 4.2 hectares per head. The Burdekin runs 1.35 million on 11.8 million, or 8.7 hectares per head, across about 500 businesses averaging 28,000 to 30,000 hectares. A Fitzroy property carrying a comparable herd needs roughly half the land, putting average holdings near 11,700 hectares and the business count near 950. That implies about 2,850 head per business, which sits inside the observed Queensland commercial range. Agricultural employment in the region of about 3,600 is consistent with 900 to 1,200 businesses. **Confidence: moderate to high, two independent methods agreeing.**

**Approachable operations, Murrumbidgee - estimated at 2,000.** Commodity business counts cannot be summed, because a mixed enterprise appears under beef, wool and cropping alike. Taking the larger of the beef or wool count per sub-region and allowing for partial non-overlap gives roughly 1,400 in the east and 600 in the west. **Confidence: moderate.**

**Approachable operations, Lachlan - estimated at 2,400.** The published sub-regions covering the Lachlan are more than twice the catchment's area, so their 3,526 beef enterprises scale to roughly 1,550 inside the catchment. Adding wool-only enterprises and allowing for overlap gives about 2,400. **Confidence: low to moderate. The sub-regional geography is a poor fit and this figure carries the most uncertainty of any here.**

## Closing these properly

Every estimate above is a catchment-clipped derivation, and the business already holds the data to replace all of them.

**Frontier's own property layer** gives approachable operations, average holding size and the size distribution directly, per zone, with no vintage problem and no geographic mismatch. **ABARES Catchment Scale Land Use of Australia** clipped to these boundaries gives grazing area by class, which fixes the Lachlan estimate and puts all four zones on one methodology.

Worth flagging what that produces: **the most commercially valuable figure on these cards is one only this business can compute.** Nobody else can tell a buyer how many recruitable grazing operations sit in a catchment and what they average, because nobody else has read the land. Published statistics get you to an informed estimate. Frontier gets you to a number nobody can argue with.
