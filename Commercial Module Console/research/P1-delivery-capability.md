# P1: delivery capability. What that means, and what it displaces

**12 August 2026.** Written against Kieren's scoping call:

> *"Purchasers are buying modules of 125k ha to deliver ACCUs. Year 1 their primary interest is are we recruiting to target, year 3+ they are interested in are we delivering ACCUs. This is what they are purchasing. Everything else layers in underneath that. We need to show our delivery capability primarily."*

He is right, and the arithmetic makes the case harder than the assertion does.

---

## 1 · The module is at month 4.4 and the console is at year 5

Agreement executed 1 April 2026. Today is **month 4.4 of the twelve-month recruitment year**. On the development plan's own recruitment curve, expected position today is around **8,900 ha of 100,000**, or **11,100 of 125,000**.

| | Real, today | On the console |
|---|---|---|
| Hectares recruited | ≈ 11,000 | 103,952 |
| ACCUs delivered | **zero** | 761,856 |
| Crediting years elapsed | **zero of five** | three of five |

**So the console answers neither of Kieren's two questions.** It cannot answer the year-1 question, because recruitment is not on the surface at all. And it answers the year-3 question with numbers from a year that has not happened.

**Between today and the first ACCU there are eight dated milestones and roughly twenty months.**

| Month | Date | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Oct 2026 | Recruitment checkpoint, per the risk register |
| 12 | Apr 2027 | All hectares recruited, registration applications lodged |
| 14 | Jun 2027 | All projects baselined |
| 15 | Jul 2027 | All declared with the CER, 90-day assessment complete |
| 18 | Oct 2027 | HORIZON retraining complete |
| 20 | Dec 2027 | Cohort configuration finalised |
| 22 | Feb 2028 | Schedule 2 readiness confirmed, R² ≥ 0.85 |
| 24 | Apr 2028 | First crediting year opens |

**That twenty months is the entire lived experience of a module holder who signs this year, and none of it exists on the surface.** A prospect being shown the console in August 2026 is being shown a state they will not see until 2030, skipping the two years they are actually buying into.

This is the same finding I have been circling for three rounds, but Kieren's framing states it better than mine did. It is not that the console is dishonest about time. It is that it has skipped the part the customer is buying.

---

## 2 · What P1 has to answer in year 1

*Are we recruiting to target.* This is a funnel question and the funnel is already specified, on slide 11 of the development plan, with benchmark conversion rates.

Scaled to 125,000 ha at the plan's own rates and 2,400 ha average project size:

| Stage | Volume to fill the module | Benchmark rate |
|---|---|---|
| Properties screened via HORIZON | **1,240** | pre-screening, 500 ha+ |
| Responsive contacts | **372** | ~30% response |
| Farm visits | **149** | ~40% visit rate |
| KCTs executed | **52** | ~35% conversion |
| Hectares contracted | **125,000** | ~2,400 ha average |

**Five numbers, actuals against benchmark, tracked weekly.** That is a complete answer to "are we recruiting to target", it is legible to anyone who has run a pipeline, and every figure in it is real today and moves every week.

Three things it gives that nothing on the current surface gives.

**It is the only content that changes between visits.** Kieren's question is the Tuesday question. A funnel with movement answers it; a 2030 delivery chart does not.

**It exposes velocity, which is the real year-1 risk.** The plan's own risk register rates "insufficient eligible land area recruited within Year 1" at Medium with a Month 6 review. That checkpoint is eight weeks away. A holder who can see conversion rates against benchmark can see the risk coming, which is a stronger position than being told about it at a quarterly.

**It converts HORIZON from a claim into a visible input.** Pre-screening is what makes the 1,240 a targeted list rather than a phone book. That is the differentiator doing work in the year-1 story, without any measurement claim attached.

Then the hectares that convert climb the ladder that is already built: Registered, Baselined, Crediting. **The ladder is the right object, it exists on the surface, and it is currently sized to a state four years out instead of to the month it is in.**

## 3 · What P1 has to answer from year 3, and how it is answered today

*Are we delivering ACCUs.* From April 2028 that is issuance against schedule, and the current build already has the shape of it.

**But a holder considering a purchase today asks the same question now, and this module has no answer.** Zero delivered, zero scheduled, first credit twenty months away.

So today, delivery capability can only be evidenced by delivery that has already happened somewhere else. That is the existing book:

- **41 ACCU issuances across 8,067 ha**
- **81 measured and validated increases across 15,259 ha**
- **30,000+ physical soil cores**
- Model agreement with cores at **±15% on the eight credited projects checked** (Gayathri, Teams, 11 Aug, needs confirming)
- **Bias adjustment of −6.5%, calibrated against cores**, already on the surface

**This is the point where Kieren's P1 and the predicted-versus-cored-versus-issued exhibit become the same requirement.** I have been arguing for that exhibit on credibility grounds for three rounds. Under Kieren's framing it is not a credibility flourish, it is the only available evidence for the P1 question on a module with no history. There is nothing else to show.

One number worth chasing, because it may be the strongest single line available. The BHP dossier reports that **scheme-wide, only 52 soil carbon projects have ever been credited, with 438,966 ACCUs issued** `[moderate]`. If AgriProve's 41 issuances represent a large share of that, then delivery capability is a one-line, publicly verifiable statement. Ops should size it before anyone builds around it. If the share is small, drop it and lead with the cores.

## 4 · Kieren and Matthew are not in conflict

Worth stating, because it looks like they might be.

Matthew, via Hobbs: *"demonstrate what it currently does from the perspective of what it eventually can do."*
Kieren: *"land the basics first... everything else layers in underneath that."*

**Both point the same way.** Matthew's instruction has two halves and the build shipped the second. Kieren is saying the first half is P1 and naming exactly what it consists of. The future state is not being cut, it is being put in its correct position, which is the frame around the delivery story rather than a substitute for it.

The consequence is that **the console has two modes and it built the wrong one for the next twenty months.** Year 1 and 2 lead with recruitment and readiness. Year 3 onward leads with issuance against schedule. Whether that is one surface that changes what it leads with as the module ages, or two, is a real product decision and it should be made deliberately rather than by default.

## 5 · Where this leaves the BHP dossier

The dossier is strong and most of it is now P2 or later. Being explicit, because it was built before this ruling.

**Goes straight into P1.** Section 3.4, the module-against-obligation sizing. One module year is 44% of what BHP-operated facilities surrendered in FY2024-25, 69% on an equity basis, 19% of the modelled FY2029-30 gap, and five module years cover 25% of the cumulative FY2026-30 gross gap. That is module output expressed in the buyer's own units, physical quantity against physical obligation, no price anywhere. It answers "what am I buying" better than any panel currently on the surface.

Note the precision though: that table is a **why-buy** argument, not an are-we-delivering one. BHP is a prospect, not a holder. It belongs on the surface and it belongs in a different panel to the delivery answer.

**Also P1, as a delivery-durability argument.** The 3.0 ACCUs/ha/yr planning rate sits at **27% of the proposed 11 ACCUs/ha/yr cap**. A module priced and scheduled at 3.0 does not have its delivery revised when the reform lands. Holders of aggressive-rate supply do. That is a delivery claim, not an integrity claim, and it is the strongest version of the cap story.

**Stays in the dossier as pre-meeting intelligence, not console content.** Sections 9 and 0.2. Do not pitch the FY2030 target, do not assume BHP has a soil carbon position, do not multiply BMA by 100%, do not name Fiona Wild, do not raise EUDR or SBTi FLAG.

**Defers.** Nature, rehabilitation, water, natural capital accounts, offset monitoring. All of it is well evidenced and none of it is what the purchaser is buying.

**And one tension worth naming rather than papering over.** The dossier's through-line is *"BHP's compliance obligation gives us the meeting, and BHP's own estate is the contract"*, which makes the module a pilot for a larger estate play. Kieren's ruling is that the module is the product and everything else layers underneath. Both can be true in the end state, but they are different build orders, and Kieren's is the right one for a surface whose reader is signing a module. The dossier's own evidence supports him: BHP has published **no position on soil carbon, agricultural carbon or biodiversity credits**, so leading with a natural capital stack to a buyer who has never expressed a view on any of it, while under-answering the thing they are actually purchasing, is backwards.

## 6 · What this ruling forces a decision on

| Decision | Why it cannot be deferred |
|---|---|
| **One surface with two modes, or two surfaces** | Everything about the layout follows from it. A demo in August 2026 needs the year-1 mode; the current build is the year-5 mode |
| **What the demo is set to** | Real month 4.4 with ~11,000 ha and honest emptiness, or a simulated later state. Both are defensible. The current answer, a 2030 state with measured badges and no stated as-at date, is not |
| **Whether the recruitment funnel is shareable** | The funnel is AgriProve's operating data. Showing conversion rates to a module holder is unusual transparency. Matthew's radical-transparency position says yes, but it should be a decision |
| **The existing book's issuance record** | If predicted-versus-cored-versus-issued is the P1 evidence, the data has to be assembled and the ±15% figure confirmed. That is an Ops request with a date, not a design task |
| **Where the four live figure defects sit** | The +83,000 against a ±121,000 band, the final-year rate needing 221,000 ha, the straight-line legend, and the four different todays are all in the delivery panels. They are P1 by definition now |

The last row matters most. **Kieren has just made the delivery panels the whole product, and those are the panels carrying every arithmetic defect I found.** Whatever else changes, those four have to be right before anything goes external.
