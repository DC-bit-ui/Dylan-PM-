## Seven verbatim Hobbs framings that work

Real transcript distillation produced these specific framings, in approximate visit order. Every one landed (positive sentiment; customer ended the visit by requesting documentation). These should be **battle-card content** for any rep encountering the same objections.

### 1. The 25-year commitment — "agree not to build a trailer park home"

*"You agree to continue running this for 25 years as an agricultural enterprise, and agreed to not build a trailer park home."*

Bounds the commitment with humour. Makes the actual restriction feel real but minimal. Pair with: *"make a commitment to be better. Make a commitment to keep your land in the agricultural system for 25 years."*

### 2. The concrete dollar anchor — 6K-in / 45K-out

*"The guy had 83 hectares. It was one of the early projects. He paid 6000 to have it sampled, and he just got his check for 45,000."*

Real recent case. Concrete dollar input, concrete dollar output. Anchors the ACCU revenue discussion in real customer outcomes, not abstractions.

### 3. Annual crediting via satellite — "not leaving money on the table"

*"AgriProve just came out with this soil carbon model. They partnered with this satellite company, and they go over every property every month, and so they can predict if your soil carbon has gone up or down. So the second that it goes up, you get credited. Boom, they'll take the measurements. You're not leaving money on the table, whereas it was unclear previous to this. It can get you on basically an annual crediting cycle, as opposed to three years, four years, five years."*

Materially reframes the timeline objection. Many customers carry the 3-5-year-crediting model in their head from older information.

### 4. Ratchet — downside protection

*"Nothing works on a ratchet. So let's say one year you capture four ACCUs per hectare, and boom, you get paid for that. Now the next year, you lose six per hectare. Nothing happens until you reach that previous high. So if the next year you get eight, then you get paid for the two that you've increased on top of that original four."*

Pre-empts the "what if I have a bad year?" objection with the actual mechanism. Worth memorising the exact "nothing works on a ratchet" phrasing.

### 5. The disinformation reframe — soil carbon ≠ tree planting

*"The impression in the carbon world is that people think you have to lock up your land, destock, and then plant trees, which is obviously not what the soil carbon scheme is all about... the soil carbon element is that it doesn't require you to change the land use in that drastic way."*

Surfaces the unspoken misconception before the customer voices it. Specifically targets the rural disinformation narrative.

### 6. Additionality — "30 minutes a year"

*"Every year, you just have to have a 30-minute conversation with one of the AgriProve teams to fill out that form."*

Trivialises the ongoing reporting burden. Customers expect carbon projects to require ongoing heavy admin; "30 minutes a year" recalibrates that expectation.

### 7. Risk framing — "downside is zero credits"

*"Essentially, the only risk, as long as you don't violate any of the conditions, is that you don't get any carbon credits. But if you do increase your productivity, which you certainly will upon putting in inputs, then by definition, that means your carbon is going [up]."*

Reframes the risk as "no credits issued" rather than "money lost". This is structurally different from how most commercial commitments are presented — and is the truth.
