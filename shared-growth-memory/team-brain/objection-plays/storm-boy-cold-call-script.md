Cold Call Phone Script

AgriProve -- Riverina Field Research Program

Pure cold call -- no prior contact -- data scrape list. Primary close: farm visit with Hobbs. Secondary close:
HORIZON Snapshot.

Part 1 -- Opening Lines

Choose one opener based on your read of the prospect. All keep AgriProve out of the first sentence.
Reason for calling is in sentence two or three -- not after a warm-up.

OPTION 1 -- RESEARCHER FRAME

      BEN

     G'day [name], it's Ben here -- I'm assisting an ag researcher out of Albury who's running a field
     research program across the Riverina. He's visiting farms in the area and I thought your place might
     be worth including. Is that something you'd be open to?

      Clean and non-threatening. Works best when you want to get Hobbs to the door before soil carbon comes
      up at all.

OPTION 2 -- ASSESSMENT FRAME

      BEN

     G'day [name], Ben here -- I'm conducting an assessment of farming operations across the Riverina
     as part of an outreach program based out of Albury. Your property came up and I wanted to reach
     out directly. Have I caught you at an okay time?

      "Your property came up" creates mild curiosity without being misleading. Works well when you have some
      basic property data from the scrape.

OPTION 3 -- GRAZIER-FIRST FRAME (RECOMMENDED FOR
LARGER OPERATIONS)

      BEN

     G'day [name], it's Ben -- I'll keep it quick. I'm coordinating farm visits for an ag researcher from the
     US who's based in Albury for a few months. He's a grazier himself, written books on grazing
     management, and he's been visiting operations across the Riverina. I thought your place was worth
     a look -- is that something you'd be open to?

      Puts Hobbs front and centre from word one. Best for experienced graziers where the credentials do the
      heavy lifting.

OPTION 4 -- CURIOSITY FRAME

      BEN

     G'day Ben here. We're running a regional program out of Albury looking at what's working across
     Riverina grazing operations -- specifically the ones generating strong results. Your place came up.
     I'm not going to take up much of your time -- I just wanted to see if you'd be open to a farm visit.
     Paddock conversation or Fence-line chat Practical grazing session
     We're researching ways that help our current partners/clients grow more grass/get better results.
     (We assume that if they're growing more grass/getting better results, they're creating more soil
     carbon." )

      "Specifically the ones generating strong results" is a soft compliment that positions them as selected, not
      randomly dialled.

OPTION 5 -- STRIPPED BACK (FOR VERY TIME-POOR FARMERS)
      BEN

     G'day [name], Ben here. I'm coordinating farm visits for an ag researcher based in Albury -- he's
     spending a few months looking at operations across the Riverina. Would you be open to having him
     come out?
      Nothing wasted. Straight to yes/no in under 15 seconds.

Part 2 -- Secondary Response

Once they're still on the line and want to know more, transition into this. Keep it conversational -- not
a rehearsed pitch.

EXPAND ON THE PROGRAM

      BEN

     Our company is running an assessment of the Albury/Riverina region through an outreach field
     research program -- essentially looking for innovative producers who are doing cool things and
     generating results. I'm wondering if you'd be open-minded to host a short visit to see what's working
     for you.

WHEN THEY ASK "WHO IS YOUR COMPANY?"
      FARMER
     Who is your company?

      BEN

     I'm with a company called AgriProve. We're researching ways to help our current partners and
     clients grow more grass and get better results. We've found that if a producer is growing more grass
     and getting better results, they're generally creating more soil carbon -- that's the connection.

WHEN THEY ASK "YOU'RE NOT GOING TO TRY TO SELL ME
SOMETHING, ARE YOU?"

      FARMER

     You're not going to try to sell me something, are you?

      BEN

     No -- we're looking for ways to help the clients we're already working with have greater success.
     And I'd assume that if you wanted to be part of a soil carbon project, you probably would have
     already looked into it. This is just a genuine knowledge-sharing visit.
      Honesty about the soil carbon connection builds more trust than evasion. If they push further, be straight:
      "We do work in soil carbon -- I won't pretend it's completely unrelated -- but the visit itself isn't a pitch."

Part 3 -- Common Objections

"WE LOOKED AT CARBON A FEW YEARS AGO AND IT DIDN'T
STACK UP"

      FARMER

     We looked at carbon a few years ago and it didn't stack up.
      BEN

     Yeah, fair call -- the early programs were pretty rough. The ACCU price was twelve, thirteen dollars
     back then and the methodology was a lot clunkier. The spot price is sitting around $35 now and the
     framework's matured significantly. I'm not saying it's right for everyone, but it's worth a fresh look.

"WE'RE PROBABLY TOO SMALL"

      FARMER

     We're only about 200 ha -- probably too small.

      BEN

     The threshold used to be higher -- around 300 to 400 was where we'd draw the line. We've
     become a lot more flexible. Smallest project I've got running is around 160 ha. We'd run a quick
     desktop assessment first to see what's actually eligible on your place -- no cost, takes a couple of
     days.

"NOT INTERESTED RIGHT NOW"

      FARMER

     Not really interested at the moment.

      BEN

     No worries at all -- is it alright if I touch base in a few months when things settle down a bit?
      A soft door-leave costs nothing and keeps the lead alive. Never a hard hang-up.

Part 4 -- Closing the Visit

As soon as there's a yes signal -- close immediately. Don't narrate more of the offer.

CLOSE WITH TWO SPECIFIC OPTIONS

      BEN

     Good stuff. He's flexible -- are you around Monday or Wednesday afternoon next week? I'll send
     his number through as well so you've got it if anything changes.
      Two specific options closes visits. Open-ended availability ("are you free sometime?") produces open-ended
      outcomes.

IF THEY WANT NUMBERS BEFORE COMMITTING TO A VISIT

      FARMER

     What sort of returns would we even be looking at?

      BEN

     Hard to give you a real number without looking at your country first. What we can do is run a free
     desktop soil carbon report -- takes a couple of days, no obligation. It uses satellite data and about
     20,000 soil data points from live projects across Australia. Shows your carbon potential and what
     we'd exclude. All I need is your lot and plan numbers -- they're on your rates notice.
      The HORIZON Snapshot converts skeptical farmers. Offer it as a standalone next step -- don't ask for a
      Snapshot and a visit in the same breath.
Hard Rules

   � No company name in the first sentence. Earn the conversation first.
   � Reason for calling is in sentence two or three -- not after a warm-up. Farmers are straight to the

       point.
   � Be honest about the soil carbon connection when asked directly. Evasion kills trust faster than

       the product does.
   � Close with two specific days. Open-ended availability produces open-ended outcomes.
   � Don't offer the Snapshot and a visit in the same breath. One ask at a time.
   � Soft door-leave on every no -- "Is it alright if I touch base in a few months?"
