## 2026-05-13 update — the curiosity-frame cold-open is working

Ben has converged on a specific cold-call framing that's producing "very short" calls ending in farm-visit bookings. He articulated it in-flow on 2026-05-13. Capture verbatim — this is the validated language pattern as it stands today.

### The full sequence

1. **Open Aubrey-based.** *"We're Aubrey based"* establishes geography before company. Same trick Hobbs uses — substitutes a place for a corporate name.
2. **The Hobbs-as-attractor curiosity frame.** Hobbs is from America, Texas rancher, on board as resident grazier — *"he's keen to see how we do it over here."* Note Ben's deliberate phrasing: this version honours the prospect ("he's keen to see") rather than positioning AgriProve as the teacher. Ben explicitly avoided *"we want to show him how it's done"* as "a bit arrogant."
3. **The "selected, not solicited" implicit compliment.** *"Looking at what's working across the Riverina grazing operation, specifically ones generating strong results, and your property has come up as one of those."* Puts the power on the prospect — they've been chosen because they're doing cool stuff.
4. **Tone commitment.** *"Padded conversation,"* *"fence line chat,"* *"very lean back, not sort of too intense."* This is a deliberate posture, not just a vibe.

### Sourcing question handler (when prospects ask "how did you find me?")

> *"It's part of the regional outreach program. It's, you know, all available on, on your website and, you know, contacting Riverina Enterprises that are doing good things and, you know, with you having a website, then it would have made life simple for us to be able to reach out to you as part of that Outreach program."*

This handles the question without dodging. Acknowledges the public-data source plainly + reinforces the curated framing.

### The "take it back to the team" confidence shield

Ben got this from Harry (LawrieCo) early on. When a prospect raises something technical Ben can't field on the spot:

> *"I prefer to give you the right information and take it back to the team."*

This lets Ben stay in the conversation without overclaiming expertise. He uses it when he's in agronomy/technical territory he doesn't fully own. It's the calling-discipline equivalent of Hobbs's "I'm just the Grazier" de-positioning — same purpose (manage the gap between rep knowledge and customer expectation), different mechanism (defer to team rather than minimise own role).

### Ben's read of why it's working

> *"I think playing on that a little bit is why I'm getting some easy — not easy, but the conversation is very short, and it's a yep, and it's a booking."*

The curiosity frame is doing the heavy lifting. Conversations are short because the framing answers the prospect's "why are you calling me" question before they ask.

### The Hunter region adaptation problem (open)

The framing was built for the Riverina cohort. Ben is moving into the Hunter region (different farm sizes, different operations) and is asking: *"what's another layer for getting on farm in the Hunter?"* His coping framework: *"I don't have to be Hobbs. I can just be me and be okay with that"* — but he wants to be armed.

This is a real product question for the system: how does the curiosity-frame language need to adapt for a region with different topology, different scale, different cultural texture? Worth a focused chat with Hobbs before he leaves, anchored on this question specifically.

**"It's Ben calling here from AgriProve in Albury"** — used verbatim or near-verbatim in 8 of 14 sales calls. Diametric opposite of Hobbs's company-invisible opens.

### Rapport-build pattern

Geography-anchored, content-aligned. Different from Hobbs's identity-aligned style:

- **Central Coast home reference** — Ben mentions where he lives, asks where the customer lives. Used to deep effect with Thompson (golf-club detail) and Boag (rainfall mils that day).

## Top 3 transferable Hobbs moves Ben should adopt

### 1. Hold value-prop content for the visit

The discipline shift: use phone purely to book the visit, save the full content for in-person where it can be paired with live math on the operator's actual hectares. The phone-pitch produces shallow info-share, not commitment moments.

**Cost-benefit:** Ben loses the impression that he's been "thorough" on the call. Gains: an in-person moment that's worth booking + a customer who isn't already saturated with the pitch by the time they sit down.

### 2. Tier-1 reframes verbatim — especially for 25% / 25-year / "lock up"

These are one-sentence kills. Ben is paraphrasing the structure but losing the punch. **The exact words do the work.**

- 25% reframe: *"You're taking 25% out of what you weren't going to get anything from before."*
- 25-year reframe: *"Our contract basically boils down to: make a commitment to be better, make a commitment to keep your land in the agricultural system for 25 years."*
- Lock-up reframe: *"We don't do trees. It's nothing to do with trees. It's to do with your soil."*

Worth Ben rehearsing these verbatim until they're reflex.

### 3. Sequenced low-commitment close on the call itself

Hobbs's ladder: visit → Snapshot → land-mgmt call → registration. Ben uses fragments but doesn't *name the ladder*.

On a stalling call (Hills, Milawa), ending with *"I'll come back with concerns I can quash"* is weaker than:

> *"Let me do three things — send you the [next-thing], book a [low-friction-thing], and circle back [specific-date]."*

The named ladder reduces cognitive load on the customer and makes the next step feel small.
