## ⚠️ INTERPRETATION SHIFTS — flagged on verbatim re-read

The following corrections apply to statements made in the earlier handoff (`2026-05-11-claude-code-handoff.md`) and policy review draft (`2026-05-11-policy-review-draft.md`). The earlier documents contain inaccuracies that this file supersedes.

### Shift 1: "Competitive Business" is NOT defined in the Letter of Offer

The handoff stated: *"Competitive Business" = soil carbon measurement, quantification, and issuance of soil carbon units*

**That text does not appear in the document.** The term "Competitive Business" is used in cl 21.1 but never defined. The Letter of Offer says only:

> "you will not, without the prior written consent of the Company, directly or indirectly, anywhere within Australia for the Restraint Period, on your own account or for any other person, firm, or business"

The term is left to contextual interpretation. This is legally significant — an undefined restraint term may be narrower or broader than assumed, and its enforceability depends on how a court would read it in context.

### Shift 2: cl 21.1.1 has a critical limiter ("except where")

The handoff omitted the exception clause. The full text reads:

> "be employed in, provide services to, conduct or otherwise engage in a Competitive Business, **except where the capacity in which you are so employed or provide services does not involve you performing work of a similar nature to that performed by you in the course of your employment with the Company**"

This narrows the non-compete significantly. Employment at a competitor is permitted if the role does not involve similar work.

### Shift 3: cl 21.1.6 is NOT "inevitable disclosure"

The handoff described cl 21.1.6 as an "inevitable disclosure" restriction. The actual text is:

> "accept any employment or render any services that would **require** you to reveal, base judgement upon, or otherwise use any Confidential Information referred to in clause 19"

This is about employment that *requires* use of Confidential Information — a higher threshold than "inevitable disclosure" (which concerns inadvertent or unavoidable use). Different legal standard. The term "inevitable disclosure" does not appear in the document.

### Shift 4: cl 21.1.2 is scoped to Key Clients with whom you had dealings

The handoff paraphrased this as covering "all clients/customers/suppliers." The actual text restricts it to:

> "Key Clients with whom you had dealings in the 12-month period prior to termination of your employment"

### Shift 5: Geographic cascading fallback does not exist

The handoff referenced "cascading fallback: state, 50km." No such text appears in the document. The geographic scope is stated once in cl 21.1:

> "anywhere within Australia for the Restraint Period"

There is no fallback to state/territory or 50km radius. Cl 21.4 defines "Key Client" — it is not about geographic scope.

## cl 19 — CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION (perpetual)

### cl 19.1 — Definition

> For the purpose of this letter, "Confidential Information" means:
>
> − all trade secrets, industrial processes, intellectual property, or any information concerning the business, affairs, or finances of the Company; and
>
> − all trade secrets, industrial processes, intellectual property, or any information concerning the business or finances of the suppliers, clients, or customers of the Company; and
>
> − any information which has been disclosed to the Company or any of its Related Bodies Corporate by any third party (including clients or customers) under any agreement which required the Company or any of its Subsidiaries to keep such information confidential.
>
> Without limiting the above definition, "Confidential Information" includes, but is not limited to:
>
> 19.1.1 The Company's business plans and forecasts;
>
> 19.1.2 The Company's financial records, reports, accounts, and proposals;
>
> 19.1.3 The Company's materials, manuals, and staff training materials;
>
> 19.1.4 All data, know how, algorithms and technology associated with the Company's operations;
>
> 19.1.5 Client's records;
>
> 19.1.6 Client lists and names of client contacts;
>
> 19.1.7 The contents of the Company's project, candidate and client databases, and file storage;
>
> 19.1.8 Client lists and names of client contacts that you have developed while in the employment of the Company, but have stored outside of the Company's technology platform, such as in Social Media/Professional Networking platforms, such as, but not limited to, LinkedIn, Twitter, and Facebook.

### cl 19.2 — Obligation scope

> During the course of your employment with the Company, or after the termination of your employment, you must keep confidential all Confidential Information that becomes known to you in the course of your employment with the Company.

### cl 19.3 — Prohibited acts

> You must not:
>
> 19.3.1 publish or divulge the Confidential Information to any person, corporation, firm, or entity; or
>
> 19.3.2 use the Confidential Information for any purpose other than those of the Company.

### cl 19.4 — Duty to prevent third-party misuse

> You must take all reasonable steps to prevent all third parties from misusing Confidential Information as outlined in clauses 19.3.1 and 19.3.2.

### cl 19.5 — Notification duty

> You must immediately notify the Company if you suspect Confidential Information has been improperly used or disclosed.

### cl 19.6 — Client confidentiality

> It is expressly forbidden to discuss Confidential Information revealed by clients outside the Company. Sensitive client information should only be discussed with other employees where absolutely necessary.

### cl 19.7 — Removal from premises

> Confidential Information or copies of Confidential Information must not be removed from the Company's premises, except where your employment requires it and where the Company has given consent.

### cl 19.8 — Perpetual obligation

> Your obligations under this clause exist both during your employment and after your employment ceases.

### cl 19.9 — Breach = serious misconduct

> Any breach by you of this clause shall be regarded by the Company as serious misconduct for which you may be dismissed without notice or payment of salary in lieu of notice.

### cl 19.10 — Return of documents on termination

> On termination of your employment, you must return to the Company all papers, data, records and documents in your possession which relate in any way to the Confidential Information of the Company.

## cl 20 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY (total assignment)

### cl 20.1 — Assignment scope

> You agree that the Company owns absolutely free from encumbrances all Intellectual Property made, discovered or created by you (whether alone or in conjunction with others, and whether created outside your normal place of work or during or outside standard working hours):
>
> 20.1.1 in the course of, or as a consequence of, performing your duties;
>
> 20.1.2 using the Group's or any client's premises, resources or facilities;
>
> 20.1.3 directly or indirectly as a result of your access to the Confidential Information; or
>
> 20.1.4 in respect of, or associated with, any of the Company's services.

### cl 20.2 — Disclosure duty

> Irrespective of the termination of the employment, you must promptly give the Company full details of all Intellectual Property which you created during the employment which may be of benefit to the Company or any Group Company.

### cl 20.3 — Cooperation duty

> You agree, during and after the employment at the Company's expense, to do all such acts and things including without limitation executing documents, as the Company may request, in respect of the Intellectual Property, to enable the Company to:
>
> 20.3.1 apply for any registered rights in such Intellectual Property;
>
> 20.3.2 be effectively vested with such Intellectual Property; and
>
> 20.3.3 obtain, maintain, defend, and enforce such Intellectual Property.

### cl 20.4 — Trust pending assignment

> Prior to the Company being enabled under clause 20.3, you will hold in trust for the Company (or any relevant Group Company) any rights you hold in the Intellectual Property.

### cl 20.5 — Do not imperil IP (no publishing)

> You shall not do or omit to do anything which will or may result in imperilling the Intellectual Property vesting in or being assigned to the Company (or any relevant Group Company) under this Agreement, including without limitation, publishing any of it.

### IP Definition (following cl 20.5)

> Intellectual Property means all intellectual property rights including, patents, utility models, designs and trademarks, domain name, applications for such rights and the right to make such applications, inventions, copyright, know-how and similar rights in any part of the world.

## cl 21 — DUTY FOLLOWING TERMINATION (RESTRICTIVE COVENANT)

### cl 21.1 — Preamble and restraint

> You acknowledge and agree that the Company has invested a substantial amount of time and effort to develop business relationships with its clients and suppliers. You also acknowledge and agree that you will have access to and will in your role be involved in developing Confidential Information. In particular, you acknowledge that, having regard to the Confidential Information that you will have access to in your employment, you will not be able to separate your knowledge of that Confidential Information if you were to perform work of a similar nature for a Competitive Business. To protect the Company's goodwill and Confidential Information, you agree that you will not, without the prior written consent of the Company, directly or indirectly, anywhere within Australia for the Restraint Period, on your own account or for any other person, firm, or business:

### cl 21.1.1 — Non-compete (with exception)

> be employed in, provide services to, conduct or otherwise engage in a Competitive Business, except where the capacity in which you are so employed or provide services does not involve you performing work of a similar nature to that performed by you in the course of your employment with the Company;

### cl 21.1.2 — Non-solicitation (Key Clients)

> approach, canvass, solicit or endeavour to entice away from the Company, or have dealings with in connection with a Competitive Business, Key Clients with whom you had dealings in the 12-month period prior to termination of your employment;

### cl 21.1.3 — Non-solicitation (employees)

> employ, engage, solicit, or entice away or endeavour to employ, engage, solicit, or entice away from the Company any of the Company's employees with whom you had dealings in the 12-month period prior to termination of your employment;

### cl 21.1.4 — Non-solicitation (contractors)

> employ, engage, solicit, or entice away or endeavour to employ, engage, solicit, or entice away from the Company any of the Company's candidates, contractors or consultants with whom you had dealings in the 12-month period prior to termination of your employment;

### cl 21.1.5 — Procuring breach

> counsel, procure or otherwise assist any person to do any of the acts referred to above; or

### cl 21.1.6 — Employment requiring use of Confidential Information

> accept any employment or render any services that would require you to reveal, base judgement upon, or otherwise use any Confidential Information referred to in clause 19.

### cl 21.2 — Severability

> The covenants set out in clause 21.1 shall be construed and have effect as separate covenants, with the covenant contained in each sub-clause being separate from each other. If any of the covenants are invalid or unenforceable for any reason, such invalidity on enforceability shall not prejudice or affect the validity or enforceability of the other covenant.

### cl 21.3 — Restraint Period

> "Restraint Period" means a period of three (3) months from the date of termination of your employment.

### cl 21.4 — Key Clients

> 21.4.1 "Key Client" means any person, corporation or business who or which are or were at the relevant time clients of or key suppliers to the Company.
>
> 21.4.2 A list of "Key Clients" pertaining to the above will be given to you upon termination of this agreement.
>
> 21.4.3 In the event you are contacted by a Key Client contact during the "Restraint Period", you must refer the client back to the Company.

### cl 21.5 — Reasonableness acknowledgement

> You acknowledge and agree that the restrictions contained in this clause are reasonably required in the circumstances to protect the Company's business and in particular, its goodwill and Confidential Information.
