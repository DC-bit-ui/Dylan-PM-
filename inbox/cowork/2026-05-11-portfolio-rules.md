# 9 Portfolio Rules — Full Text with Clause Citations
**Date:** 2026-05-11
**Source:** Synthesised from 14 compliance categories across 30 documents
**Purpose:** Standing rules for all career-portfolio content creation

---

## Rule 1: Do not disclose proprietary information

**Full text:** Do not disclose proprietary methodologies, SOC models, geotagged data, algorithms, know-how, technology specifics, customer contract terms, financials, ACCU volumes, or project yields.

**Governing clauses:**
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1: "all trade secrets, industrial processes, intellectual property, or any information concerning the business, affairs, or finances of the Company"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1.4: "All data, know how, algorithms and technology associated with the Company's operations"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.3.1: "publish or divulge the Confidential Information to any person, corporation, firm, or entity"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.8: "Your obligations under this clause exist both during your employment and after your employment ceases"
- Letter of Offer cl 20.1: Company owns all IP created during employment
- DRAFT Protected Information Policy s3.1: Geotagged datasets restricted absolutely
- DRAFT Access Policy: PROPRIETARY class restricted to Directors and nominated persons

**Scope:** Perpetual (cl 19.8). Applies during and after employment.

## Rule 2: Do not include personal information

**Full text:** Do not include personal information of customers, employees, or stakeholders.

**Governing clauses:**
- Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) — Australian Privacy Principles
- Privacy Policy POLPRV (active, Ed 1, 19/03/2024): AgriProve's obligations under APPs
- DRAFT Access Policy s3.1: Private Information defined as "information that pertains to an individual employee, including but not limited to personal contact details, medical records, financial information, and performance evaluations"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1.5: "Client's records"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1.6: "Client lists and names of client contacts"

**Scope:** Statutory (Privacy Act) + contractual (cl 19). Perpetual.

## Rule 3: Do not claim to speak for AgriProve

**Full text:** Do not claim to speak for AgriProve or represent company positions.

**Governing clauses:**
- HSE003 Duty of Care & Code of Conduct s5.3: Public comment restrictions (signed at onboarding, contractually binding)
- Delegation of Authority POLDoA s16: Media delegation — only authorised persons may make public statements
- Letter of Offer cl 13.2: "use your best endeavours to promote the development, profitability, interests and welfare of the Company" (implying: do not undermine)

**Scope:** During employment (cl 13). HSE003 signed obligation — binding regardless of policy retirement status.

## Rule 4: Describe role and outcomes using public information only

**Full text:** Do describe your role, responsibilities, and outcomes using only publicly available information.

**Governing clauses:**
- This is the affirmative safe harbour. No clause prohibits describing your own role and responsibilities in general terms.
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1 definition of Confidential Information does not extend to: general industry knowledge, publicly available company information (website, press releases, public filings), or personal professional skills.
- Position Description (250226 Product Manager Innovation): Role description is company-internal but the *type of work* (product management, stakeholder engagement, roadmap ownership) is general professional knowledge.

**Boundary:** "I managed a product roadmap for a soil carbon measurement platform" = safe. "The roadmap included [specific features/timelines]" = Confidential Information.

## Rule 5: Do not publish work product

**Full text:** Do not publish any work product created during employment — PRDs, code, design specs, data models, research outputs are assigned IP.

**Governing clauses:**
- Letter of Offer cl 20.1: "the Company owns absolutely free from encumbrances all Intellectual Property made, discovered or created by you (whether alone or in conjunction with others, and whether created outside your normal place of work or during or outside standard working hours)"
- Letter of Offer cl 20.5: "You shall not do or omit to do anything which will or may result in imperilling the Intellectual Property vesting in or being assigned to the Company... including without limitation, publishing any of it"
- Letter of Offer IP definition: "all intellectual property rights including, patents, utility models, designs and trademarks, domain name, applications for such rights and the right to make such applications, inventions, copyright, know-how and similar rights in any part of the world"

**Scope:** Total. Covers IP created outside normal hours and workplace. Perpetual per cl 20.2 ("irrespective of the termination of the employment"). This is the broadest clause — it captures anything created that is associated with the Company's services.

## Rule 6: Anonymise all case studies

**Full text:** Anonymise all case studies — remove identifiable customer, property, or project details. Do not reference specific client relationships, including LinkedIn connections.

**Governing clauses:**
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1.5: "Client's records"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1.6: "Client lists and names of client contacts"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.1.8: "Client lists and names of client contacts that you have developed while in the employment of the Company, but have stored outside of the Company's technology platform, such as in Social Media/Professional Networking platforms, such as, but not limited to, LinkedIn, Twitter, and Facebook"
- Letter of Offer cl 19.6: "It is expressly forbidden to discuss Confidential Information revealed by clients outside the Company"
- Privacy Policy POLPRV: Personal information obligations under APPs
- DRAFT Protected Information Policy s3.1: Geotagged data restricted absolutely

**Scope:** Perpetual (cl 19.8). Cl 19.1.8 is unusually specific — it explicitly extends to LinkedIn connections developed during employment. This means even publicly visible LinkedIn connections cannot be cited as portfolio evidence of client relationships.

## Rule 7: Reference the regulatory framework as professional credentials

**Full text:** Reference the regulatory framework (CFI Act, ERF, Carbon Industry Code, AFSL) as professional credentials without disclosing internal compliance details.

**Governing clauses:**
- Carbon Industry Code of Conduct (v1.0, June 2018): Public document — referencing it is safe
- CFI Act, ERF Method Determinations: Public legislation — referencing them demonstrates regulatory literacy
- AFSL Risk Policy: Internal compliance details are Confidential Information per cl 19.1
- Terms of Use POLTOU s4: IP rights in company's public-facing terms

**Boundary:** "I work within the ERF regulatory framework and the Carbon Industry Code of Conduct" = safe. "AgriProve's AFSL compliance process involves [specifics]" = Confidential.

## Rule 8: Conduct portfolio work outside working hours on personal devices

**Full text:** Conduct all portfolio work outside working hours on personal devices and infrastructure.

**Governing clauses:**
- Letter of Offer cl 13.1: "devote the whole of your working time and attention to the business of the Company... and you must not be engaged in any other activity unless you have the written approval of the Company"
- DRAFT AUP s1.1–1.4: Do not use AgriProve resources for personal use; do not store personal files on company storage; do not store non-public AgriProve data on personal devices
- DRAFT AUP s4.2.8: Storing work-related data on personal devices prohibited unless explicitly approved
- DRAFT AUP s4.2.9: Uploading work data to cloud/third-party apps prohibited unless explicitly approved
- DRAFT AUP s4.2.10: Emailing work data to non-company addresses prohibited unless explicitly approved

**Scope:** During employment. The DRAFT AUP is unapproved but cl 13.1 is binding and provides the same coverage. After termination, cl 19.10 requires return of all documents — portfolio work should never have involved company data in the first place.

## Rule 9: Post-employment restraint awareness

**Full text:** Be aware of the 3-month post-employment restraint (Letter of Offer cl 21) — avoid creating portfolio content that could evidence that future employment would *require* you to use Confidential Information (cl 21.1.6).

**Governing clauses:**
- Letter of Offer cl 21.1: Restraint scope — "anywhere within Australia for the Restraint Period"
- Letter of Offer cl 21.1.1: Non-compete with exception — permitted if "the capacity in which you are so employed or provide services does not involve you performing work of a similar nature"
- Letter of Offer cl 21.1.6: "accept any employment or render any services that would require you to reveal, base judgement upon, or otherwise use any Confidential Information referred to in clause 19"
- Letter of Offer cl 21.3: Restraint Period = 3 months from termination
- Letter of Offer cl 21.4: Key Client definition (clients/key suppliers at relevant time)

**Scope:** 3 months post-termination for cl 21.1.1–21.1.5. Cl 21.1.6 arguably extends beyond the Restraint Period since it cross-references cl 19, which is perpetual — but the chapeau of cl 21.1 limits all sub-clauses to "the Restraint Period." Legal advice recommended on this point.

**Key nuance (corrected):** "Competitive Business" is used but NOT defined in the Letter of Offer. The cl 21.1.1 exception means employment at a competitor is permitted if the role does not involve similar work. The portfolio should demonstrate breadth of PM capability, not depth of competitive domain knowledge.
