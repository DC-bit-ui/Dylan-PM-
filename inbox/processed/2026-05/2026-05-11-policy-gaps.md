## 1. DRAFT Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

**File:** `240308 POLICY e1 DRAFT Acceptable Use.docx`
**Status:** DRAFT, unapproved. Author: Stephen Warnken. 6 pages.
**Last modified:** 2024-03-08

### Relevant sections (verbatim)

#### Section 1 — Key points

> 1.1. Do not use AgriProve resources for personal use.
> 1.2. Do not use AgriProve accounts or AgriProve logins for personal use.
> 1.3. Do not store personal files (files relevant to a person and not relevant to AgriProve) on AgriProve storage devices or storage platforms.
> 1.4. Do not store non-public AgriProve data, or content on personal storage devices.

#### Section 4.2 — Prohibited activities (relevant selections)

> 4.2.5 disclosing confidential or proprietary company information to any unauthorised third party
> 4.2.8 storing work-related data on personal devices, including but not limited to personal computers, laptops, mobile phones, tablets, and storage devices, unless explicitly approved by the company
> 4.2.9 uploading work-related data to cloud-based services or other third-party applications, unless explicitly approved by the company
> 4.2.10 emailing or sharing work-related data with non-company email addresses, unless explicitly approved by the company.

#### Section 6 — Distribution of Data (THE AI GAP)

This is the section that was supposed to cover AI usage. Verbatim, the entire section reads:

> 6. Distribution of Data (including input into AI or online text compression/summarisation tools)
> Cyber Security
> Sharing of data – internal and external
> 6.1. NDAs and Privacy
> 6.1.1. Non-disclosure Agreement's do not circumvent AgriProve's responsibilities under Privacy laws.

That is the complete text of Section 6. The heading mentions AI but the body contains only a single sentence about NDAs and privacy. There are no rules about:
- What data can or cannot be input into AI tools
- Which AI tools are approved or prohibited
- Data classification requirements for AI input
- Consent or approval processes for AI usage
- Output handling (what to do with AI-generated content)
- Model training opt-out requirements
- Prompt injection or data leakage risks

#### Section 7 — Working from Home

> 7.1. When videoconferencing, if available, use a blurred or false background.
> 7.2. Do not use public or non-password protected Wi-Fi networks, only connect to password protected private Wi-Fi networks, or a personal hotspot from a mobile device connected to a mobile data network.

### Portfolio impact

The AUP's key points (s1.1–1.4) and prohibitions (s4.2.8–4.2.10) reinforce Portfolio Rule 8 (personal devices/infrastructure). The AI gap (s6) means there are no organisational rules governing AI tool usage for any purpose, including portfolio creation. Self-imposed conservative limits are necessary.

## 2. DRAFT Access Policy for Sensitive Information

**File:** `240321 DRAFT Access Policy for Sensitive Information v1.docx`
**Status:** DRAFT, unapproved. Author: Stephen Warnken. 6 pages.
**Last modified:** 2024-09-27

### Relevant sections (verbatim)

#### Section 1 — Intent

> 1.1. This policy establishes guidelines for accessing private and confidential information within the workplace to ensure the protection of sensitive data and uphold the trust of employees, clients, and stakeholders.

#### Section 2 — Definitions

> Private Information is information that pertains to an individual employee, including but not limited to personal contact details, medical records, financial information, and performance evaluations.

> Confidential Information is defined under AgriProve's Code of Conduct, Confidentiality Deed, as well as AgriProve's employee Letter of Offer. In brief, Confidential Information is sensitive to the organisation, including trade secrets, financial data, proprietary methodologies, client lists, strategic plans, and information marked as Confidential.

> Under the control of AgriProve is defined as Devices, Platforms, Applications or Systems which are not owned by AgriProve and, which are only accessible by one person, or can be closed, deleted, or removed in any way, through one person's discretion, are not considered to be under the control of AgriProve.

#### Section 3.2 — Confidential Information access

> a) Access to confidential information is restricted to authorised personnel who require such information to perform their job functions effectively.
> b) Confidential information should be accessed and used solely for legitimate business purposes and must not be disclosed to unauthorised individuals or entities.
> d) Unauthorised access, use, or disclosure of confidential information is considered a serious breach of trust and may result in disciplinary action, legal consequences, and termination of employment.
> e) Confidential information should be appropriately labelled and stored in secure systems with restricted access controls.

#### Section 3.5 — Extended (confidentiality classifications)

> Confidential Restricted Classes:
>
> PERSONNEL — employee personnel files
> FINANCIAL — transactions relating to company financials, financial models, forecasts, projections
> PROPRIETARY — relating to business strategy, intellectual property

> Denotes authority — (A) view all, (S) view some which has been specified in writing.

#### Confidential Information on personal devices

> Confidential Information may be hosted on a personal device while that device is under the care and control of that authorised person. Confidential Information must immediately be deleted from all devices and all storage systems, not under the control of AgriProve, and must stop all activities which would add confidential information to any device or storage system not owned by AgriProve, upon notification of termination, suspension, redundancy or other identifier in which employment or engagement or ownership or an Agreement is to result in termination or cessation.

#### Information received but not yet classified

> Information received to an address, platform, application or device not under the control of AgriProve must be transferred to be under the control of AgriProve as soon as practicable. Withholding information pertinent to AgriProve's business may result in disciplinary and/or legal action.

### Portfolio impact

The three restricted classes (PERSONNEL, FINANCIAL, PROPRIETARY) reinforce Portfolio Rules 1, 2, and 6. The "under the control of AgriProve" definition is noteworthy — personal devices are explicitly *not* under AgriProve's control, which means Confidential Information on personal devices must be deleted on termination. This reinforces the clean separation required by Portfolio Rule 8.

## 3. DRAFT Protected Information Policy

**File:** `2230817 DRAFT Protected Information Policy v1.docx`
**Status:** DRAFT, unapproved. Author: Stephen Warnken. 2 pages.
**Last modified:** 2023-08-17

### Full text (verbatim — the document is very short)

#### Section 1 — Intent

> 1.1. The purpose of this document is to define restricted datasets / IP held by AgriProve which may not be distributed outside of the organisation under any circumstance.
> 1.2. This requirement is additional to AgriProve's responsibilities to protect Client and AgriProve data under Australian Privacy Principles, and international and regional Data Protection and Privacy constraints.

#### Section 3 — Restricted data / IP

> 3.1. Geotag(s), or another identifier which may geographically position or locate a dataset
> 3.1.1. Geotagged datasets, includes but is not limited to:
> 3.1.1.1. laboratory results of a soil sample which includes the geotag
> 3.1.1.2. imaged data (for example, photographic, infra-red, spectroscopy, spectral or other) which includes a geotag.
> 3.1.2. Exclusions:
> 3.1.2.1. Geotags on specific datasets may be distributed outside of the organisation if a mutual agreement is established prior to the collection of that tagged dataset, identifying the purpose for collection and distribution of that specific data. For example, a Shape file is a geotagged dataset.

#### RACI

> Responsible: Each person is responsible to comply with this document. It is the responsibility of all leaders to inform staff and contractors of this document and maintain standards of business ethics within their teams.
> Accountable: The final authority for this document lies with the Board of Directors.

### Portfolio impact

This is the absolute restriction on geotagged data — "may not be distributed outside of the organisation under any circumstance" (s1.1). Reinforces Portfolio Rule 1 (no geotagged data) and Rule 6 (anonymise — ensure anonymisation cannot be reverse-engineered to a location). Note: the document is incomplete — Sections 4 and 5 are empty headings, suggesting it was abandoned mid-draft.

## CRITICAL GAP: AI/GenAI Usage Policy — Recommendation

### Current state

AgriProve has **no AI/GenAI usage policy**. The DRAFT AUP s6 heading mentions "AI or online text compression/summarisation tools" but the body contains only one sentence about NDAs. There are no rules governing:

- Employee use of AI tools for work purposes
- Data classification requirements for AI input
- Approved vs. prohibited AI tools
- Output ownership and review requirements
- Model training data implications
- Client data in AI tools
- AI-generated content disclosure

### Risk assessment [high confidence]

This is the single most critical organisational policy gap identified in this assessment. Every employee with access to company data can currently input it into any AI tool without violating a specific policy — they'd only violate cl 19 (confidentiality) if the data constitutes Confidential Information, which requires case-by-case judgment rather than clear rules.

### Self-imposed rules for portfolio work (pending organisational policy)

Given the absence of policy, the following conservative limits apply to any AI-assisted portfolio work:

1. **No AgriProve proprietary data into any AI tool for portfolio creation.** This includes: client data, financial data, methodology details, system architecture, internal documents, meeting transcripts, internal communications.
2. **No AI tool prompts that describe internal AgriProve operations.** Even without pasting data, describing how systems work in a prompt could constitute disclosure per cl 19.3.1.
3. **AI tools may be used for:** general writing assistance, formatting, public information research, PM methodology advice — anything where the input does not contain or describe Confidential Information.
4. **Output review:** All AI-generated portfolio content must be reviewed against the 9 Portfolio Rules before publishing. AI tools may inadvertently generate content that resembles or implies Confidential Information based on public training data — this doesn't create a breach, but could create an appearance issue.

### Recommendation to leadership [ASSUMPTION — Dylan has not requested this]

AgriProve should prioritise completing the AUP Section 6 with a standalone AI Usage Policy or addendum covering, at minimum:

- Approved AI tools list (with data classification requirements per tool)
- Prohibited uses (client data, financial data, IP-related data)
- Approval process for new AI tool adoption
- Data classification gate: which information classes can be input into which tool categories
- Output ownership clarity (company IP per cl 20, or tool-specific?)
- Training data opt-out requirements for enterprise tools
- Incident reporting for inadvertent data disclosure via AI tools

This recommendation is noted but not actioned — it falls outside the scope of the portfolio compliance assessment and would require Dylan to raise with leadership at his discretion.
