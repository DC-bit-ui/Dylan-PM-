What happens if I sell my property?

Short answer: selling is a normal, well-worn process, and you are never locked in. Your project moves with the land or comes off it cleanly, and the credits you have earned stay yours.

The short version.

A carbon project does not trap you on your land.

A registered soil carbon project "runs with the land". That sounds technical, but it works in your favour: it means the project is tied to the property, not to you personally. When you sell, you have real, straightforward options, and the law already has a clear path for each one.

Better still, AgriProve stays on as the project's proponent through the sale. That means the buyer does not have to learn how to run a carbon project, take on the reporting, or do anything to keep it going. The compliance work stays with us.

The one real commitment you make is to keep the land in an agricultural system for the permanence period. Not a set crop or a set practice, just farmland rather than a housing estate or a quarry. You keep full ownership and title the whole way through.

You are not signing away your ability to sell. You are adding an income stream a buyer can inherit, or that you can wind down on your terms.

When you sell.

Three ways it can go.

Tap each one to see exactly how it works. Every path here follows the Clean Energy Regulator's own published process for selling a property with a carbon project on it.

1. You sell the whole property

The buyer continues the project, and they keep earning from it.

The project transfers with the property. This is the most common outcome, and the Regulator processes these transfers as routine.

  1. The project, its records and the legal right to run it transfer to the buyer as part of the sale.
  2. AgriProve can remain the project proponent, so the buyer inherits the income without inheriting the workload.
  3. We lodge the proponent variation with the Clean Energy Regulator. They aim to process these within 90 days.
  4. You and the buyer agree how to handle credits in the current reporting period. We can time it so credits already earned are issued to you before settlement.
A carbon project is a feature you can sell, not a liability you pass on.

2. You sell only part of the property

We shape the project around what you keep. The sold block comes off.

Your whole property never has to be in the project. We design the project boundary around your plans, including land you might sell or develop later.

  1. If the carbon area sits entirely on the land you are keeping, you carry on exactly as before. The sold block simply was not part of the project.
  2. If a block you are selling contains part of a carbon area, we remove that area from the project before the sale. The rest continues.
  3. Planning a sale or a wind farm or a development on one block? Tell us up front and we leave it out of the project from day one.
Flexible by design. You decide which paddocks are in.

3. You want out entirely

You can revoke the project. Treat it as a last resort: it means repaying credits already issued.

If neither you nor a buyer keeps the project running, you can revoke it. This is the one path with a real cost, so it is worth understanding clearly. Revoking means returning an equivalent number of all the credits the project has ever been issued, buying them back on the market if you have already sold them. If the project was never issued credits, there is nothing to return.

  1. You rarely need to revoke. Selling to someone who keeps farming, or simply pausing the activities while the project stays registered, avoids it altogether.
  2. There is a natural review at the five year mark. If the project has not delivered credits or no longer stacks up, you can step away before any credits are in play, with nothing to return.
  3. If you do revoke after credits were issued, the exiting landholder repays them. A simple way to stay covered: hold your credits rather than selling them, so you always have them on hand.
Revoking is a choice of last resort, not a trap. Transferring or pausing almost always beats it.

Your credits.

The credits you earn are yours.

The thing landholders worry about most is the credits. Here is how that actually works.

Earned means earned

Credits issued to you for carbon you have already stored are yours. Selling the property does not claw them back.

A genuine asset

Australian Carbon Credit Units are a recognised, tradable asset with domestic and international demand. Holding them is optionality, not risk.

We carry the long-term risk

AgriProve is the registered project proponent. If stored carbon is ever lost after the permanence period, that liability sits with us, not with you.

Timed around your sale

We can structure the reporting period so credits earned before settlement are issued to you, not the buyer.

Common questions.

Questions landholders actually ask.

The honest answers, in plain English.

Does my whole property have to be in the project?
No. You can enrol a quarter, a half, or whichever paddocks suit your management. We build the project around your plans, so if there is land you may sell, develop, or keep out for any reason, we leave it out from the start.
What if the government changes the scheme over 25 years?
No one can promise what any government does over 25 years, and we will not pretend otherwise. What we can say: once your project is registered it sits inside a legal framework that makes retrospective change hard. Historically, changes to the credit scheme have applied to new projects going forward, not to projects already registered. Your registered project is a binding arrangement with the Clean Energy Regulator and the Australian Government.
What if my land goes backwards, say a fire or a drought?
The scheme is built for this. A risk buffer is held back from every project precisely to absorb natural reversals like fire and drought, and the rules account for events outside your control. We work through these situations with you when they happen. You are not personally exposed to a bill the moment the weather turns.
What if I want to subdivide later?
You can subdivide, with one rule of thumb: land that stays in the project needs to remain farmland. So if a block might become housing, industry or similar, keep it out of the project from the start, or take it out before that change happens. Tell us your plans and we design the boundary around them.
What happens to my project if AgriProve is no longer around?
AgriProve is the registered proponent, so we carry the reporting and compliance. Your agreement gives you the right to replace the proponent, so if we were ever not performing you can bring in another provider or step in yourself. The credits you have already earned stay an asset you hold either way.
Twenty five years feels like a long commitment.
It is the permanence period the project commits to, but it is not a 25 year handcuff on you personally. As this page lays out, you can sell, pass the property to family, hand the project to a buyer, or wind it down. There is also a review at the five year mark to check the project is still working for you. The land keeps doing what land does. The project just sits alongside it.
