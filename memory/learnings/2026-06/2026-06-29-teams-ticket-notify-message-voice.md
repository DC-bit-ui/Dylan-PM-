## What was captured

Dylan edited an AI draft of a short internal Teams message (notify a colleague that tickets were raised). The deltas between the draft and his posted version reveal voice preferences for this message type.

### Verbatim — Dylan's posted version
> Cadel, I raised two tickets to track off this thread:

> * AP-2572 — the Glenholme/Dalmore duplication bug, just for tracking. Reckon you've already fixed it, can you confirm and close if so? - https://agriprove.atlassian.net/browse/AP-2572

### Voice deltas (draft → Dylan's edit)
- **Opener:** dropped "Hey" — opened with the first name + comma only ("Cadel,"). For a quick ticket-notify message he doesn't always use the "Hey [name]" greeting from the close-team profile.
- **Lead line:** "I raised two tickets to track off this thread:" — first person, plain, states the action and purpose ("to track") up front.
- **Link placement:** each ticket's URL is appended **inline at the end of its own bullet** with " - <url>", not collected on a separate line or as a "·"-separated trailer.
- **Kept verbatim from the draft:** the casual content held up — "Reckon you've already fixed it, can you confirm and close if so?" and the one-line ticket summaries. So content/tone matched; the edits were structural (opener + link placement).

## How to apply
When drafting Dylan's short internal Teams notifications (especially "I raised / I did X, here are the links"):
- Open with the first name + comma (no "Hey") for quick notify-style posts.
- Lead with the action and its purpose in one line.
- Put each link inline at the end of its bullet (`— summary ... - <url>`), one item per bullet.
- Keep summaries to one casual line; "Reckon", "just for tracking", direct asks are on-voice.
