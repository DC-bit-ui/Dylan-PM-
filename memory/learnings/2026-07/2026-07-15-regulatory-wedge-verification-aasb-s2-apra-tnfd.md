# Regulatory wedge verification — AASB S2 / APRA / TNFD (for the bank pitch)

**Date:** 2026-07-15 · **Method:** independent web research (primary + advisory sources) · **Confidence:** [high] on the disclosure obligation; [moderate] on APRA trajectory (live consultation).

## Verdict in one line
The disclosure wedge is **real and live** — but the honest framing is "the law requires you to quantify physical-risk-vulnerable assets; credible, audit-grade compliance for an ag book needs asset-level data that doesn't exist at scale — we are that," NOT "you are legally required to buy this." APRA capital relief is **not live** (don't claim it); it's a well-signposted 2026 direction where the *data gap is the blocker* — which is a first-mover story, not a current benefit.

## AASB S2 — the disclosure obligation [high — lean on it]
- Mandatory. **Group 1** entities report for annual periods beginning on/after **1 Jan 2025** (Group 2: 1 Jul 2026; Group 3: 1 Jul 2027). Major banks incl. large agri-lenders are Group 1.
- **Para 29(c):** entities must disclose the **amount and % of assets/business activities vulnerable to climate-related physical risks** → requires **location/asset-level** physical-risk assessment across the portfolio (postcode/country proxies are called out as weak / not audit-grade). This is the crux that supports the pitch.
- **Relief valve:** must use "all **reasonable and supportable** information available **without undue cost or effort**" — a proportionality scaling for data/resource limits. So the law does NOT compel buying paddock-level telemetry; it creates *demand* and an *audit-credibility gap*. Frame the pitch as closing that gap, not as legal compulsion.
- AASB S2 **scenario analysis** requires ≥2 scenarios incl. a **1.5°C** pathway (goes beyond IFRS S2) → supports the resilience/counterfactual/projection framing.
- Financed-**emissions** got extra transition time (Dec 2025 amendments) — that's the emissions side (Ruminati's turf). **Physical risk is the un-serviced gap** — the distinct opening for AgriProve.

## APRA capital — soft/forward-looking [moderate — do NOT overclaim]
- **CPG 229** is *guidance*, "imposes no new regulatory requirements or obligations." Institutions *may* consider climate in their **ICAAP**.
- APRA (2024–25 corporate plan) said it **will begin incorporating climate risk into the capital framework**; in **H1 2026** APRA is consulting on capital/liquidity enhancements (starting with standardised credit-risk risk weights). So momentum is real.
- BUT: no mandatory explicit climate capital adjustment exists yet, and industry notes the blocker is **"absence of robust and standardised data."** → Position: "this positions you ahead of where APRA is heading, and the standardised-data gap is exactly what we close" — first-mover, not "capital relief today."
- APRA CVA (2022) was exploratory; new insurance-affordability CVA results due ~mid-2026.

## TNFD — the next wave [high on facts; not yet mandatory in AU]
- **733 adopters**, >US$22T AUM, **179 financial institutions** by late 2025 (deck's "733+" checks out).
- **ISSB** is taking over nature standard-setting (Exposure Draft targeted by CBD COP17, Oct 2026). Australia is *considering* nature mandates — **not yet mandatory**. So frame TNFD as "voluntary but accelerating, ISSB formalising — TCFD circa 2017," not "mandatory."

## How to say each claim (pitch guardrails)
- ✅ "You are already legally required (AASB S2, since Jan 2025) to disclose the share of your book vulnerable to physical climate risk — at asset level to be audit-credible."
- ✅ "The measurement infrastructure to do that at paddock scale doesn't exist. That's the gap we fill."
- ⚠️ "APRA is moving climate into the capital framework (2026 consultation); the blocker is standardised data. This positions you first." — NOT "delivers capital relief."
- ⚠️ TNFD "next wave, accelerating, ISSB formalising" — NOT "mandatory now."
