## The two motions (read this first — most consequential pattern)

Hobbs handles phone and on-farm as **fundamentally different motions**, not as the same motion at different surfaces. This is the single most important pattern for any rep adopting his approach:

| | Phone | On-farm |
|---|---|---|
| Rapport / conversion split | **100% rapport, 0% conversion** | 70% rapport, 30% conversion |
| AgriProve mentioned? | **Almost never** — referred to as "my company," "carbon satellite model" | Named, positioned ("verification machine," "accountants for soil carbon") |
| Value props deployed? | None | Full — ratchet, 25/75, carbon-tax-vs-payment, case-study cheques |
| Live math on hectares? | Never | Every visit |
| Objection plays? | None — call is too short and rapport too thin | Full — all 22 framings deployed as needed |
| Goal of the interaction | **Earn the right to the visit.** Convert a cold or warm contact into a calendar slot OR a low-commitment artifact (HORIZON Snapshot) | Convert. The full sales motion runs here. |
| Persona leaned on | "I'm a farmer from America on a fellowship" — heavy persona use | "I'm just the Grazier observing from the outside" — de-positioning |

**Implication for other reps:** if Ben has been treating phone calls as compressed versions of farm visits (pushing value props, doing math, addressing objections in 5 minutes), that approach is backwards from Hobbs's pattern. **Hobbs's phone discipline is to hold all sales content back until he's earned the visit.** The phone is purely rapport infrastructure.

## The on-farm headline pattern

Hobbs almost never opens with carbon. He runs a **30-60 minute reconnaissance phase** asking about the operation, soils, genetics, rotation, family history. Once the operator has fully described their world, he transitions with a question:

> *"What do you know about carbon?"* or *"Let me just talk first about what could possibly make you guys money."*

The carbon pitch then lands on already-warm ground because the operator has just spent 30+ minutes explaining everything good they're doing. The whole motion is built around making AgriProve feel like an acknowledgment of work the operator's already doing, not a sales pitch for a new thing.

**Why this works:** by the time Hobbs starts talking carbon, the operator has been validated as a capable, thoughtful land manager. The reframe lands as *"by the way, you should be getting paid for what you already do"*, not *"here's a new project to take on."*

## Sales motion sequence

```
1. RECON (30-60 min)              Listen. Ask operator questions (rotation logic,
                                  weaning timing, EBV vs phenotype). Establish
                                  peer-operator credibility. No mention of
                                  AgriProve except in passing.

2. TRANSITION QUESTION             "What do you know about carbon?" /
                                  "Let me talk first about what could make you
                                  guys money." — surfaces the opportunity.

3. POSITION AGRIPROVE              "AgriProve is basically the verification machine.
                                  They're like the accountants for soil carbon."
                                  De-positions himself ("I'm just the Grazier
                                  who's come in and sort of observing from the
                                  outside").

4. MATH ON THEIR HECTARES (live)    Pulls out calculator. Uses their actual
                                  hectare count, current spot price, 3 ACCUs/ha,
                                  75% share. Always lands as a tangible number.

5. CASE STUDY CHEQUE                Names one of three memorised real cheques:
                                  - [Queensland NRM] project: $60k → $500k
                                  - 83-ha → $45k cheque
                                  - 1000-ha → ~$14k baseline cap
                                  Proof of return, not theory.

6. METHODOLOGY (only when asked)    Ratchet explanation. Satellite + monthly
                                  monitoring. ISO 9001 / most-audited.
                                  Sampled when the operator asks "how does it
                                  actually work."

7. OBJECTION PLAYS                  Whichever territory surfaces. He has stock
                                  framings for each (see Language Bank below).

8. SEQUENCED LOW-COMMITMENT CLOSE   Always names the next concrete action,
                                  never asks for commitment in the room:
                                  HORIZON Snapshot → 1hr land-management call
                                  with Ben → optional registration.
```

**Critical:** he never asks for commitment in the room. The close is a sequenced funnel of low-friction next steps. By the time the customer would have to commit, they've already invested enough touchpoints that the commitment feels small.

## 10 signature moves

### 1. De-positioning ("I'm just the Grazier")
Used in **4 of 6 visits**. Strips the salesperson dynamic. Makes AgriProve's back-office machine the protagonist, not him.

> *"Remember, I'm just the Grazier who's come in and sort of observing from the outside."*

### 2. Live math on hectares
Never lets the conversation stay abstract. Pulls out the calculator, uses their actual numbers. The output is always a tangible dollar figure on their actual property.

### 3. Three cheque-sized case studies memorised
- **$60k → $500k Queensland NRM project**
- **83-ha → $45k cheque** (early project)
- **1000-ha → ~$14k baseline cap** (cost-side anchor)

Uses them as proof of return, not theory. Operators remember dollar figures, not abstractions.

### 4. Reduces the 25-year contract to two clauses
Every time the long-commitment question comes up:

> *"Our contract basically boils down to: make a commitment to be better, make a commitment to keep your land in the agricultural system for 25 years."*

Removes the bureaucratic dread. Makes the 25-year sound like good practice + don't subdivide.

### 5. Refuses to play agronomist
When asked *"what should we do to increase carbon?"* he stays in lane:

> *"We let you guys farm the way you think you should farm. We're not an agronomy service."*

Reinforces the agnostic positioning. Protects the customer relationship from feeling like the sale comes with prescriptions.

### 6. Names what AgriProve absorbs (risk-buffer line)
The audit-machine line + the risk-buffer line do parallel work. Together they explain why the 25% success fee is fair and why the operator's downside is bounded.

> *"AgriProve also functions as kind of a risk buffer. The only thing the farmer pays is the upfront sampling cost."*

### 7. Validates the cynic
When operators push back hard (*"why pay for what we'd do anyway?"*, *"doesn't seem to add up"*), he praises the question:

> *"You're poking the argument at its weakest point — that's a good idea."*

Disarms without conceding. Doesn't always convert (visit 5 stayed neutral) but never escalates.

### 8. Big macro framing for sophisticated operators
Scope 3 supply chain, beef-as-wine provenance, digital twins for bankability, soil-carbon-as-land-acquisition-derivative. Used selectively with operators who have a strategic mindset (visits 1, 3, 4).

### 9. Vulnerability as trust device
> *"I'm an American just trying to figure out what's going on here."*
> *"I'm just learning under the hood."*

Counter-intuitively builds credibility because he's not pretending to be the regulator. Disarms the "salesperson" framing.

### 10. Sequenced low-commitment close
Always closes by naming the next concrete low-commitment action. Never asks for commitment in the room:
- **HORIZON Snapshot first** (free, low-effort)
- **Then 1hr land-management call with Ben** (medium-effort)
- **Then optional registration** (commitment moment)

## Verbatim language bank (22 framings, ranked by impact)

These are the verbatim phrases that did the heaviest lifting. **Transferable to other reps. Use the exact words — paraphrasing weakens them.**

### Tier 1 — Single-sentence kills

1. **Revenue split objection:** *"You're taking 25% out of what you weren't going to get anything from before."* — re-frames the split as a success fee.

2. **Methodology / ACCU mechanics:** *"It works on a ratchet. Year one you baseline. Year two it goes up, boom, you get credited. Year three you lose something, no penalty. You only get credited when you beat the previous high."* — most-repeated framing across visits, landed every time.

3. **"Carbon = lock up the land" misconception:** *"We don't do trees. It's nothing to do with trees. It's to do with your soil."* — kills the conflation in one sentence.

4. **25-year commitment fear:** *"Our contract basically boils down to: make a commitment to be better, make a commitment to keep your land in the agricultural system for 25 years."*

5. **Sale-of-property objection:** *"It only becomes a problem if you've been making money doing it. If you've been making credits, why would they throw away a revenue stream?"* — inverts the frame.

### Tier 2 — Positioning + macro hooks

6. *"AgriProve is basically the verification machine. They're like the accountants kind of for soil carbon."* — clean single-sentence positioning.

7. *"If we don't find a system that works like this, it's just going to become a carbon tax. So it's much better for the farmer to get paid for the good work, instead of taxing everybody, which includes the farmer."* — flips political skepticism.

8. *"AgriProve also functions as kind of a risk buffer. The only thing the farmer pays is the upfront sampling cost."*

9. *"The beautiful thing about AgriProve is that we let you guys farm the way you think you should farm. We're not an agronomy service."*

10. *"If our management and our production and our profitability are all growing, then by definition our carbon should be growing as well. You can't decrease soil health for 30 years and expect everything to go up."*

### Tier 3 — Future-orientation hooks (for sophisticated operators)

11. *"The future of beef is going to look like the past 50 years of wine. Instead of just red or white, you'll want from this vineyard, this year."* — provenance hook.

12. *"With these kind of numbers for 30 years, haven't been getting paid for that. And you should have."* — the moment when a good operator realises they've been leaving money on the table.

13. *"The people who figure out how to use the land the best will be the people who end up with all the land."* — long-arc legacy hook.

14. *"It becomes interestingly enough like a land acquisition tool. A financial derivative of productivity."* — reframes carbon for buyers/aggregators.

15. *"Down the line when Woolworths and Coles have to account for their entire supply chain down to the farm — if you're sitting there holding those ACCUs, they'll have to buy them at some point."* — Scope-3 inevitability.

### Tier 4 — Methodology + customer-aligned moves

16. *"Now we can run the satellite over every month. The second we see carbon levels are up, that triggers sampling, so you never leave any money on the table."* — explains methodology upgrade.

17. *"Your flat country can go up and your hill country goes backwards. If they're in the one project, you went up by three, down by three, you get zero. So we register them as two projects. We pick up the cost."* — methodology + customer-aligned incentive.

18. *"Then we kick off the excruciating process that you don't have to handle at all."* — sequencing while reassuring on regulator burden.

19. *"We're now the most audited company for soil carbon in Australia. ISO 9001 certification."* — trust/track-record hook.

### Tier 5 — Tactical responses

20. **DIY objection:** *"I would not recommend it. It's like, if you want to do that, you might as well do your own taxes."*

21. **De-positioning:** *"Remember, I'm just the Grazier who's come in and sort of observing from the outside."*

22. **ACCU framing for financially-literate operators:** *"It's the only thing that goes straight to your bottom line, not a cost item on your balance sheet."*

- **Cross-cultural connections** — Hobbs is American (Texas/ranching background); he references his "we were in California when our first kid was born" and "Margaret/French name pronunciation" stories.
- **Names Australian ag figures** when the operator mentions them: Mark Lucas, Will Donovan, Wilmot/Alistair McLeod, Gabe Brown, Elaine Ingham. Signals he's in the conversation, not adjacent to it.

## Phone style (distinct from on-farm)

**Reminder of the principle:** phone is 100% rapport, 0% conversion. The goal is earning the visit, not pitching the product. Six observed Aircall transcripts, all outbound, zero hard rejections — even ideologically-suspicious prospects left the call wanting to stay in contact.

### Cold-open framings (ranked by effectiveness)

1. **"[First name], it's Hobbs over at Aubrey."** — geography substitutes for company name. Works only with warm contacts. The single most efficient open in the set.

2. **"My name's Hobbs. I'm a farmer from America. Have I caught you at a good time?"** — three-beat permission-asking open. "Farmer from America" does enormous work: memorable, low-threat (not a corporate caller), implies he's not selling anything.

3. **"Hi. I'm looking for [station name]."** — station-first, name-second. Confirms he has the right place before claiming attention. Positions him as someone with a list, not a random caller.

4. **"Did I catch you at a bad time?"** — negative-frame permission ask. Easier to say "no this is fine" than to answer "is this a good time?" which feels like a pre-commitment.

5. **"This is Hobbs, the American."** — when contact doesn't immediately place him. Persona as calling card; company never named.

6. **"[First name], it's Hobbs, your favorite American."** — branding existing relationship in the opening line. Asserts warmth as both greeting and rapport-claim.

**Hobbs never opens with "I'm Hobbs from AgriProve" on the phone.** AgriProve is functionally invisible in cold-open phone work.

### First-90-seconds pattern (consistent across all 6 calls)

In order:
1. **Self-identification as "the American" / "a farmer from America"** — identity before purpose
2. **Permission/timing check** — "have I caught you at a good time?" / "did I catch you at a bad time?"
3. **Frame-setter that is NOT about AgriProve** — "fellowship," "writing my second book," "looking at farms that are doing cool stuff," "gathering information on the industry"
4. **The ask compressed into one sentence** — typically "would you be open to me visiting for a couple of hours"

He never reaches value prop, methodology, or revenue split in the cold call. The whole call is about earning the visit.

### Phone-specific signature moves (not in farm-visit data)

#### The "fellowship" device
The dominant phone frame. Academic, time-bound ("another month or so"), no commercial intent. Appears in 4 of 6 calls. Gives him a reason to be calling that isn't sales.

#### The "second book" hook
Used on the Alex Sloane re-engagement call: *"I've started writing my second book."* Non-AgriProve reason to be talking. Pure phone-specific de-positioning.

#### "Doing cool stuff"
Implicit compliment in 3 cold opens: by being on his calling list, the prospect is "doing cool stuff." Disarms "why are you calling me specifically?" by reframing it as "you've been selected."

#### Self-deprecating cold-list transparency
Stormvic13 call: *"the nerds on the tech team scraped anybody with a property bigger than 500 hectares."* Owns the awkwardness rather than dodging it. Trades polish for honesty.

#### Ideological mirroring (high-risk, doesn't transfer)
James Tehan call: prospect went deep into government-surveillance / Catherine Austin Fitts territory. Hobbs did NOT counter-frame, defend the industry, or change subjects. He mirrored. Said he was "a big fan" of the same commentator. The call ended warm rather than as a rejection. **Wouldn't transfer to a less ideologically flexible rep.**

#### The Kanye line
Stormvic13 call, when the prospect apologised for being short: *"In the words of Kanye West, I'm not mean, I'm just focused."* Recognisable pop figure disarming the moment. Hobbs-specific reflex — risky for someone without his timing.

### What each phone call sets up

He's **never** trying to close on the phone. He's angling for either:
- **An in-person visit booking** (4 of 6 calls produced one — same-day or within a week)
- **An artifact handoff** (HORIZON Snapshot / "carbon satellite model" sent by email — keeps the relationship live between calls)

### Transferability — what Ben can adopt vs what's Hobbs-specific

| Move | Transferable to Ben? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Farmer from America" identity | No | Persona-bound. Identity-specific. |
| "Fellowship" / "writing a book" frame | Partial | Structurally transferable — Ben could use a different non-commercial reason ("I'm checking in with operators in the region for our quarterly report") but the move IS transferable |
| Permission-ask in negative frame ("bad time?") | **Yes — pure technique** | Has nothing to do with persona. Should be standard. |
| Station-first opener | **Yes** | Confirms the right party before claiming attention. Generic technique. |
| Self-deprecating cold-list transparency | **Yes** | "Honestly, you popped up on a list of farms over a certain size that are doing interesting things" — works without Hobbs's persona |
| "Doing cool stuff" implicit compliment | **Yes** | Structurally transferable |
| Hold all value props back | **Yes — most important transferable lesson** | This is the discipline. Phone is rapport infrastructure, not pitch surface. |
| Ideological mirroring | **No** | Too risky for someone without Hobbs's specific cultural flexibility |
| Kanye-style pop-cultural disarming | No | Timing-specific, persona-bound |

The headline transferable insight: **Ben should stop trying to convert on the phone. The phone's job is the visit booking. Hold all value props for in-person.**

## Known gaps (drive the interviews)

1. **Reading the customer** — how does he tell interest vs politeness? Body language? What signals tell him to push vs back off? Not in transcripts.

2. **Close detection** — what's the moment he knows they're going to convert? What does he say or do then? Not surfaced clearly.

3. **The Additionality weakness** — 2 of 3 visits ambiguous. Why is this one harder to handle?

4. **What he's stopped doing** — what did he do early that he's stopped? What didn't work? Not in transcripts.

5. **What others get wrong** — when he watches Ben or other reps, what do they miss that he'd do differently? Only he can tell us.

6. **Property-specific adjustments** — does he adjust based on operator type (seedstock vs commercial, dryland vs irrigated, family vs corporate)? Partially visible in transcripts, worth confirming.

7. **Family/decision dynamics** — handling the partner question, multi-generational signoff, accountant involvement. Some visible (visit 2, 6) but not systematically.

8. **The HORIZON Snapshot moment** — when and how does he bring it up? What does it do for the conversation? Worth understanding the trigger more precisely.

9. **Failed phone opens** — all 6 sampled phone calls landed warm. No data on what doesn't work on the phone. Worth asking: when has a cold open bombed? What did the prospect say or do?

10. **The phone-to-visit transition decision** — what makes him decide on a call "this is a visit-worthy prospect" vs "this is HORIZON Snapshot only"? The implicit qualification logic is invisible in transcripts.

These gaps are what the interviews need to capture. See `briefings/hobbs-interview-questions.md`.
