# Hobbs Interview Question Set — 4 × 30-min Sessions

**Purpose:** Capture the tacit knowledge of Hobbs's sales motion that doesn't show up in farm-visit transcripts. The 47 distillates already pulled give us *what he says*. These interviews need to capture *what he doesn't articulate in front of customers* — his reading of the room, his sequence decisions, what he's stopped doing, what others miss.

**Time-bound:** Hobbs leaves ~2026-06-02. Four sessions of ~30 min each = 2 hours of his time, ideally one per week.

**Recommended approach:** weekly 1:1, 30 min, Granola-recorded. Don't hand him the questions in advance — read the room and pull on threads as they come. If a 1:1 doesn't land, ask him to record himself talking through the questions solo (he might prefer that anyway).

**Use of output:** every interview gets transcribed (Granola), distilled via Pass 0, the findings update `coaching/hobbs-profile.md` directly, closing the gaps named at the end of that doc.

---

## Session 1 — Opening + sequence (30 min)

The bulk distillation shows he runs a 30-60 minute reconnaissance before any carbon talk. Need to surface the deliberate choices he's making in those minutes.

### Questions

1. **The walk-in.** Walk me through your first 5 minutes on a farm. Before you've even sat down. What are you doing, what are you looking at, what are you saying?

2. **The recon questions.** You typically ask 30-60 minutes of operator questions before bringing up carbon. What's your default question set? In what order? What changes the order?

3. **The transition.** How do you know when the recon phase is done and it's time to talk carbon? What's the cue you're waiting for? Sometimes you ask *"what do you know about carbon?"*, sometimes *"let me just talk first about what could make you guys money."* When do you use which?

4. **Sequence through topics.** Once you're into the carbon pitch, do you have a default path? You typically: position AgriProve → math on hectares → case study cheque → methodology (when asked) → objection plays → sequenced close. Is that universal or does it vary?

5. **The math moment.** You always do live math on their hectares. Why? Have you ever tried *not* doing that? What changes if you do it later in the conversation vs early?

6. **Visit length.** What's your ideal visit duration? When have you cut a visit short? When have you stayed longer than planned?

## Session 2 — Reading the customer (30 min)

This is the biggest gap. Transcripts don't capture body language, attention shifts, micro-disengagement. Need to get inside what he's reading.

### Questions

1. **Interest vs politeness.** How do you tell whether someone's genuinely interested vs being polite? What's the signal?

2. **Body language during the math.** When you pull out the calculator and start doing dollars-on-their-hectares, what are you watching for? Have you ever stopped mid-calculation because of what you saw?

3. **The "lean-in" moment.** Most visits have a moment where the operator visibly shifts from neutral to engaged. What does that look like? What's just happened?

4. **The "lean-out" moment.** What does it look like when you've lost them? What do you do when you see it?

5. **The cynic.** Visit 5 (Monaro southwest) had a sharp-cynic operator who pressed *"why pay for what we'd do anyway?"* and broader disinformation. You held ground on policy logic but didn't convert in the room. Looking back — what did you read in him? Was he ever going to convert? What would you do differently next time?

6. **Family dynamics in real time.** When the partner is in the room (or not), how does that change what you do? Multi-generational operators — how do you handle the dynamic between the older and younger person?

7. **The close detection.** What's the moment you know they're going to convert? What do you see or hear? What do you say or do at that moment?

## Session 3 — Objection plays + tactical edges (30 min)

Distillates surfaced 22 framings, but: the Additionality territory came up as a weakness (2 of 3 visits ambiguous). Several other plays are observed but not fully systematic.

### Questions

1. **The Additionality weakness.** When the Additionality question comes up — *"prove that what I'm doing now is different from what I would've done anyway"* — your framings have landed less consistently than other territories. Why is this one harder? What's the conversation actually about underneath?

2. **The "we'd do it anyway" objection.** This is the harder version of Additionality. When an operator says *"this is good practice, we'd do it regardless,"* what's the right response? Is it conceding the point? Reframing? Walking away?

3. **The "doesn't seem to add up" objection.** Sometimes operators feel something's off with the model but can't name what. How do you handle that vague distrust? Is it different from a specific objection?

4. **Choosing case studies.** You have three memorised cheques — Queensland $60k → $500k, 83-ha → $45k, 1000-ha → $14k baseline. When do you use which? Are there situations where naming the wrong one backfires?

5. **The HORIZON Snapshot moment.** When do you bring it up? Is it always the close-step? Or do you sometimes mention it earlier as a hook? What does it actually do for the conversation?

6. **The de-positioning move.** *"I'm just the Grazier observing from the outside"* — when did you start using this? Was it deliberate or did it emerge? Have you ever NOT used it?

7. **The macro framing.** You only use Scope 3 / wine-provenance / digital twins / land-acquisition-derivative with sophisticated operators. How do you decide an operator is in that bucket?

## Session 4 — What you've stopped doing + what others miss (30 min)

The hardest stuff to extract because it's negative space. What he doesn't do, why, and what he sees in others.

### Questions

1. **Things you've stopped doing.** What did you do early in your time at AgriProve that you've stopped? Why?

2. **Failed moves.** Has there been a framing or approach you tried that bombed? Walk me through one.

3. **Things you don't say.** Are there topics you deliberately avoid bringing up unless asked? Why?

4. **What Ben gets wrong.** When you watch Ben on a call or hear about his outreach, what does he miss that you'd do differently? What does he do well that you'd want to learn from?

5. **What Claudia gets wrong (and right).** Same question for Claudia and her research/calling approach.

6. **What we get wrong about you.** When the team describes your approach, what do they get wrong? What do they oversimplify?

7. **The next Hobbs.** If you were training the next person to do this job after you, what's the one thing you'd start with? What's the one thing nobody can be taught?

8. **The handover, real talk.** What's the biggest risk when you leave? What's the thing that worries you most about AgriProve not having you in the room?

## How we use the outputs

After each session:

1. **Transcribe via Granola** (or otter.ai if Granola isn't recording)
2. **Distill via Pass 0** — same schema as farm visits, but explicitly Hobbs-as-subject (no PII generalisation needed since Hobbs is the speaker, not a customer)
3. **Update `coaching/hobbs-profile.md`** — fill the gap sections (currently 8 named gaps), add new framings to the language bank, refine the sequence model
4. **Note new question threads** — anything Hobbs surfaces that opens a new question becomes input to the next session

By session 4, the profile should be close to ready for Hobbs himself to validate. Final step (week 3): walk him through the profile and ask *"is this you?"* — the gaps he names there are the highest-priority captures.

---

## What if Hobbs would rather record himself?

Some people open up more when there's no interlocutor. If Hobbs prefers, send him this question set + a Granola/voice-memo brief and let him talk through it solo. The output is potentially richer because he can pause, think, restart.

Trade-off: no follow-up probing in the moment. Could mix both — solo recording for some sessions, live 1:1 for others.

---

## Ground rules for the interviews

1. **No agenda visible to Hobbs** — present this as "we want to make sure your approach doesn't walk out the door when you leave." Not as a performance review or scrutiny.
2. **No interruptions when he's mid-thought** — even if he wanders. The wandering is often where the gold is.
3. **Push back gently when you suspect performance answers** — *"that's what you'd say in a presentation; what actually happens when..."*
4. **Capture verbatim language** — even more important than the content. His phrasings ARE the IP.
5. **Plan one session to be on-farm together** if logistically possible — observing him in action surfaces things he can't articulate in an interview room.
